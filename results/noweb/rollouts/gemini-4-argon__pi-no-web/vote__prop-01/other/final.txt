Choosing whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 1 depends on your personal priorities regarding housing programs and state spending. Because voters have different perspectives on these issues, it is not possible to provide a simple "yes" or "no" recommendation. 

According to the provided ballot summary, Proposition 1 is a legislative statute that asks voters to decide whether to authorize $11.25 billion in state general obligation bonds for housing affordability programs. 

**Reasons to consider voting "Yes":**
According to the ballot text, a "yes" vote supports authorizing $11.25 billion in bonds to fund several specific housing initiatives, including:
*   Multifamily rental housing and supportive housing
*   Mortgages for veterans
*   Preservation of existing affordable housing
*   Downpayment assistance programs

The ballot summary notes that this measure is backed by supporters such as US Vets, the CA Federation of Teachers, Self-Help for the Elderly, Habitat for Humanity CA, and the CA Alliance of Child & Family Services.

**Reasons to consider voting "No":**
While the ballot summary states that no formal opponents were submitted, voters who choose to vote "no" often weigh the financial obligation the state would incur. According to the measure's fiscal impact statement, authorizing these bonds would result in an increased cost to the state of $500 million to $600 million per year for approximately 25 years in order to repay the debt. A "no" vote would prevent the state from taking on this additional bond debt. 

Ultimately, your vote depends on whether you believe the benefits of funding these specific housing affordability programs outweigh the long-term annual costs to the state budget.
