Deciding how to vote on Proposition 40 depends on your priorities regarding taxation, health care funding, and state budget rules. Rather than a simple "yes" or "no," here is a breakdown of what a vote either way means based on the ballot summary you provided:

**A "Yes" vote** supports enacting a one-time 5% tax on certain taxpayers who hold assets over $1 billion, with the resulting revenue dedicated primarily to health care. 
* **Supporters:** According to the ballot text, this side is backed by U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders and SEIU United Healthcare Workers West. 
* **Fiscal Considerations:** According to the fiscal impact statement, a "yes" vote would generate a temporary revenue increase of tens of billions of dollars spread over several years to fund health care, though these funds would be exempt from state spending limits and constitutional requirements that normally direct a portion of state revenues to public schools.

**A "No" vote** opposes this one-time tax on billionaires and maintains current tax laws and constitutional budget requirements.
* **Opponents:** According to the ballot text, this side is backed by the California Primary Care Association, the California School Boards Association, and the California Taxpayers Association. 
* **Fiscal Considerations:** According to the fiscal impact statement, enacting the tax could lead to a possible ongoing decrease of less than $1 billion per year in regular income tax revenue from billionaires. Opponents may also take issue with the measure's exemption from constitutional school funding guarantees, as reflected by the opposition of the California School Boards Association. 

Ultimately, your vote depends on whether you favor raising temporary funds for health care through a one-time wealth tax, or if you share the concerns of opponents regarding the potential loss of ongoing income tax revenue and the bypassing of traditional school funding rules.
