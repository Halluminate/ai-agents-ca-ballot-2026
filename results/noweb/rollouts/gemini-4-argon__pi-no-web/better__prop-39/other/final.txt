Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 39 depends on how you weigh changes to voting requirements against their potential financial and accessibility impacts. According to the provided ballot summary, this initiative constitutional amendment would change state voting rules regarding voter identification.

**A "Yes" Vote**
According to the ballot summary, voting "yes" supports requiring citizens to present government-issued identification when voting in person, as well as invalidating mail ballots that do not include the last four digits of a designated government-issued identification number on the envelope. 
* **Supporters:** As listed in the ballot summary, organizations supporting the measure include The Transparency Foundation, the Latino American Political Association, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, and the CA Women’s Leadership Association. Proponents of voter ID initiatives generally argue that such requirements enhance election integrity and boost public confidence in voting outcomes.

**A "No" Vote**
According to the ballot summary, voting "no" opposes adding these new government-issued identification requirements for in-person and mail-in voting, keeping current verification rules in place.
* **Opponents:** As listed in the ballot summary, organizations opposing the measure include ACLU California Action, Common Cause, and the League of Women Voters of California. Opponents of strict voter ID mandates generally argue that these measures create unnecessary barriers for eligible voters and lead to valid ballots being rejected over technicalities.

**Fiscal Impact**
The ballot summary's fiscal impact statement notes that implementing these new voting requirements would result in state and local costs ranging between the tens of millions of dollars and the low hundreds of millions of dollars each year.
