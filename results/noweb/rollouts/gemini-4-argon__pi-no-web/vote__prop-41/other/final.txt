Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 41 is a personal decision that depends on your views regarding state tax policy, government spending limits, and financial oversight. Rather than a simple yes or no, here is an overview of what the measure entails and the groups on either side, based on the ballot information provided:

**Overview of the Measure**
According to the ballot summary, Proposition 41 is a constitutional amendment that addresses how new state taxes are managed. Specifically, it would:
* Nullify any state taxes enacted after January 1, 2026, that exempt their revenues from the existing voter-approved state spending limit.
* Require both pre-election and recurring audits for programs funded by new state special taxes. 

According to the fiscal impact statement, the net financial effect of the proposition is unknown because it depends on future actions and decisions by voters, the Legislature, and other policymakers.

**Voting "Yes"**
A "Yes" vote is in favor of prohibiting new state taxes from bypassing the state spending limit and establishing new mandatory audit requirements for special taxes. 
* **Supporters:** According to the ballot text, this measure is supported by the California Society of Certified Public Accountants (CalCPAs), the CalAsian Chamber of Commerce, and the California Taxpayers Association (CalTax).

**Voting "No"**
A "No" vote is against adding these new restrictions and audit requirements to the state constitution, thereby maintaining the current rules regarding the state spending limit and how new special taxes are overseen.
* **Opponents:** According to the ballot text, this measure is opposed by U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders and SEIU United Healthcare Workers West.
