Choosing how to vote on Proposition 4 depends on your priorities regarding campaign finance and the use of taxpayer funds, so it is not possible to provide a simple "yes" or "no" recommendation. 

Based on the ballot summary provided, here is a breakdown of what a vote for each side means and the organizations backing each position:

**A "Yes" Vote**
According to the ballot measure summary, a "yes" vote supports repealing the existing prohibition against using public money for political campaigns. This would allow state and local governments the option to create public funding programs for candidate elections, while explicitly prohibiting them from using money earmarked for education, transportation, or public safety for these programs. 
* **Supporters:** This position is backed by organizations listed in the ballot summary, including the League of Women Voters of CA, the California Nurses Association, Mental Health Advocacy, Social Security Works, and Consumer Watchdog.

**A "No" Vote**
According to the ballot measure summary, a "no" vote keeps the current prohibition in place, barring state and local governments from offering public funds for candidate election campaigns. A "no" vote would also avoid the measure's estimated fiscal impact, which projects ongoing state costs of a few hundred thousand dollars each year for the Fair Political Practices Commission to field questions about public campaign finance programs.
* **Opponents:** This position is backed by organizations listed in the ballot summary, including the California Taxpayers Association, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, the Family Business Association of California, and United Latinos Action.
