Deciding whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 1 depends on your individual priorities regarding housing initiatives and state spending. 

According to the ballot information provided, a **"yes" vote** supports authorizing $11.25 billion in state general obligation bonds to fund various housing affordability programs. Supporters—which include US Vets, the California Federation of Teachers, Self-Help for the Elderly, Habitat for Humanity CA, and the CA Alliance of Child & Family Services—favor the measure as a way to fund multifamily rental housing, mortgages for veterans, supportive housing, the preservation of existing affordable housing, and downpayment assistance. 

A **"no" vote** opposes authorizing these bonds. While the ballot summary notes that no formal arguments in opposition were submitted, voters who lean toward voting "no" may be concerned with the measure's financial obligations. According to the provided fiscal impact statement, passing the proposition would result in increased state costs of $500 million to $600 million annually for roughly 25 years to repay the bonds. A "no" vote would prevent the state from taking on this additional debt.
