Choosing whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 39 depends on your perspective on voting requirements and state spending. According to the provided ballot summary, the initiative is a constitutional amendment that would establish new identification rules for both in-person and mail-in voting, carrying an estimated fiscal impact of tens of millions to low hundreds of millions of dollars each year to implement.

Here is a breakdown of the two sides based on the ballot information provided:

*   **A "Yes" Vote:** Voting yes means you support requiring citizens to present government-issued identification when voting in person, as well as invalidating mail ballots that do not include the last four digits of a designated government-issued ID number on the envelope. According to the ballot summary, this measure is supported by organizations such as The Transparency Foundation, the Latino American Political Association, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, and the CA Women’s Leadership Association.
*   **A "No" Vote:** Voting no means you oppose implementing these new identification mandates for in-person and mail-in voting, keeping current voting rules in place and avoiding the associated implementation costs. According to the ballot summary, the measure is opposed by groups including ACLU California Action, Common Cause, and the League of Women Voters of California.
