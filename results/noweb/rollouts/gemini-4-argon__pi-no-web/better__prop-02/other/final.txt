Deciding whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 2 depends on your perspective regarding how California should manage its state budget and prepare for future economic downturns. 

According to the ballot information provided, **voting "yes"** is supported by groups and officials such as the California Professional Firefighters, the Los Angeles Area Chamber of Commerce, Gavin Newsom, Xavier Becerra, and Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel. Proponents of this measure favor increasing the state's Rainy Day Fund—originally established by voters in 2014—to ensure that state budget reserves are higher. The stated goal of supporters is to secure stable funding for essential services, including education, health care, and public safety, when the state faces economic downturns.

While the provided ballot summary notes that no formal opposition was submitted for this measure, **voting "no"** on measures that increase budget reserve caps generally aligns with the view that state funds could be better utilized elsewhere. In broader fiscal debates, critics of expanding rainy day funds often argue that placing more money into restricted reserves limits the legislature's flexibility to address immediate policy needs, invest in current programs and infrastructure, or provide direct tax relief to residents.

Ultimately, your vote depends on whether you prioritize building larger state reserves to cushion against future budget deficits or prefer keeping those funds available for immediate state spending or tax reductions.
