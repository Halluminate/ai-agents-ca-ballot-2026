Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 37 depends on your priorities regarding housing policy and bond programs, so it is not possible to provide a simple "yes" or "no" answer. 

According to the ballot summary provided, Proposition 37 is an initiative statute that would authorize $25 billion in bonds to create a fixed-rate mortgage program for eligible middle-income buyers purchasing newly constructed homes priced below approximately $1.5 million. The summary notes that the bonds would be repaid through mortgage payments rather than by the state, resulting in no direct state or local costs.

**Voting "Yes"**
As noted in the provided text, the measure is supported by the United Nurses Associations of California, the California Conference of Carpenters, and California State Treasurer Fiona Ma. Voters who favor this measure may want to establish a self-funding loan program—covering up to 17% of the purchase price—to help qualifying California residents who can pay at least 3% down afford newly constructed homes without creating direct fiscal costs for state or local governments.

**Voting "No"**
According to the ballot information, no formal opposition was submitted against the measure. However, a "no" vote would mean keeping the current system in place and choosing not to authorize $25 billion in bonds for this specific loan program. Voters might consider a "no" vote if they disagree with the program's specific eligibility criteria, such as limiting the loans to newly constructed homes, or if they prefer different approaches to addressing housing affordability.
