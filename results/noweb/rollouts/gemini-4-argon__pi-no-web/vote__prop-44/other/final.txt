Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 44 depends on your personal priorities regarding healthcare regulation and how community clinics are managed. Rather than a simple "yes" or "no," here is a breakdown of the measure based on the ballot summary you provided to help you make an informed decision:

**Overview and Fiscal Impact**
According to the provided ballot summary, Proposition 44 is an initiative statute that applies to nonprofit Federally Qualified Health Centers—community clinics that deliver primary care to medically underserved areas and populations. The measure would impose penalties on these clinics if they spend less than 90% of their revenue on "program services" that advance their charitable purpose, which includes (but is not limited to) patient services. 

According to the fiscal impact analysis in the summary, implementing this measure would result in increased state costs in the low tens of millions of dollars annually, which would be funded through fees.

**A "Yes" Vote**
* **What it means:** Voting "yes" supports requiring nonprofit Federally Qualified Health Centers to spend at least 90% of their revenue on program services and imposing financial or regulatory penalties on clinics that do not meet that threshold.
* **Supporters:** According to the ballot summary provided, no official supporters were submitted for this measure.

**A "No" Vote**
* **What it means:** Voting "no" opposes imposing the 90% program spending requirement and its associated penalties, keeping current funding and operational rules for these community health clinics in place.
* **Opponents:** According to the ballot summary, the measure is opposed by several healthcare groups, including the American Academy of Pediatrics CA, the California School Nurses Organization, the California Academy of Family Physicians, and the California Medical Association.
