Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 45 depends on how you weigh the goal of speeding up the approval of certain infrastructure projects against the goal of maintaining current environmental review timelines and protections. 

Based on the ballot summary provided, here is an overview of the different perspectives and impacts to help inform your decision:

**A "Yes" Vote**
According to the ballot summary, voting "yes" supports amending the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) to set strict deadlines for completing environmental reviews and resolving lawsuits that challenge project approvals. This would expedite the review process for most housing, transportation, water, and health projects. 
* **Supporters:** The measure is backed by groups focused on infrastructure, healthcare, and housing development—specifically the California Children’s Hospital Association, the California Water Association, and the California Council for Affordable Housing. 

**A "No" Vote**
Voting "no" means keeping the current CEQA environmental review processes and legal timelines in place without the new deadlines for these specific projects. 
* **Opponents:** The measure is opposed by environmental, conservation, and health advocacy organizations—specifically the Coalition for Clean Air, CA Environmental Voters, Physicians for Social Responsibility (LA), and the National Wildlife Federation. 

**Fiscal Considerations**
According to the official fiscal impact statement in the ballot summary, passing the measure would result in initial costs for state and local governments, likely in the high tens of millions of dollars—and potentially exceeding $100 million—annually, though some of these costs would be covered by fees. The longer-term financial effects on state and local governments are uncertain and could be either positive or negative.
