Deciding whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 38 depends on how you weigh the potential benefits of state-funded medical research against the financial obligations of issuing new state bonds. 

According to the provided ballot summary, Proposition 38 would authorize $8.4 billion in general obligation bonds for immunology and immunotherapy research, with half of the funding allocated to a single University of California-affiliated nonprofit medical research institute and the other half distributed as research grants.

* **Reasons to consider voting "Yes":** A "yes" vote supports dedicating significant public funding toward advancing immunology and immunotherapy research. According to the ballot text, the measure is backed by medical and advocacy organizations including the Michael J. Fox Foundation, Alzheimer’s Los Angeles, the Kidney Cancer Association, and the American Nurses Association\CA. Voters in favor may also consider the fiscal summary's note that some or all of the state's repayment costs could potentially be offset if the funded research generates revenue.

* **Reasons to consider voting "No":** A "no" vote opposes taking on new state debt for this initiative. Based on the fiscal impact statement in the provided text, repaying the bond would result in increased state costs of $500 million to $600 million annually for approximately 20 years. The ballot summary lists Robert M. Kaplan, Ph.D., as an opponent of the measure. Voters against the proposition may be concerned about the guaranteed long-term financial cost to the state or the provision that directs half of the $8.4 billion to a single research institute. 

Ultimately, the choice depends on your individual priorities regarding state spending and public investment in medical research.
