I can't pick a side on this one for you—it's a genuine values question, not a factual one. But here's the core tradeoff:

**The case for yes:** It keeps an existing revenue stream ($5–15B/year) for schools and health care rather than creating a new tax, and it applies only to incomes over ~$371,000. If you prioritize stable education/health funding and believe high earners can bear the rate, yes makes sense.

**The case for no:** It converts a temporary tax into a permanent one. If you're concerned about California's high top tax rates driving residents or businesses out of state, or you prefer spending restraint and tax sunsets being honored, no makes sense.

Your answer likely hinges on two questions: Do you think the funded programs deliver enough value to justify the revenue, and do you think the tax meaningfully affects where wealthy people and businesses locate? Happy to dig into either if that helps you decide.
