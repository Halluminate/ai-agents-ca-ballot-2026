But I'm happy to help you think it through. Some key considerations on each side:

**Arguments for Yes:**
- Would raise tens of billions for health care from a very small number of ultra-wealthy taxpayers
- One-time tax, not ongoing
- Strong support from labor and health care worker advocates

**Arguments for No:**
- Amends the state constitution, which is hard to reverse
- Exempts the revenue from school funding requirements and spending limits—likely why the School Boards Association opposes it
- Wealth taxes can drive high-net-worth individuals to leave the state, potentially reducing ongoing income tax revenue (noted in the fiscal impact)
- Notably, the California Primary Care Association opposes it despite health care being the stated beneficiary
