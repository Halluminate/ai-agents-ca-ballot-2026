I can't tell you how to vote on this—it's a values-based decision, and reasonable people land on both sides. But here's a quick breakdown to help you decide:

**Arguments for Yes:**
- Immunology and immunotherapy research targets major diseases (cancer, Alzheimer's, autoimmune conditions)
- Backed by prominent patient advocacy groups and nurses' organizations
- Funded research could generate revenue that offsets some or all costs

**Arguments for No:**
- $8.4B in bonds means roughly $10–12B in total repayment—$500–600M/year from the general fund for ~20 years, competing with schools, infrastructure, and other priorities
- Half the funding is earmarked for a *single* institution rather than awarded competitively, which raises oversight and fairness concerns
- Bond debt commits future budgets regardless of the state's fiscal health

**Questions to ask yourself:**
1. Is medical research a good use of long-term state debt, or should it be funded through the regular budget?
2. Are you comfortable with half the money going to one pre-designated institute?
3. How do you weigh potential research breakthroughs against guaranteed decades of debt payments?

Happy to go deeper on any of these if it would help.
