What I can offer instead:

**What the measure does:** Amends the state constitution to block any new state tax on personal property (things other than real estate) and any tax applied retroactively. It would also void conflicting taxes enacted after January 1, 2026.

**Arguments supporters make:** It protects taxpayers from surprise taxes on things they already own, prevents retroactive taxation (which many consider fundamentally unfair since people can't plan around it), and locks in tax certainty.

**Arguments opponents make:** It permanently ties the state's hands on future revenue options — notably, it would block proposed wealth taxes on very high earners, which typically tax assets (personal property). It could constrain funding for schools, healthcare, and other services, and takes tools off the table before voters even know if they'd want them.

**Questions that might help you decide:**
- Do you think the state should preserve the option of wealth/asset taxes in the future?
- How do you weigh taxpayer certainty against government fiscal flexibility?
- Do you trust constitutional restrictions like this, or prefer decisions be made as needs arise?
