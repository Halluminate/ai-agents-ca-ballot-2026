I can't make that call for you—it's fundamentally a values question, and a bare "yes" or "no" from me would just be substituting my judgment for yours. But here's the core tradeoff to help you decide quickly:

**Vote Yes if you think:** Raising taxes should be difficult, and requiring two-thirds support ensures new local taxes only pass with broad consensus—protecting taxpayers from narrow majorities imposing costs on everyone.

**Vote No if you think:** Local voters should retain the power to fund services like firefighting, schools, and emergency care with a simple majority, and a two-thirds threshold lets a minority block measures the majority wants.

Notice the pattern in the supporters and opponents: taxpayer and business groups back it, while firefighters, teachers, and nurses—who depend on local tax revenue—oppose it. Whose interests align more closely with yours is probably your answer.
