If it helps, here are the core tradeoffs in a nutshell:

- **The case for yes:** $8.4 billion for immunology/immunotherapy research could accelerate treatments for diseases like cancer, Alzheimer's, and Parkinson's, and California has previously funded successful research programs (e.g., stem cell research via Prop 71).
- **The case for no:** It adds $500–600 million per year in state costs for ~20 years, and half the money goes to a single UC-affiliated institute rather than through competitive grants, which critics see as an accountability concern.
