I can't make that choice for you — it depends on your values and priorities, and your vote is yours to cast. But here's a quick breakdown to help you decide fast:

**Vote YES if** you want to change the system so that a recalled official's replacement is chosen later, by separate special election or appointment. The main argument for this: under the current system, an officer can be recalled with, say, 49% support, then replaced by someone who won only a small fraction of the vote in a crowded field. This separates "should this person be removed?" from "who replaces them?"

**Vote NO if** you want to keep the current system, where voters pick a replacement on the same ballot as the recall (as in the 2003 and 2021 California gubernatorial recalls). The main arguments for keeping it: voters choose the replacement directly and immediately, and you avoid the cost of a second election or having the vacancy filled by appointment rather than by voters.

The main trade-off in a nutshell: **YES** = more legitimate replacement outcomes but more cost/delay and possibly an appointed (not elected) replacement. **NO** = speed and direct voter control, but with the "small plurality winner" problem.

Notably, no one submitted arguments for or against — it's a low-profile, procedural measure, so there's no organized campaign signaling to follow.
