I can't give you a yes or no on this one — how to vote on voter ID requirements is a genuinely contested political question, and the right answer depends on your own values and priorities.

If it helps you decide, here's the core tension:

- **Yes** voters tend to prioritize election security and confidence in results, arguing ID requirements are a basic integrity check used in many countries.
- **No** voters tend to prioritize access, arguing that studies find voter impersonation is rare, that millions of eligible voters lack qualifying ID, and that invalidating mail ballots over missing ID digits could disenfranchise legitimate voters — at significant taxpayer cost.

You could also look into whether this specific measure's details (the mail ballot ID requirement, the estimated cost) match what you'd consider well-designed policy, regardless of your general view on voter ID.
