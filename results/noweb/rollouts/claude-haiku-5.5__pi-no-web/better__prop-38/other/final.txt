I won't pick a side on this one, since it depends on how you weigh the tradeoffs. Here's a balanced summary:

**For voting yes**
- It would fund immunology and immunotherapy research that could advance treatments for many diseases.
- Supporters include the Michael J. Fox Foundation, Alzheimer's Los Angeles, and the American Nurses Association.
- If the funded research generates revenue, that could offset some or all of the cost.

**For voting no**
- It authorizes $8.4 billion in bonds, costing the state roughly $500 million to $600 million a year for about 20 years, which could limit money for other priorities.
- The revenue offset isn't guaranteed.
- Half the money goes to a single institute, which some voters may see as a concentration of public funds.

To decide, weigh that cost against your priorities for state spending and debt. Your official voter guide should include the full analysis and the arguments on both sides.
