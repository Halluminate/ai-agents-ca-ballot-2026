I won't pick yes or no for you, since this comes down to how you weigh state spending and debt. Here's what you're weighing:

**What it does:** Authorizes $11.25 billion in state bonds for affordable rental housing, supportive housing, preserving existing affordable housing, veterans' mortgages, and down payment assistance.

**Cost:** The state would pay about $500–600 million a year for roughly 25 years, or about $12.5–15 billion in total repayments, including interest.

**Arguments for:**
- It directly funds building and preserving affordable housing.
- It targets veterans and homebuyers.
- Supporters include veterans' groups, a teachers' union, and Habitat for Humanity.

**Arguments against:**
- It commits billions in future state money to debt payments that could go to other priorities.
- Some critics argue that spending alone won't lower housing costs unless zoning and permitting rules also change.
- The voter guide shows no opposition arguments were submitted, so check nonpartisan analyses to hear the other side.

**How to decide:** You'd likely lean yes if you think the state should directly fund housing and you're comfortable with the added debt. You'd likely lean no if you prioritize limiting state debt or think other housing policies would work better.
