**What it does:** It sets deadlines for environmental review under CEQA, and for resolving lawsuits over approvals, for certain projects, including most housing, transportation, water, and health projects.

**Arguments for yes:**
- Supporters argue that CEQA reviews and lawsuits can take years and add cost, slowing housing, water, and hospital projects.
- Firm deadlines would make project timelines more predictable.
- Listed supporters include the California Council for Affordable Housing, the California Water Association, and the California Children's Hospital Association.

**Arguments for no:**
- Opponents worry that compressed timelines could weaken review of air quality, wildlife, and public health impacts.
- They may argue that shorter windows limit public input and the ability to challenge projects in court.
- Listed opponents include the Coalition for Clean Air, California Environmental Voters, Physicians for Social Responsibility LA, and the National Wildlife Federation.

**Cost:** State and local governments would face likely initial costs in the high tens of millions, potentially over $100 million a year, partly offset by fees. Longer-term effects are uncertain.
