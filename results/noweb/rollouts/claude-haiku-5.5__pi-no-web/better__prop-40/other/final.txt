I can't give a yes or no on this, since it depends on values and priorities where reasonable people disagree. Here's a balanced summary to help you decide.

**What it does**
- Imposes a one-time 5% tax on taxpayers with assets over $1 billion.
- Directs most of the revenue, estimated at tens of billions of dollars over several years, primarily to health care.
- Exempts that revenue from the constitutional school funding requirement and the state spending limit.
- May reduce income tax revenue from billionaires by under $1 billion a year.

**Arguments for yes** (supporters include Sen. Bernie Sanders and SEIU United Healthcare Workers West)
- It would raise a large sum for health care by asking the wealthiest residents to contribute more.
- Supporters argue that extreme wealth should contribute directly to public needs.

**Arguments for no** (opponents include the California School Boards Association and California Taxpayers Association)
- Schools wouldn't automatically receive a share of the revenue.
- Assets can be hard to value and tax fairly, and critics worry that wealthy residents might leave or reduce investment in California, shrinking income tax revenue.
- Because the tax is one-time, it doesn't create a lasting source of health care funding.

**Questions to weigh**
- Do you support a one-time tax on billionaires to fund health care?
- How much do you prioritize protections for school funding?
- How concerned are you about revenue losses or people leaving the state?

The official voter guide and the Legislative Analyst's full analysis have more detail on both sides.
