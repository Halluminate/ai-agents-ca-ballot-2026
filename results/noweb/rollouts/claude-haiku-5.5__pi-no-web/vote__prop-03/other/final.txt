I can't give you a yes or no on this one. It's a contested political question, and the right vote depends on your priorities. I can help you weigh it, though.

**What it does:** It makes permanent the higher income tax rates on people earning over $371,000 (adjusted for inflation), which voters approved in 2016. Without this measure, those rates would expire in 2031. It would keep roughly $5 to $15 billion a year flowing to schools and health care. Because it's a constitutional amendment, changing it later would require another vote.

**Arguments for yes:**
- It prevents a large drop in school and health funding after 2030.
- It applies only to high earners.
- Voters already approved these rates once, so this extends an existing tax rather than creating a new one.
- Supporters include the California Teachers Association, the California State PTA, and school nurses.

**Arguments for no:**
- California's top income tax rates are already among the highest in the country, and critics say they push high earners and businesses out of state.
- Locking the tax into the constitution limits future lawmakers' flexibility in budgeting.
- Income from high earners, such as capital gains and stock options, fluctuates a lot, so the revenue can be unpredictable.
- Opponents include the California Taxpayers Association, the Family Business Association of California, and the California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce.

**To decide,** ask yourself whether high earners should keep paying these rates to fund schools and health care, and whether you'd rather lock that in or leave it to future legislatures. The official ballot arguments and the nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office analysis are in your state voter guide at voterguide.sos.ca.gov.
