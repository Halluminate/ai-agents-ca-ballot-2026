I can't give you a yes or no on this one. It's a judgment call on a contested policy, and it depends on how you weigh housing affordability against the financial risk of a large state program. Here's a quick breakdown.

**What it does:** Authorizes up to $25 billion in bonds to fund fixed-rate mortgages covering up to 17% of the price of newly built homes under about $1.5 million. Buyers must be California residents who live in the home, meet income limits, and put at least 3% down. Borrowers repay the bonds through their mortgage payments.

**Case for yes:**
- Helps middle-income buyers afford new homes in a high-cost state.
- Focuses on new construction, which could add housing supply.
- The official fiscal summary projects no direct state or local costs.

**Case for no:**
- $25 billion is a large commitment. The "no state cost" claim assumes borrowers keep paying. If defaults rise, bondholders take losses, and the state could face pressure to step in.
- Subsidized demand can push prices up if new supply doesn't keep pace.
- Limiting aid to new homes leaves out buyers of existing homes, and critics may see it as favoring builders.

**To decide:** Read the Legislative Analyst's full analysis and the arguments for and against in the official voter guide. This summary lists no opponents, but that doesn't mean the measure is uncontroversial.

**Simple test:** Vote yes if you want the state to expand homeownership through new construction and you're comfortable with the financial risk. Vote no if you'd rather the state stay out of this kind of lending or think the money is better spent elsewhere.
