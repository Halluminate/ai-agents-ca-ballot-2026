I can't give you a yes or no on this one. It's a judgment call about how much debt the state should take on, and reasonable voters disagree. Here's what to weigh:

**Arguments for:**
- $8.4 billion could speed up research into immunology and immunotherapy, which may lead to treatments for cancer, neurological, and other diseases.
- Supporters include patient and disease organizations such as the Michael J. Fox Foundation, Alzheimer's Los Angeles, and the Kidney Cancer Association.
- If the research generates revenue, part or all of the state's cost could be offset.

**Arguments against:**
- It would cost roughly $500 to $600 million a year for about 20 years. These are general obligation bonds, repaid from the state's general fund, so the money competes with schools, health care, and other priorities.
- Half the funding goes to a single institute, which raises questions about competition and oversight.
- The revenue offset isn't guaranteed.

**Questions to ask yourself:**
- Is the potential medical payoff worth borrowing this much?
- Am I comfortable with half the money going to one institution?
- Do I trust the state to manage a long-term debt commitment of this size?

For more detail, read the Legislative Analyst's analysis and the official arguments in the state voter guide at voterguide.sos.ca.gov. California also funded stem cell research with bonds in 2004 (Prop 71), so looking at how that worked out may help.
