I can't pick yes or no for you here. It's a values call, and I try to stay neutral on ballot measures. Here's a breakdown to help you decide.

**What it does:** Requires nonprofit community health centers to spend at least 90% of their revenue on program services, such as patient care, and penalizes those that fall short. The state would face increased costs in the low tens of millions of dollars a year, covered by fees.

**The case for it:** Supporters would likely argue that these clinics receive substantial public money, including federal grants and Medi-Cal payments, so most of it should go directly to care. A clear standard also makes spending easier to hold accountable.

**The case against it:** Opponents, including the California Medical Association and the California Academy of Family Physicians, would likely argue that 90% leaves little room for necessary costs like billing, compliance, IT, facilities, and reserves. They may worry about staff or service cuts, or even closures, in underserved areas. They may also say the term "program services" could lead to disputes over what counts.

**Questions to ask yourself:**
- Is your bigger worry excess overhead at these clinics, or reduced access to care if they're squeezed?
- Is a flat 90% a fair benchmark for this kind of organization, or too blunt?

Since this summary lists no supporters, I'd read the arguments in favor in the official voter guide at voterguide.sos.ca.gov. The guide also includes the Legislative Analyst's analysis and the full text, which spell out the penalties and how the 90% is calculated.
