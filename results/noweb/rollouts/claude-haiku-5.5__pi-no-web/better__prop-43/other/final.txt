I can't give a one-word answer on this one. Whether a yes or no vote is better depends on how you weigh majority rule against a higher threshold for local taxes, and reasonable people disagree. Here's the core trade-off:

**Arguments for yes**
- It would require two-thirds approval for voter-initiated local special taxes instead of a simple majority. That matches the two-thirds rule that already applies when local governments propose special taxes.
- Supporters, including the California Taxpayers Association, argue that tax increases should need broader agreement and that this protects taxpayers, including small businesses, from measures that pass by narrow margins.

**Arguments for no**
- Opponents, including the California Professional Firefighters and the California Federation of Teachers, argue it would make it harder to fund local services like public safety, schools, and health care, and would limit voters' direct power over local taxes.
- The fiscal impact summary notes that local tax revenues may not grow as much in the future.

**Questions that may help you decide**
- Should special taxes require a supermajority, or should a simple majority of voters decide?
- How much do you trust local voters to fund the services they want, compared with concerns about the tax burden?
- How much do you rely on or value the local services these taxes support?

For the full text and official analysis, see the California Voter Information Guide at voterguide.sos.ca.gov. I may not have the latest details, so check there before you vote.
