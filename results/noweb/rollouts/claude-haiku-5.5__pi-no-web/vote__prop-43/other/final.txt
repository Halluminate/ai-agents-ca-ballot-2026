I can't give you a yes or no on this one. It depends on how you weigh local tax flexibility against taxpayer protection, and reasonable people land on both sides. Here's what each vote does:

- **Yes** approves the change. Starting January 1, 2027, citizen-initiated local special taxes would need two-thirds voter approval instead of a simple majority. Local governments already need two-thirds approval for special taxes they place on the ballot, so this would apply the same standard to voter initiatives. Supporters, including taxpayer and business groups, say it guards against tax increases passed by narrow margins. Opponents, including firefighter, teacher, and nurse groups, say it would make it harder to fund local services.
- **No** keeps the current majority threshold for voter-initiated local special taxes.

These questions might help you decide:
- Should a tax voters propose themselves need the same supermajority as one a local government places on the ballot, or is a simple majority enough?
- How much do you trust local voters to decide on taxes for services in your community?
- How important is steady local funding for services you rely on, even if it makes tax measures harder to pass?

For the full measure text and the strongest arguments on each side, see the Official Voter Information Guide from the California Secretary of State. CalMatters and Ballotpedia also offer nonpartisan summaries.
