I won't pick a side for you on this one, since it depends on how you weigh the tradeoffs. Here's a quick way to decide:

- **Vote yes** if you'd rather fill a recall vacancy through a later special election or appointment than through a replacement chosen on the same ballot as the recall.
- **Vote no** if you'd rather keep the current system, where voters pick the replacement at the same time they vote on the recall.

The main arguments, in general terms since no official ones were submitted:

- **For the change:** Separating the two decisions lets voters focus on each one. A replacement chosen from a crowded field on the recall ballot can also win with a small share of the vote.
- **Against the change:** Appointment means voters don't choose the replacement directly, and a separate special election can add cost and delay. The official analysis says the net cost effect is unknown, with possible savings or costs in the millions depending on the office.

For more detail, check your official voter information guide or a nonpartisan source like the League of Women Voters.
