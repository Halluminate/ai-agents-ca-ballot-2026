I can't give a yes or no on this one. The answer depends on how you weigh the trade-offs, which is a values call. Here's a balanced summary:

**Arguments for yes**
- It would fund fixed-rate mortgages covering up to 17% of the price of new homes under about $1.5 million, aiming to make homeownership more attainable for middle-income buyers.
- The bonds are repaid from borrowers' mortgage payments, and the measure lists no direct state or local costs.
- Supporters include the State Treasurer and the nurses' and carpenters' unions.

**Arguments for no**
- The $25 billion bond authorization is a large public financing commitment, even if repayment comes from mortgages rather than the state budget.
- Some economists argue that subsidized financing can push up prices in tight housing markets without adding supply.
- Some benefits may go to buyers who would have bought a home without the program.
