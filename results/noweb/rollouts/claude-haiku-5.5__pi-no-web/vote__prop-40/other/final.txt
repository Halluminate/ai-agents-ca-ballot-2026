I won't pick yes or no for you on this one. It depends on how you weigh taxing billionaires to fund health care against the risks of a wealth tax, and reasonable people land on both sides.

**The case for yes**
- A one-time 5% tax on assets over $1 billion could raise tens of billions of dollars, mostly for health care.
- Supporters, including Sen. Bernie Sanders and SEIU United Healthcare Workers West, argue that billionaire wealth has grown fast, that many billionaires pay lower effective tax rates than wage earners, and that a one-time tax avoids an ongoing burden.

**The case for no**
- Valuing wealth, especially private businesses and other hard-to-price assets, is difficult and can lead to disputes.
- Critics warn that wealthy residents could leave, which could reduce income tax revenue. The state estimates a possible ongoing loss of under $1 billion a year.
- Because the money is one-time, critics say it can't sustain ongoing health programs.
- The revenue is exempt from school funding requirements, a concern for opponents like the California School Boards Association.

**A quick way to decide:** Vote yes if you support taxing billionaires' wealth to fund health care and you're comfortable with the school funding exemption. Vote no if you'd rather avoid a wealth tax or think the risks outweigh the benefits.

For the full official analysis and arguments, check the California Secretary of State's voter information guide or the Legislative Analyst's Office.
