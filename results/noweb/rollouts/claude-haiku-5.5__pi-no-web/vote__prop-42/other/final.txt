I won't pick yes or no for you here. This is a contested political question about how the state raises revenue, and reasonable people land on both sides.

**Yes** would add a constitutional ban on:
- New state taxes on personal property, meaning anything you own other than real estate (which could include investments, not just cars or boats)
- New taxes that apply retroactively based on past activities

It would also void conflicting taxes enacted after January 1, 2026.

**No** would leave the state's current tax powers in place, so lawmakers or voters could still pass taxes like these.

**Supporters** (including the California Professional Firefighters and the State Building & Construction Trades Council) generally argue it protects people and businesses from new property taxes and surprise retroactive taxes.

**Opponents** (including Sen. Bernie Sanders and SEIU United Healthcare Workers West) likely argue it closes off a way to raise revenue for public services, especially from wealthy individuals.

The official fiscal analysis says tax revenue could grow more slowly in the future. The January 1, 2026 date and retroactivity language suggest it's aimed at a proposed one-time tax on billionaires' wealth, but check the official voter guide from the California Secretary of State for the full text and arguments from both sides.
