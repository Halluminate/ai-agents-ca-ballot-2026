I can't give you a yes or no on this one. It depends on how you weigh spending limits against funding priorities, and reasonable people disagree.

**What it does**
- Nullifies state taxes enacted after January 1, 2026 that exempt their revenue from the voter-approved state spending limit.
- Requires a pre-election audit and recurring audits of programs funded by new special taxes.
- The official summary says the net fiscal effect is unknown because it depends on future decisions by voters and lawmakers.

**Common arguments for**
- Prevents lawmakers from getting around the spending limit voters approved.
- Adds transparency and accountability for special-tax programs.
- Supporters include CalTax, CalCPAs, and the CalAsian Chamber of Commerce.

**Common arguments against**
- Could limit how new taxes for priorities like health care can be structured.
- Audit requirements could delay or add costs to new programs.
- Could reduce the state's flexibility to respond to future needs.
- Opponents include Sen. Bernie Sanders and SEIU-UHW.

**Questions that might help**
- Should revenue from new taxes count against the spending limit, or is exempting some taxes a reasonable tool?
- Is more oversight of special-tax programs worth the possibility of slowing them down?
- Which matters more to you: limiting government spending growth, or keeping flexibility to fund services?
