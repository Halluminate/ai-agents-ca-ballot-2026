I'm not going to pick a side here, since this comes down to values and priorities that reasonable people weigh differently. Here's a balanced summary:

**Reasons to vote yes**
- It funds several kinds of housing programs: multifamily rental housing, veterans' mortgages, supportive housing, preservation of existing affordable units, and down payment assistance.
- Supporters, including US Vets, the California Federation of Teachers, and Habitat for Humanity California, argue that state funds can leverage federal and private money to address a serious affordability shortage.

**Reasons to vote no**
- It costs the state about $500 to $600 million a year for roughly 25 years, which competes with other budget priorities and commits future taxpayers.
- Critics may argue that borrowing doesn't address the main drivers of high housing costs, such as local zoning, permitting rules, and construction costs.
