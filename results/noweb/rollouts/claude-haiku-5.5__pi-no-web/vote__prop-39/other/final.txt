I'd rather not give a yes or no on this one, since it's a contested political question. The right vote depends on how you weigh election security against making sure every eligible citizen can vote. Here's what each choice would do:

- **Yes** approves the measure. In-person voters would need government-issued ID, and mail ballot envelopes would need the last four digits of an ID number. Ballots missing that number would be invalidated.
- **No** keeps current voting rules.

**Supporters**, including the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, argue it strengthens public confidence in elections and that government ID is already required for many everyday activities.

**Opponents**, including the ACLU and the League of Women Voters of California, argue it could keep eligible voters who lack ID from casting ballots, that in-person fraud is rare, and that small envelope errors could throw out valid votes. The official fiscal estimate puts implementation costs at tens of millions to a few hundred million dollars a year.
