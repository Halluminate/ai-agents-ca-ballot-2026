I'd rather not give a one-word answer on this one, since it depends on how you weigh the tradeoffs. Here's a neutral summary:

**What it does:** Requires nonprofit Federally Qualified Health Centers (community clinics serving medically underserved areas) to spend at least 90% of revenue on program services that advance their charitable purpose, such as patient care. Clinics that fall short face penalties. The state would face increased costs of low tens of millions of dollars per year, covered by fees.

**Supporters:** None submitted an argument.

**Opponents:** American Academy of Pediatrics California, California School Nurses Organization, California Academy of Family Physicians, and California Medical Association.

**Key questions:** Is a fixed 90% spending requirement an effective way to make sure clinic revenue goes to patient care? Would it leave enough room for necessary administrative, compliance, and operating costs, or could it reduce services in underserved areas? The official voter guide has the full arguments for and against and the nonpartisan fiscal analysis.
