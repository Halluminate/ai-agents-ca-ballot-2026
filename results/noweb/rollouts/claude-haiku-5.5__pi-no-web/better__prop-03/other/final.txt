I won't pick a side on this one, since it depends on how you weigh the tradeoffs. Here's a quick look at each side:

**Arguments for yes:**
- It makes permanent the existing tax rates on incomes over $371,000 (adjusted for inflation), so school and health care funding doesn't lose this revenue when the tax expires in 2031.
- It would preserve an estimated $5 billion to $15 billion in annual state income tax revenue.
- It affects only high earners and extends rates voters already approved rather than creating a new tax.

**Arguments for no:**
- It makes a tax permanent that would otherwise expire. As a constitutional amendment, changing it later would generally require another statewide vote.
- Opponents argue that high income tax rates can push high earners and businesses out of state. Many small businesses are taxed as pass-throughs, so higher individual rates hit them directly.
- Earmarking revenue limits lawmakers' flexibility, and income from high earners can swing sharply with the stock market.

The official California Voter Information Guide and the Legislative Analyst's Office analysis are good places to dig deeper.
