I'll stay neutral on how you should vote, so I won't give a bare yes or no, but here's a quick rundown.

**What it does:** It raises the cap on California's Rainy Day Fund, so the state can save more during good years and draw on it during downturns.

**Case for yes:**
- A bigger cushion could soften cuts to schools, health care, and public safety in a recession.
- It has broad support and no organized opposition.

**Case for no:**
- Money held in reserve isn't spent on current needs or returned to taxpayers.
- Some people prefer the current cap, or want to see the full text for any other budget-rule changes before agreeing to it.

Which way it points depends on how you weigh fiscal caution against spending or tax relief now. The state voter guide and the Legislative Analyst's Office analysis have the full details.
