I don't think I should give a one-word answer on this. It turns on values that reasonable people weigh differently. Here are the main tradeoffs.

**Reasons someone might vote yes:**
- Separating the recall vote from the replacement process could keep a replacement from winning with a small plurality in a crowded field on the same ballot.
- A later special election or an appointment gives more time to vet and select a successor.
- Depending on the office, the measure could save millions of dollars.

**Reasons someone might vote no:**
- Today, voters decide both whether to remove an official and who replaces them in one step. This measure ends that.
- Appointment replaces a direct public vote with a decision by officials, which some see as less democratic.
- Depending on the office, a separate election could cost millions more and extend the time before the office is filled.
- The fiscal impact is uncertain, and no supporters or opponents submitted arguments.
