I'm not going to pick a side on a contested ballot measure, since the answer depends on your own values and priorities. Here is a neutral summary.

**What a "yes" vote does:**
- Nullifies state taxes enacted after Jan 1, 2026 that exempt their revenues from the voter-approved state spending limit.
- Requires pre-election and recurring audits of programs funded by new special taxes.
- Supporters would likely point to fiscal accountability and keeping new revenue under the spending limit.

**What a "no" vote does:**
- Leaves current law in place, so future taxes could be structured to exempt their revenues from the spending limit, and no new audit requirements would apply.
- Opponents would likely point to reduced flexibility to raise and spend new revenue, and to the added constitutional constraints.

**Key uncertainty:** The nonpartisan fiscal analysis says the net fiscal effect is unknown, because it depends on future decisions by voters and policymakers.

**Questions that may help you decide:**
- How much do you value spending limits and audit requirements relative to flexibility in funding new programs?
- Do you think the audit requirements would improve accountability or add burdens?
- How do you weigh the supporters' and opponents' arguments?

For more detail, the official voter guide and the Legislative Analyst's Office analysis cover the full text and fiscal estimates.
