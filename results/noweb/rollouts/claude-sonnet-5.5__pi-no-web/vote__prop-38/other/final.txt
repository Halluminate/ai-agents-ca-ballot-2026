I won't give a yes or no on how to vote. It depends on how you weigh the tradeoffs, and that's your call. Based on the summary you shared, here's how I'd frame it.

**Reasons people might vote yes**
- It dedicates a large, stable source of funding to immunology and immunotherapy research, which relates to cancer, autoimmune disease, and neurodegenerative conditions.
- Major patient and health organizations support it.
- Some or all of the cost could be offset if the research generates revenue.

**Reasons people might vote no**
- The state would pay roughly $500-600 million a year for about 20 years, or about $10-12 billion total, which competes with other budget priorities.
- Half the money goes to a single institution rather than to competitive grants.
- The revenue offset is uncertain.
- Critics may object to funding research through bonds and a ballot measure instead of the regular budget process.

**Questions to ask yourself**
- Do I want the state to take on long-term debt for medical research?
- Am I comfortable with half the funds going to one institute?
- How likely do I think the research is to produce returns that offset costs?
