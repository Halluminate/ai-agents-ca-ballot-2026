I'm not going to give a yes or no. How to vote on a contested political question is your call, but here is what the measure turns on.

**What it does:** Voters would decide only whether to remove an official. The replacement would then be chosen afterward by special election or appointment, instead of on the same ballot.

**Reasons to vote yes**
- A replacement can't win with a small plurality in a crowded field on the same ballot as the recall.
- The replacement is chosen through a more deliberate process.
- Recalls driven mainly by political motives could become less attractive.
- Costs could fall for some offices, according to the fiscal note.

**Reasons to vote no**
- Voters lose the chance to pick a replacement at the same time they remove someone.
- If the replacement is appointed, voters have less say.
- There could be a vacancy or interim period, and a second election could cost millions for some offices.
- Recalls could become a weaker check on officials.

**Rule of thumb:** If you value a deliberate replacement process and avoiding plurality winners, lean yes. If you value direct voter choice and a quick, single-step process, lean no.

The key detail is who fills the vacancy for each office, a special election or an appointment. The full text and your official voter guide's nonpartisan analysis will say. Since no supporters or opponents submitted arguments, those sources matter even more.
