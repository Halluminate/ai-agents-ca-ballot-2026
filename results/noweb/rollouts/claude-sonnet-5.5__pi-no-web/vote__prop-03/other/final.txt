I won't give a yes or no on this one. It's a contested tax-and-spending question that comes down to your own values, and that's your call. Here's what the choice involves.

**Reasons people vote yes**
- It avoids a revenue drop of $5-15 billion a year when the current tax expires in 2031.
- It gives schools and health programs more predictable funding.
- The tax applies only to very high earners (over about $371,000, adjusted for inflation).

**Reasons people vote no**
- It turns a temporary tax into a permanent one, so voters wouldn't get another chance to reassess it.
- High-income revenue is volatile, and a budget that depends on it can swing in downturns.
- Opponents worry about effects on small businesses (especially pass-through owners), and about high earners leaving the state.
- As a constitutional amendment, it's harder to change later.

**Questions that may help you decide**
- Do you prefer locking in funding, or keeping a sunset that forces periodic re-approval?
- How much do you trust the allocation of revenue? The title mentions schools and health care, but the summary only mentions education, so check the full text.
- How do you weigh the benefits of stable funding against the economic risks opponents raise?
