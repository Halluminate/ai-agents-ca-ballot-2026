I'm not going to give a yes or no on this one. It turns on value judgments about taxes and public spending, and that's your call. Here's what each side is weighing.

**Reasons people vote yes**
- It prevents a revenue drop of roughly $5 to $15 billion a year when the current tax expires in 2031.
- It gives schools and health care a more predictable funding source.
- It applies only to high earners (above about $371,000).
- Voters already approved these rates once.

**Reasons people vote no**
- Making the tax permanent removes the sunset, which forces a periodic public re-vote.
- The state's budget already depends heavily on high earners and capital gains, which makes revenue volatile. The $5 to $15 billion range reflects that.
- Opponents worry about high earners and businesses leaving the state, or about California's competitiveness.
- As a constitutional amendment, it's harder to change later, and earmarking revenue limits budget flexibility.

**A rough guide**
- If you value stable education and health funding and are comfortable with higher taxes on top earners, the yes case will likely appeal to you.
- If you prefer sunset provisions, lower top tax rates, or more budget flexibility, the no case will likely appeal to you.

The official state voter guide and the nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office analysis give more detail on the fiscal estimates.
