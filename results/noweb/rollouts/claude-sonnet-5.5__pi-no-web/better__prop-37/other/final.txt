I won't give a one-word vote on a ballot measure, since it depends on your own priorities. Here are the main considerations.

**Reasons to lean yes**
- The fiscal analysis finds no direct state or local costs, because the bonds are repaid from mortgage payments.
- It could help middle-income buyers with the down payment gap and encourage new home construction.
- Labor groups and the State Treasurer back it, and no opposition was submitted.

**Reasons to lean no, or questions to weigh**
- It applies only to newly built homes, which may favor new construction over existing homes.
- Boosting buyer demand without much added supply could push prices up.
- "No direct costs" assumes loans are repaid. Defaults or a housing downturn could create risk, and indirect effects may not be captured.
- No submitted opposition doesn't mean there are no critics or tradeoffs.

For more detail, read the full text of the measure and the Legislative Analyst's Office analysis in the voter guide. I'm also happy to go deeper on any part of it.
