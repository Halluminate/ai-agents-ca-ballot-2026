I won't reduce this to a one-word answer. How to vote on a contested measure depends on your values, and that's your call. Here's a quick way to decide.

**What it does**
- A **yes** vote adds this to the state constitution. Any state tax enacted after Jan. 1, 2026 whose revenue is exempted from the state spending limit would be nullified. New special taxes would also face pre-election and recurring audits.
- A **no** vote leaves current law alone. New taxes could still be written to exempt their revenue from the spending limit.

**Arguments for yes**
- New tax revenue should be subject to the same spending limit as other revenue, so no one can sidestep it.
- Audits add transparency and accountability for dedicated taxes.

**Arguments for no**
- Voters and lawmakers should be able to create new, dedicated funding sources, such as for health care, without those funds counting toward the cap.
- A constitutional amendment is hard to reverse, and audit mandates add cost and procedural hurdles.
- It may be aimed at specific tax proposals rather than being a neutral rule. The opposition from a health care workers' union and Bernie Sanders hints at this, so check the voter guide for context.

**Questions to ask yourself**
- Should the spending limit apply to all new state revenue, or should voters be able to exempt particular taxes?
- Do you favor extra guardrails on new taxes, or more flexibility to fund new priorities?
