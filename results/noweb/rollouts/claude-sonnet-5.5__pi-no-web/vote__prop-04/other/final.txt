I won't give a one-word yes or no, since this comes down to values and priorities that are yours to weigh. Here's what I can lay out instead.

**What your vote means**
- **Yes:** Repeals the ban, so state and local governments could create public funding programs for candidate campaigns. Money earmarked for education, transportation, or public safety couldn't be used.
- **No:** Keeps the ban in place.

Going by the summary, the measure allows these programs but doesn't create or fund one. The few hundred thousand dollars a year is only the state's cost of advising governments. Any real program's costs would come from later decisions.

**Arguments for a yes vote (as supporters frame them)**
- Public funding could reduce candidates' reliance on large donors and special interests.
- It could make it easier for people without wealthy backers to run.
- It gives state and local governments the option to try it.

**Arguments for a no vote (as opponents frame them)**
- Taxpayer money would go to political campaigns, including for candidates taxpayers oppose.
- That money could be spent on other priorities.
- Programs might not limit the influence of big money, such as through independent spending, and could cost more over time.

**Questions that may help you decide**
- Do you think public funding would reduce the influence of private money, or just add a new cost?
- Do you want state and local governments to have this option, even if you wouldn't support a specific program?
- Does the limit on using education, transportation, and public safety funds address your concerns about costs?
