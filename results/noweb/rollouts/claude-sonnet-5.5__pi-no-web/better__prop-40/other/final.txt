I don't give a yes or no recommendation on contested ballot measures, since it depends on your values and priorities. Here are the main considerations from the summary you shared.

**Reasons people might vote yes**
- It could raise tens of billions of dollars, mainly for health care.
- It applies only to taxpayers with assets over $1 billion.
- It's a one-time tax, not a permanent rate increase.

**Reasons people might vote no**
- It exempts the new revenue from constitutional school funding requirements and the state spending limit, which is a concern for school groups.
- It could reduce income tax revenue by up to $1 billion per year if billionaires leave or change their tax behavior.
- The revenue is temporary, while health care costs are ongoing.
- Opponents include school, taxpayer, and primary care groups.

The nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office analysis and the official voter guide go into more detail. I'm happy to go deeper on any of these points.
