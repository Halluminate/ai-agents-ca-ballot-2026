I can't give you a yes or no. This is a contested political question, and the choice depends on your own values and priorities. Here are the main considerations on each side, based on the summary you shared.

**Arguments for a "yes" vote**
- It would raise tens of billions of dollars, mainly for health care, from a very small group of taxpayers.
- The tax is one-time, so it doesn't create a permanent new tax burden.
- Supporters see it as a way to address funding gaps in health care and reduce wealth inequality.

**Arguments for a "no" vote**
- Wealth taxes are hard to administer, especially when valuing private or illiquid assets, and may face legal challenges.
- The measure could prompt some billionaires to leave California. The fiscal analysis notes a possible ongoing loss of up to $1 billion a year in income tax revenue.
- The revenue is temporary, but health care needs are ongoing.
- It exempts the revenue from the constitutional school funding requirements and spending limit. Opponents may see that as a problematic precedent, and school groups are among them.

**Questions that might help you decide**
- Do you think a one-time wealth tax is a good way to fund health care, or would you prefer other mechanisms?
- How much weight do you give the risk of reduced future tax revenue?
- How do you feel about changing the state constitution to exempt this revenue from school funding rules?
