I'm not going to give a one-word vote recommendation. How to vote on a ballot measure is your call, and this one comes down to a values tradeoff. Here's a quick rundown.

**What it does:** It raises how much California can save in its rainy day reserve, so there's a bigger cushion during recessions.

**Reasons to vote yes**
- State revenue swings with the economy and stock market. A bigger reserve can soften cuts to schools, health care, and public safety during downturns.
- It has broad support and no submitted opposition in the voter guide.

**Reasons to vote no**
- Money held in reserve isn't available for current needs or tax relief, and some may think the state already saves enough.
- It's a constitutional amendment, so it's harder to undo later. Rules for tapping the fund still apply, so the money isn't always easy to use when needed.
- No submitted opposition doesn't mean no one has concerns.

**A tiebreaker:** Would you rather the state save more for downturns, or keep more flexibility to spend or cut taxes now?
