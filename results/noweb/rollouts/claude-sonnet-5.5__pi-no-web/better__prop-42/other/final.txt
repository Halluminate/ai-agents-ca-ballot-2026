I'm going to skip the one-word answer. Which way to vote on a tax measure depends on your values and priorities, and I don't think I should tell you how to vote. Here is what each choice means.

**A "yes" vote** would amend the state constitution to:
- Bar new state taxes on owning personal property (everything other than real estate).
- Bar new state taxes that apply retroactively based on past activity.
- Nullify conflicting taxes enacted after January 1, 2026.

**A "no" vote** keeps the constitution as is, so lawmakers and voters could still propose taxes of those kinds.

**Arguments commonly made for yes:**
- It gives taxpayers and businesses predictability and protects against retroactive taxation.
- It guards against asset-based taxes that could expand over time.
- Putting limits in the constitution makes them harder to change.

**Arguments commonly made for no:**
- It limits the state's future ability to raise revenue, including through wealth-type taxes.
- Constitutional amendments are hard to reverse if circumstances change.
- The fiscal analysis notes that revenues might not grow as much, which could affect funding for public services.

To weigh it yourself, you could read the nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office analysis and the full text in the official voter guide. It's also worth checking who funds the campaigns on each side.
