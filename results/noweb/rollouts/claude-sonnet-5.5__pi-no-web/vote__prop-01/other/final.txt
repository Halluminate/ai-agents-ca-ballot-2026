I won't give a one-word answer on this. How to vote depends on your own priorities, and reasonable people land on both sides. Here's a quick way to weigh it.

**Reasons people vote yes**
- It funds affordable rental housing, supportive housing, veterans' home loans, and downpayment help, which target housing costs and homelessness.
- Bonds spread the cost over decades rather than requiring cuts or tax increases now.
- Housing advocates, veterans' groups, and senior and teacher organizations back it.

**Reasons people vote no**
- Repaying it costs $500-600 million a year for about 25 years, roughly $12.5-15 billion total against $11.25 billion borrowed, so interest is a real cost.
- That annual payment competes with other budget priorities like schools, health care, and transportation.
- Some argue the bigger drivers of high housing costs are zoning, permitting, and construction costs, and that subsidies alone won't fix them.
- Some question how efficiently and transparently the money gets spent.

**Questions to ask yourself**
- Is housing affordability among the top issues you want the state to spend on?
- Are you comfortable with the state taking on this debt?
- Do you think bonds and subsidies are the right tool, or would you prefer regulatory reform?

"No opponents submitted" only means no one filed an official argument against it, not that the measure has no downsides.

The nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's analysis in your official voter guide covers how the money would be allocated. I can go deeper on any of these points if you'd like.
