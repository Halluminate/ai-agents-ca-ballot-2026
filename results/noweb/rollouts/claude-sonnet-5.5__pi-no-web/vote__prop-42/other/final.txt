I'm not going to give a one-word vote recommendation. This is a contested measure, and the answer depends on your values and priorities. Here's what might help you decide.

**What your vote would do (based on the summary)**
- **Yes:** Amends the state constitution to bar new state taxes on owning personal property (stocks, business interests, and other non-real-estate assets) and new state taxes that apply retroactively to past activity. Conflicting taxes enacted after Jan. 1, 2026 would be nullified.
- **No:** Leaves the constitution as is, so lawmakers or voters could still pass these kinds of taxes, subject to existing legal limits.

**Common arguments**
- **For:** It protects against retroactive or surprise taxes, gives businesses and investors predictability, and blocks wealth-type taxes that could push people or capital out of state.
- **Against:** It permanently takes a revenue option off the table, since changing it later would take another statewide vote. It limits future funding flexibility, and courts could read "personal property" or "retroactive" more broadly than voters expect. The fiscal note says revenue may grow less.

**Things to consider**
- Should these tax limits be in the constitution rather than left to ordinary lawmaking?
- Do you trust the measure's wording to be narrow enough?
- Both sides have labor groups (firefighters and building trades vs. SEIU-UHW), so endorsements don't split along obvious lines. Look at who is funding each campaign.
