I can't give you a one-word answer on this. Whether to vote yes or no depends on how you weigh competing values, such as election security versus ballot access, and not on a question with a clear factual answer.

**Arguments for voting yes**
- Requiring ID is a simple safeguard that can deter fraud and increase public confidence in election results.
- Many states and countries require ID to vote.
- The measure covers both in-person and mail voting.

**Arguments for voting no**
- Eligible voters without government ID, such as some elderly, low-income, disabled, rural, or young voters, could be blocked from voting.
- Mail ballots could be invalidated over a missing or mistaken number, even if the voter is eligible.
- Opponents say documented voter impersonation is very rare, so the burden outweighs the problem.
- The fiscal note estimates costs of tens of millions to low hundreds of millions of dollars per year.

**Things worth checking in the official voter guide**
- Which IDs count, and whether getting one is free and accessible
- Whether voters can fix a mail ballot with a missing or incorrect number
- The nonpartisan fiscal analysis and the full text of the amendment
