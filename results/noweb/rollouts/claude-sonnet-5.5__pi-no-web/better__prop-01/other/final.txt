I don't tell people how to vote on ballot measures, so I won't answer with just yes or no. Here are the main tradeoffs.

**Reasons people vote yes**
- It directs significant funding to housing affordability, including veterans' mortgages, supportive housing, and preserving existing affordable units.
- Bonds spread the cost of large capital projects over many years.
- The listed supporters include veterans, teachers, seniors, and housing nonprofits, and no formal opposition was submitted.

**Reasons people vote no**
- The state would spend about $500-600 million a year for roughly 25 years, which includes interest and competes with other budget priorities.
- Some argue housing costs are driven more by zoning, permitting, and construction costs than by a lack of subsidy funding.
- Some doubt that bond-funded programs deliver housing efficiently or at the scale promised.
- "No opposition submitted" only means no one filed an official argument against it, not that there are no critics.

**Questions that may help you decide**
- Do you think bond debt is a good way to fund housing, compared with other approaches?
- How do you weigh the long-term repayment cost against the housing need?
- Do you trust the agencies that would administer these funds?

For more detail, the nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office analysis and the official voter guide cover the fiscal impact and program specifics.
