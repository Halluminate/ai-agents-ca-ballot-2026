I don't take sides on ballot measures, so I won't give a one-word recommendation. Here's a short summary to help you decide.

**What your vote does**
- **Yes:** Repeals the ban on state and local governments creating public funding programs for candidate campaigns. Programs couldn't use money earmarked for education, transportation, or public safety.
- **No:** Keeps the current ban in place.

**Arguments for yes**
- Public funding can reduce candidates' reliance on large private donors and special interests.
- It can make it easier for people without wealthy donor networks to run.
- The measure only permits programs. It doesn't create one, and the stated state cost is modest (a few hundred thousand dollars a year).

**Arguments for no**
- Taxpayer money would fund political campaigns, including for candidates or views some taxpayers oppose.
- Even with the earmark limits, public money could be spent on campaigns instead of other priorities.
- Opponents doubt that public financing reduces the influence of money in politics.

The state cost listed covers only answering questions from governments. Any actual program would have its own costs, set by later decisions of state or local governments.

If you tell me which matters more to you, limiting private money in politics or limiting government spending on campaigns, I can help you work out how that maps onto this measure.
