I can't give you a yes or no on this one. Voter ID is a contested political question, and the right vote depends on how you weigh competing values, so that choice should be yours. Here is a quick breakdown to help you decide.

**Case for voting yes**
- It adds a verification step, which supporters say increases confidence in election integrity.
- Government ID is already required for many everyday activities.
- Matching the last four digits of an ID on mail ballots adds a check to mail voting.

**Case for voting no**
- Eligible voters without ID, such as some elderly, low-income, rural, student, or disabled voters, could be blocked from voting.
- Opponents argue that in-person voter impersonation is extremely rare, so the rule addresses a very small problem.
- Mail ballots could be thrown out over a missing or mistaken number.
- It would cost tens of millions to low hundreds of millions of dollars a year.
- As a constitutional amendment, it would be harder to adjust later if problems arise.

**Questions to ask yourself**
1. Which concern matters more to you: preventing ineligible voting or preventing eligible voters from being turned away?
2. Does the full text provide free IDs and a fallback for people who can't get one? Your summary doesn't say, and it matters.
3. Is the cost worth it to you?
4. Are you comfortable locking this into the constitution rather than passing it as ordinary law?
