I can't give you a yes or no on this one, since it comes down to your values rather than facts. Here's the trade-off so you can decide quickly.

**What a Yes vote does:** If an official is recalled, voters would no longer pick the replacement on the same ballot. The seat would be filled afterward by appointment or a separate special election.

**Arguments for Yes**
- Under the current system, an official can be removed with just over 50% of the vote and replaced by someone who got far fewer votes, such as 25% in a crowded field.
- Separating the two decisions lets voters judge the recall on its own merits, and the replacement is chosen in a more focused process.

**Arguments for No**
- Voters lose the ability to choose the replacement at the same time they remove someone.
- If the seat is filled by appointment, voters may not get the change they wanted, since the appointee could share the recalled official's politics.
- A separate special election could add cost and leave the office without a permanent replacement for longer.
- Critics see it as weakening the recall as a check on officials.

**Quick way to decide**
- If your bigger concern is someone taking office with only a small share of the vote, **Yes** fits that view.
- If your bigger concern is keeping voters in direct control of who takes over, **No** fits that view.

For more detail, your official voter guide's nonpartisan analysis or Ballotpedia can explain exactly how each office would be filled under this measure.
