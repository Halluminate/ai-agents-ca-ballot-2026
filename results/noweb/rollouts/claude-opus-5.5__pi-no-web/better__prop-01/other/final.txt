I can't give a one-word answer here, because it depends on your priorities and reasonable voters disagree. Here's a quick breakdown:

**Case for Yes**
- Funds several housing needs: affordable rentals, preserving existing affordable units, supportive housing for people experiencing homelessness, veterans' home loans, and down payment help.
- Bonds spread the cost of long-lasting assets like housing over the decades they're used.
- Broad support from veterans', teachers', senior, and family-services groups, with no formal opposition filed.

**Case for No**
- Cost: $500–600 million a year for about 25 years (roughly $12.5–15 billion total), paid from the state general fund. That money can't go to schools, healthcare, or other services.
- Adds to long-term state debt.
- Critics argue California's subsidized housing is very expensive per unit, so the bond may produce fewer homes than the dollar figure suggests. They also argue zoning and permitting reform would do more to address the shortage.
- Bond measures often have no organized opposition, so "none submitted" doesn't necessarily mean there are no drawbacks.

**Key questions:** Do you think public spending is an effective way to tackle housing costs and homelessness? And is that worth the added debt and the tradeoff with other budget priorities?
