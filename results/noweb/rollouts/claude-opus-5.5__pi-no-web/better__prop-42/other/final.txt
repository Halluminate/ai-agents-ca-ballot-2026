I don't take sides on ballot measures, so I won't give a yes or no. Here's a quick breakdown to help you decide.

**What it does:** It amends the state constitution to ban two kinds of new state taxes:
- taxes on owning personal property (stocks, business stakes, cars, art, or anything other than real estate)
- taxes that apply retroactively based on past activity

It also voids conflicting taxes enacted after Jan. 1, 2026. That date and the opponents suggest it mainly targets the proposed one-time "billionaire tax" on net worth, which is based on residency as of Jan. 1, 2026. If that measure is also on your ballot, the two effectively compete.

**A YES vote fits if you believe:**
- Wealth taxes are bad policy. Assets are hard to value, and the tax could drive wealthy residents (and the income taxes they pay) out of state.
- It's unfair to tax people based on things they did before a tax existed.
- Everyone, not just billionaires, deserves a lasting constitutional guarantee against new taxes on what they own.

**A NO vote fits if you believe:**
- The state should keep the option to tax very large fortunes, for example to fund health care or offset federal cuts.
- A constitutional ban is too rigid, since undoing it would take another statewide vote.
- Its reach goes beyond the billionaire-tax fight and could limit future revenue in ways that are hard to predict, as the fiscal summary suggests.

**The core question:** Should new taxes on wealth and personal property be barred in the constitution, or left as an option for future voters and lawmakers?
