I can't give you a simple yes or no on this one. It's a contested question that depends on your values and priorities. Here are the main arguments on each side:

**Arguments for (Yes):**
- Raises tens of billions of dollars, mostly for health care
- Only affects people with over $1 billion in assets
- It's a one-time tax, not an ongoing one

**Arguments against (No):**
- Some billionaires may leave the state, and the fiscal analysis projects a possible ongoing loss of up to $1 billion a year in income tax revenue
- The revenue is exempt from school funding guarantees and the state spending limit, which is part of why school boards oppose it
- Wealth taxes raise practical problems, such as valuing private holdings, and may face legal challenges
- Temporary revenue funding ongoing health costs could leave a gap when it runs out

In general, if you prioritize health care funding and taxing extreme wealth, you'll probably lean yes. If you're more concerned about billionaires leaving, the school funding exemption, or setting a precedent for wealth taxes, you'll probably lean no.
