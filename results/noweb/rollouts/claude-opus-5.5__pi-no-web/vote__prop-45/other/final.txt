I know you asked for a yes or no, but I'd rather not make this call for you. It's a genuine trade-off, and the right vote depends on what you prioritize. Here's a quick way to decide:

**Lean YES if you believe:**
- California's housing and infrastructure shortages are urgent, and CEQA reviews and lawsuits add too much delay and cost
- CEQA lawsuits are often used to stall projects for reasons that aren't really environmental
- Faster timelines for housing, hospitals, water systems, and transportation are worth the added cost

**Lean NO if you believe:**
- Hard deadlines could lead to rushed reviews that miss impacts on air quality, wildlife, or public health
- Court deadlines could limit how thoroughly legal challenges are heard, weakening a key tool communities use against harmful projects
- The cost isn't justified. It's likely in the high tens of millions a year, possibly over $100 million, and only partly covered by fees.
- Fast-tracking "most" housing, transportation, water, and health projects is too broad and could include projects with significant environmental impacts

**Worth knowing either way:** Because this is an initiative statute, the Legislature generally can't change it without going back to voters, unless the measure itself allows amendments. That makes it more durable, but also harder to fix if problems come up.

I'm working only from this summary, so check the Legislative Analyst's analysis and the pro/con arguments in your official voter guide. CalMatters and the League of Women Voters also publish nonpartisan explainers.
