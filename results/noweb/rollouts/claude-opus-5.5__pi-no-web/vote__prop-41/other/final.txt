I'm not going to tell you how to vote on this one, since it comes down to your own priorities. Here's a quick breakdown to help you decide.

**What it does:** It amends the state constitution so any new state tax enacted after Jan. 1, 2026 is nullified if it exempts its revenue from the Gann Limit, the state spending cap voters approved in 1979. It also requires audits of programs funded by new special taxes (taxes earmarked for a specific purpose), both before the election and on an ongoing basis.

**Yes might fit you if you:**
- Want the voter-approved spending limit enforced, not worked around
- Think programs funded by special taxes should be audited to confirm the money is spent as promised
- Prefer making new state taxes harder to pass

**No might fit you if you:**
- Want voters and the Legislature to keep flexibility to fund priorities like healthcare with new taxes
- See the 1979 spending formula as outdated and don't want it to override taxes voters approve
- See the audits as extra cost and hurdles meant to make new taxes harder to pass

**Key context:** The opponents, SEIU-UHW and Sen. Sanders, have backed a proposed billionaire tax to fund healthcare, and this measure appears aimed at blocking taxes like that one. If that tax is also on your ballot, your view on it may be the real deciding factor. The official voter guide (voterguide.sos.ca.gov) has a nonpartisan analysis of how they'd interact.
