I can't give you a yes or no on this one. It depends on how you weigh the tradeoffs, so it's your call. Here's a quick rundown to help you decide:

**Case for YES**
- Funds housing for several groups: renters, veterans, people needing supportive housing, and first-time buyers
- California's housing shortage and costs are severe, and supporters see this as a needed investment
- It doesn't raise taxes directly. It's repaid from the state's existing general fund.
- It has a broad coalition of supporters, and no formal opposition argument was filed.

**Case for NO**
- It costs about $500–600 million a year for ~25 years, roughly $12.5–15 billion total. That money competes with other budget priorities like schools and healthcare.
- Critics note that publicly funded affordable units in California often cost several hundred thousand dollars or more each, so the money may produce fewer homes than hoped.
- Some argue the real bottlenecks are zoning, permitting, and construction costs, which bonds don't fix.
- It adds to state debt.

**Quick gut check:** If you think public spending on affordable housing is worth the long-term repayment cost, that points to yes. If you're more concerned about state debt or doubt these programs deliver enough housing per dollar, that points to no.
