I don't make yes/no voting recommendations, but here's a quick summary so you can decide:

- **Yes:** Nonprofit community health centers would face penalties if less than 90% of their revenue goes to "program services" such as patient care. The state's enforcement costs, in the low tens of millions per year, would be covered by fees.
- **No:** Nothing changes, and clinics stay under existing oversight.

**Case for:** It pushes clinics to spend more of their money on patients and less on overhead.

**Case against:** Because the rule is 90% of *revenue*, all overhead plus any savings or reserves must fit in the remaining 10%. That's a tight limit, and penalties could take money from clinics serving underserved patients. California's pediatrician, family physician, school nurse, and medical associations oppose it.

**Worth noting:** No one submitted an argument in favor, which is unusual for an initiative.

Vote yes if you want a strict, penalty-backed spending rule for these clinics. Vote no if you think the risk to clinic budgets outweighs that.
