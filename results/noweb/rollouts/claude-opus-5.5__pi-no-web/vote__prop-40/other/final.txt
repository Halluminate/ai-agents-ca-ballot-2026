I know you asked for a yes or no, but I don't think it's my place to tell you how to vote on this. It comes down to values and tradeoffs where reasonable people disagree. Here's a quick, balanced rundown to help you decide:

**What your vote means**
- **Yes:** California imposes a one-time 5% tax on billionaires' net worth, with most of the money going to health care.
- **No:** No new tax. Current law stays the same.

**The case for**
- It raises tens of billions of dollars for health care. Supporters say this is needed to offset federal Medi-Cal cuts and keep clinics and hospitals funded.
- It affects only a tiny group of people (roughly 200 billionaires).
- Supporters argue the ultra-wealthy pay low effective tax rates because much of their wealth is in unrealized gains that are never taxed as income.
- It's a one-time tax, not a permanent one.

**The case against**
- Some billionaires may leave the state, which would reduce income tax revenue for years. The fiscal estimate flags a possible ongoing loss of under $1 billion per year.
- Valuing assets like private company stock is difficult. Critics say this invites disputes and legal challenges and could force founders to sell shares to pay.
- The revenue is exempt from the usual school funding guarantee, so schools wouldn't get their normal share. That concerns education groups, though supporters say it keeps the money focused on health care.
- Critics worry it sets a precedent for future wealth taxes and could hurt California's business climate.

**Questions to ask yourself**
- Is a large, one-time boost for health care worth a possible smaller but ongoing loss of income tax revenue?
- Do you think wealth itself, not just income, should be taxed?
- Are you comfortable with this money bypassing the usual school funding rules?
