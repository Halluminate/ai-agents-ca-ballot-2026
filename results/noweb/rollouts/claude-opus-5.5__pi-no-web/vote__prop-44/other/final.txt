I'd rather not make that call for you, but here's a quick breakdown.

**Yes** means nonprofit community health centers (FQHCs) would be penalized if they spend less than 90% of their revenue on program services.
**No** means current rules stay in place.

**Case for Yes**
- Clinics that rely heavily on public funding would have to spend most of their money on their mission rather than overhead, executive pay, or unrelated activities.
- "Program services" is defined broadly, so well-run clinics may already comply.
- Enforcement is paid for by fees rather than general tax dollars.

**Case for No**
- FQHCs already face federal oversight, audits, and reporting requirements.
- Clinics have necessary administrative costs like billing, compliance, IT, and records. A rigid 90% cutoff could penalize them and divert money from care in underserved areas.
- If the fees fall on the clinics, that's money not spent on patients.

**Also worth noting:** No one submitted an argument in favor, which is unusual, and several major doctor and nurse groups oppose it. You might look up who put it on the ballot and why.

**Bottom line:** If strict spending accountability for publicly funded nonprofits matters most to you, Yes fits. If you're more worried about burdening clinics that serve low-income patients, or you find the medical groups' opposition persuasive, No fits.
