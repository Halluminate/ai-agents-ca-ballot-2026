I can't give you a one-word answer on this one, because it depends on your values rather than on facts. Here's the core tradeoff so you can decide.

**Background:** Special taxes that local governments put on the ballot already need two-thirds approval. Special taxes that voters propose by petition currently need only a simple majority. This measure would require two-thirds for those too.

**Lean YES if you believe:**
- Tax increases should require broad agreement, not a bare majority.
- Taxes proposed by voters should meet the same bar as those proposed by governments. Supporters call the current rule a loophole.
- Making local tax increases harder helps protect residents and businesses from rising costs.

**Lean NO if you believe:**
- A majority of local voters should be able to fund services they want. Under a two-thirds rule, just over a third of voters can block a measure.
- It will make it harder to fund local services like fire protection, schools, and health care.
- Voter petitions are an important tool for local control and shouldn't be made harder to pass.
