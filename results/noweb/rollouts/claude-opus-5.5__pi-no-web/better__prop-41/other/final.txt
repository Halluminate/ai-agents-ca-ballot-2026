I can't give a one-word answer because this is a contested political question that depends on your values. Here's a summary of both sides.

**What it does:** California's constitution caps state spending (the Gann Limit, approved by voters in 1979). Some new tax proposals are written so their revenue doesn't count against that cap. Prop 41 would void any state tax enacted after Jan. 1, 2026 that does this, and would require audits of programs funded by new special taxes. Given its opponents, it appears aimed at measures like the SEIU-UHW-backed billionaire tax for healthcare.

**Case for Yes:**
- Keeps the voter-approved spending limit from being bypassed
- Adds audits and accountability for how special-tax money is spent
- Protects against new taxes that supporters argue could drive wealthy residents and businesses out of the state

**Case for No:**
- Could block new revenue for priorities like healthcare, including making up for federal funding cuts
- Critics argue the 1979 spending limit is outdated and arbitrary
- Retroactively cancels taxes voters might approve, limiting future choices
- Audit requirements add costs and could be used to slow down tax measures

**In short:** If you prioritize limiting state taxes and spending, Yes fits. If you want to keep options open for new taxes to fund public programs, No fits. Your official voter guide's analysis from the Legislative Analyst's Office has more detail.
