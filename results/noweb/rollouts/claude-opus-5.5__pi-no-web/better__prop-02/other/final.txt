I don't make voting recommendations, but here's a quick breakdown to help you decide:

**Case for yes**
- California's tax revenue swings sharply because it relies heavily on high earners and capital gains. A bigger reserve could help avoid cuts to schools and services, or tax hikes, during a recession.
- Both labor and business groups back it, and no formal opposition was filed.

**Case for no**
- Money held in reserve isn't available for current needs like housing, homelessness, or education.
- As a constitutional amendment, it's hard to change later, and withdrawals from the fund are restricted.

**Bottom line:** If you prioritize long-term budget stability, that points to yes. If you'd rather keep more money available to spend now, that points to no. The Legislative Analyst's Office analysis in your official voter guide is a good neutral source for details.
