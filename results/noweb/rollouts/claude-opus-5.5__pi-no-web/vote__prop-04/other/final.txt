I'd rather not pick for you, since this comes down to your own values, but here's a quick way to decide.

**What a Yes does:** It lifts California's 1988 ban on public campaign financing. It doesn't create any program by itself. The state, cities, or counties would each have to choose to set one up. Some charter cities, like LA and San Francisco, already have them. The listed cost covers only state oversight, and any future programs would have their own costs.

**Lean YES if you think:**
- Big donors and special interests have too much influence, and things like matching small donations can counter that.
- More ordinary people should be able to afford to run for office.
- Each community should get to decide this for itself.

**Lean NO if you think:**
- Tax dollars shouldn't fund political campaigns, including those of candidates you oppose.
- Even with the protections for education, transportation, and public safety, it takes money from other needs.
- It won't do much to curb big money, since outside spending like super PACs isn't affected.

If you usually agree with the groups on one side of the endorsement list, that's a reasonable tiebreaker.
