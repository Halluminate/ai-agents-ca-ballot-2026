I can't give a one-word answer, because this is a contested political question and the right vote depends on your values. Here's the core tradeoff:

**Vote yes if you think:**
- A replacement shouldn't take office with a small plurality. Under the current system, a replacement can win with fewer votes than the recalled official got to stay.
- Voters should decide on removal by itself, without a crowded replacement ballot alongside it. The 2003 recall had 135 replacement candidates, and the 2021 recall had 46.

**Vote no if you think:**
- Voters should pick the replacement directly in the same election, not through appointment or a later special election. Critics argue appointment could install someone voters didn't choose, possibly from the recalled official's own party.
- The current process is a fast, effective check on officials, and this change would weaken it.

No official arguments were submitted for or against this measure, so the Legislative Analyst's Office analysis in the official voter guide is a good neutral source for details.
