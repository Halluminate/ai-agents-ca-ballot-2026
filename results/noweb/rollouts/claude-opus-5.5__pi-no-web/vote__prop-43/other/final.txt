I won't pick yes or no for you, because this comes down to how you weigh competing values. Here's a quick breakdown to help you decide.

**What your vote means**
- **Yes:** Local special taxes placed on the ballot by voters through petitions would need two-thirds approval instead of a simple majority, starting January 1, 2027.
- **No:** Those measures would still pass with a simple majority.

**Background:** Special taxes (taxes dedicated to a specific purpose) proposed by local governments already need two-thirds approval under Props 13 and 218. Since a 2017 California Supreme Court decision and later appellate rulings, voter-proposed special taxes have been able to pass with a simple majority. This measure would end that difference.

**The case for Yes**
- It treats voter-proposed special taxes the same as government-proposed ones, closing what supporters call a court-created loophole.
- It requires broad agreement before raising taxes, which protects residents and businesses from increases passed by narrow margins, especially with high living costs.
- It stops well-funded interest groups from using signature drives to get around the stricter threshold.

**The case for No**
- It lets just over one-third of voters block measures most voters support, which undermines majority rule.
- It makes it harder for communities to fund services like fire protection, schools, health care, and libraries.
- Opponents say the courts correctly protected voters' initiative power, and that the two-thirds rule was meant to limit politicians, not citizens.

**Questions to ask yourself**
- Should a majority be enough to approve a dedicated tax, or should tax increases need a supermajority?
- Is the current majority threshold for voter-proposed taxes a legitimate path or a loophole?
- Think of past local tax measures in your area. Would you have wanted them to need two-thirds?

If you prioritize limiting tax increases and requiring broad consensus, you'll probably lean Yes. If you prioritize majority rule and local funding for services, you'll probably lean No. Your official voter guide also has the full arguments and rebuttals from each side.
