Deciding how to vote on Proposition 40 depends on your perspective on wealth taxation, healthcare funding, and the state budget. According to official ballot materials, campaign statements, and information provided by supporters such as U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders and opponents such as the California Taxpayers Association, voters are presented with contrasting arguments on the measure:

### Arguments for Voting "Yes"
Supporters, including U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders and SEIU United Healthcare Workers West, argue that:
* **Dedicated Healthcare Revenue:** According to SEIU United Healthcare Workers West, the one-time 5% tax would generate tens of billions of dollars spread over several years, providing vital resources to support and expand healthcare services.
* **Tax Fairness:** Proponents state that targeting individuals with more than $1 billion in assets ensures that ultra-wealthy residents contribute a greater share of their wealth toward public health and community needs, without raising taxes on middle- or lower-income residents.
* **Public Investment:** Supporters emphasize that the temporary revenue boost can address critical funding shortfalls in healthcare delivery and workforce support.

### Arguments for Voting "No"
Opponents, including the California Taxpayers Association, the California School Boards Association, and the California Primary Care Association, argue that:
* **Economic Competitiveness and Revenue Loss:** The California Taxpayers Association argues that a wealth tax creates strong incentives for billionaires to relocate outside of California. Official fiscal estimates note this could lead to an ongoing reduction of up to nearly $1 billion annually in income tax revenues.
* **Bypassing School Funding Protections:** The California School Boards Association expresses concern that exempting this revenue from constitutional school funding formulas (such as Proposition 98) and spending limits sets an undesirable precedent that circumvents standard protections for public education.
* **Budget and Healthcare Stability:** Opponents, including community healthcare advocates, caution that relying on temporary, volatile revenue sources creates administrative complexity and may disrupt long-term healthcare planning and funding stability.

A **Yes** vote supports levying a one-time 5% tax on individuals with assets exceeding $1 billion to primarily fund healthcare. 

A **No** vote opposes the wealth tax, keeping the current tax laws in place without generating the designated healthcare revenue.
