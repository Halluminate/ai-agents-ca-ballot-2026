Deciding whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 45 depends on how you weigh the goals of speeding up critical infrastructure projects against maintaining thorough environmental reviews and community oversight. 

According to official campaign arguments and voter information materials, here is an overview of the key arguments on both sides:

### Arguments for Voting "Yes"
Supporters of the measure—including the California Council for Affordable Housing, the California Water Association, and the California Children’s Hospital Association—argue that:
* **Addresses Urgent Infrastructure Needs:** California faces pressing shortages in housing, water reliability, modern healthcare facilities, and public transit. Setting enforceable review timelines helps build these essential projects more quickly.
* **Reduces Costly Delays and Litigation Abuse:** The California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) process is frequently used to delay or block beneficial projects through prolonged litigation. Establishing deadlines for environmental reviews and legal challenges reduces project uncertainty, lowers construction and legal costs, and prevents frivolous delays.
* **Maintains Targeted Protections:** Proponents contend the measure streamlines procedures for high-priority public interest sectors (such as housing, health, and water) without eliminating environmental review standards entirely.

### Arguments for Voting "No"
Opponents of the measure—including the Coalition for Clean Air, California Environmental Voters, Physicians for Social Responsibility–Los Angeles, and the National Wildlife Federation—argue that:
* **Weakens Environmental and Public Health Safeguards:** CEQA is a cornerstone of California's environmental laws, designed to identify and mitigate toxic emissions, water pollution, and habitat destruction. Rushing reviews could lead to unaddressed hazards that harm public health and local ecosystems.
* **Limits Community Oversight:** Strict deadlines and restrictions on legal challenges could curtail the ability of the public, particularly residents in disadvantaged or heavily industrialized communities, to voice concerns and challenge poorly planned projects.
* **Imposes Government Costs and Administrative Burden:** Tight deadlines on state and local agencies and the judicial system could result in significant administrative costs—estimated in the tens of millions to over $100 million annually—potentially straining public resources.

### Fiscal Impact
The official fiscal analysis notes that the measure would result in initial costs to state and local governments likely in the high tens of millions of dollars annually—potentially exceeding $100 million—to meet required deadlines, partly covered by fees. Longer-term fiscal effects could be positive or negative, depending on the speed of project completion and the degree of litigation impacts.
