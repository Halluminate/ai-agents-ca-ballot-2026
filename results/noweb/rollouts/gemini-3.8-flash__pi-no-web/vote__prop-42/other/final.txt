Deciding how to vote on Proposition 42 depends on your view of state tax policy, economic stability, and public funding. Because voting choices involve competing values and policy priorities, here is an overview of the arguments on both sides grounded in statements from the measure's official supporters and opponents.

### Arguments for a "Yes" Vote

Supporters of the initiative—including the **California Professional Firefighters**, the **State Building & Construction Trades Council of California**, and the **AMVETS Department of California**—argue that:

* **Preventing Capital and Job Loss:** Prohibiting new personal property taxes (which can include financial assets and forms of wealth taxation) helps keep high earners, businesses, and investment capital within the state, protecting jobs and long-term economic vitality.
* **Fairness and Certainty:** Banning retroactive taxes ensures that individuals and businesses are not penalized after the fact for financial decisions made under existing laws, creating greater legal and financial predictability.
* **Protecting Taxpayers:** Supporters maintain that state government should focus on managing existing revenues efficiently rather than creating new mechanisms to tax personal assets.

### Arguments for a "No" Vote

Opponents of the initiative—including **U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders** and **SEIU United Healthcare Workers West**—argue that:

* **Tax Fairness and Wealth Disparities:** Barring taxes on personal property permanently shields the wealthiest residents from potential wealth or high-asset taxes, limiting the state's ability to address income inequality.
* **Protecting Public Revenue and Services:** As noted in the measure's fiscal summary, limiting taxation options creates the possibility that future revenues will not grow as much, which opponents argue could constrain funding for critical public services like healthcare, education, and social safety nets.
* **Legislative Flexibility:** Opponents contend that locking tax restrictions into the state constitution overly restricts future lawmakers and voters from responding flexibly to budget deficits or economic crises.

---

### Summary of What Your Vote Means

* **A "Yes" vote** supports amending the state constitution to prohibit any new state personal property taxes and certain retroactive taxes, nullifying conflicting taxes enacted after January 1, 2026.
* **A "No" vote** opposes the constitutional amendment, maintaining the state's existing authority to enact personal property or retroactive taxes through the normal legislative or initiative process.
