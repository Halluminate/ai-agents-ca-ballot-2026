Deciding whether to vote "Yes" or "No" on Proposition 42 depends on your perspective regarding state tax policy, government revenue, and economic competitiveness. Voters and organizations hold differing viewpoints on the measure.

### Arguments for a "Yes" Vote

Supporters of the measure, including the California Professional Firefighters, the State Building & Construction Trades Council of California, and the AMVETS Department of California, argue that:

* **Protection from New Tax Burdens:** Passing the initiative prevents the implementation of new personal property taxes, including proposed wealth taxes on personal assets such as investments, business ownership, and personal belongings.
* **Fairness and Predictability:** Supporters contend that prohibiting retroactive taxes ensures fairness by preventing the state from taxing financial decisions made before legislation was enacted, offering greater financial certainty for individuals and small businesses.
* **Preventing Capital Flight:** Proponents argue that avoiding novel taxes on personal wealth encourages entrepreneurs, businesses, and high-income residents to remain in California, protecting the broader economy and existing tax revenues.

### Arguments for a "No" Vote

Opponents of the measure, including U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders and SEIU United Healthcare Workers West, argue that:

* **Limiting Public Revenue:** Opponents state that placing constitutional restrictions on taxing personal property and wealth hampers California’s ability to generate revenue from the ultra-wealthy to support critical public services, such as healthcare, public education, and infrastructure.
* **Restricting Legislative Flexibility:** Critics argue that constitutional prohibitions restrict future lawmakers from responding effectively to economic crises, budget deficits, or emerging fiscal challenges.
* **Protecting the Wealthiest Residents:** Opponents argue the measure primarily protects billionaires and multi-millionaires from paying higher taxes on vast non-real-estate assets, shifting the tax burden disproportionately onto working-class and middle-class residents.
