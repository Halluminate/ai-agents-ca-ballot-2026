Whether to vote "Yes" or "No" on Proposition 43 depends on differing views regarding taxation, voter thresholds, and the funding of local public services. 

Supporters, including the California Taxpayers Association, the Family Business Association of California, and the California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce, advocate for a **"Yes"** vote, arguing that:
* **Taxpayer Protections:** Requiring a two-thirds supermajority ensures broad community consensus before new taxes are implemented, helping protect homeowners, small businesses, and consumers from higher costs of living.
* **Consistency in Election Standards:** A two-thirds requirement aligns citizen-initiated special taxes with the same threshold currently required for special tax measures placed on the ballot by local elected officials, closing what supporters view as a legal loophole.
* **Fiscal Accountability:** Raising the threshold encourages local governments and advocacy groups to prioritize spending within existing budgets rather than relying on tax increases.

Opponents, including the California Professional Firefighters, the California Federation of Teachers, and the Nurse Alliance of SEIU California, advocate for a **"No"** vote, arguing that:
* **Funding Essential Services:** A two-thirds supermajority makes it much more difficult for local communities to raise revenues needed for critical public services, such as emergency medical response, fire protection, public schools, and healthcare facilities.
* **Majority Rule:** The higher threshold allows a minority—just over one-third of voters—to block measures that a clear majority of the community supports, limiting the power of local voters to direct local policy.
* **Local Self-Determination:** Retaining a simple majority vote gives communities the flexibility to address urgent local needs and infrastructure challenges through citizen-led ballot initiatives without undue procedural hurdles.
