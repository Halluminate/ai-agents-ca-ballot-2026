Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 2 depends on your perspective on state budgeting and fiscal reserves. Voters and policy analysts weigh different priorities when considering this measure:

### Arguments for Voting "Yes"
According to the official ballot materials and supporters—including the California Professional Firefighters, the Los Angeles Area Chamber of Commerce, Governor Gavin Newsom, Xavier Becerra, and Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel:
* **Fiscal Stability:** Increasing the Rainy Day Fund helps cushion the state budget against volatile revenue swings and economic downturns.
* **Protecting Essential Services:** Having larger reserves ensures that funding for critical public programs—such as K-12 and higher education, health care, and public safety—can be sustained without sudden, deep cuts during recessions.
* **Avoiding Abrupt Tax Hikes:** Maintaining robust savings reduces the likelihood that the state will need to implement emergency tax increases to balance the budget when revenues drop.

### Arguments for Voting "No"
While no formal argument against the measure was submitted to the official voter information guide, common critiques and counterarguments regarding expanding government reserve funds include:
* **Opportunity Costs:** Directing higher portions of state revenues into reserves means less money is immediately available to address pressing, ongoing needs, such as affordable housing, infrastructure repairs, or expanding current social programs.
* **Taxpayer Relief Concerns:** Some fiscal conservatives and taxpayer advocacy groups argue that surplus revenues beyond basic reserve needs should be returned to taxpayers through rebates or tax relief rather than held indefinitely by the government.
* **Budget Flexibility:** Setting higher mandatory reserve thresholds can restrict the Legislature’s discretion in allocating funds during regular budget cycles.
