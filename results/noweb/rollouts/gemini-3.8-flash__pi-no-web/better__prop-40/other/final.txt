Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 40 depends on a voter's perspective on wealth taxes, healthcare priorities, and state funding rules. Supporters and opponents offer contrasting views on the measure's potential benefits and drawbacks.

### Arguments for Voting "Yes"
According to supporters, including U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders and the SEIU United Healthcare Workers West, a "yes" vote is supported by arguments that:

* **Funds Critical Healthcare Services:** The measure generates tens of billions of dollars dedicated primarily to expanding and strengthening healthcare access, services, and staffing across California.
* **Addresses Wealth Inequality:** Imposing a one-time 5% tax on individuals with more than $1 billion in assets ensures that the state's wealthiest residents contribute to public needs without raising taxes on middle- or lower-income residents.
* **Provides a Substantial Revenue Boost:** Supporter materials argue that a one-time infusion of tens of billions of dollars can make lasting capital investments and improvements in community health infrastructure.

### Arguments for Voting "No"
According to opponents, including the California School Boards Association, the California Primary Care Association, and the California Taxpayers Association, a "no" vote is supported by arguments that:

* **Bypasses Public Education Guarantees:** By exempting the new tax revenues from constitutional school funding requirements (such as Proposition 98), opponents argue the measure undermines standard constitutional protections designed to support K–14 education.
* **Risks Broader Tax Revenue Losses:** The state's fiscal analysis notes a potential ongoing loss of up to several hundred million dollars annually in income tax revenue if wealthy taxpayers relocate or alter their investments to avoid California taxation.
* **Concerns Over Tax Policy and Fairness:** Business and taxpayer advocacy groups argue that targeted wealth taxes can create economic uncertainty, discourage investment in the state, and set a concerning precedent for future selective taxation.

Ultimately, voters who prioritize raising dedicated funding for healthcare from the highest-wealth residents generally favor the initiative, while voters concerned about exemptions from school funding guarantees, state economic competitiveness, or wealth taxes generally oppose it.
