Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on this measure depends on your views regarding state tax policy, government spending, and public education funding. 

**Supporters**, including the California Teachers Association, the California State PTA, the CA School Nurses Organization, and Planned Parenthood Affiliates of California, argue that:
* **Reliable Revenue for Public Services:** Making the tax rates permanent secures an estimated $5 billion to $15 billion annually in ongoing funding for K–12 schools, community colleges, and healthcare programs.
* **Progressive Tax Structure:** The tax applies only to top earners making over $371,000, ensuring that higher-income residents contribute more toward public infrastructure without raising taxes on middle- and lower-income households.
* **Preventing Budget Cuts:** Letting the current tax provisions expire could force significant reductions in school staffing, programs, and student support services.

**Opponents**, including the California Taxpayers Association, the Family Business Association of California, and the California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce, argue that:
* **Permanence of "Temporary" Taxes:** Voters initially approved these higher rates as temporary measures, and opponents contend that extending them indefinitely goes against the original commitment to let them sunset.
* **Economic Competitiveness:** California already maintains some of the highest personal income tax rates in the nation, which critics argue harms business growth, reduces economic competitiveness, and could incentivize high earners and businesses to relocate to lower-tax states.
* **Revenue Volatility and Spending:** High-income tax revenues can fluctuate widely with economic cycles, and opponents argue that the state should prioritize spending discipline and budgetary reform rather than relying on continued high tax rates.
