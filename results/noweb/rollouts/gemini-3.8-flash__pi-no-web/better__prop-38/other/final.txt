Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 38 depends on how voters weigh the potential benefits of dedicated state funding for biomedical research against the long-term fiscal obligations of state borrowing. 

Supporters—including advocacy organizations such as the Michael J. Fox Foundation, Alzheimer’s Los Angeles, the Kidney Cancer Association, and the American Nurses Association\California—argue that the measure provides vital resources for lifesaving medical innovation. In contrast, opponents, including health policy researcher Dr. Robert M. Kaplan, argue that using billions in general obligation bonds for specialized research is a risky and costly use of state taxpayer funds.

### Arguments for Voting "Yes"
* **Advancing Medical Treatments:** Proponents contend that dedicated funding for immunology and immunotherapy could accelerate breakthroughs and therapies for debilitating conditions such as cancer, Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, and autoimmune disorders.
* **Long-Term Economic and Health Returns:** Supporters point out that successful research could generate revenue through patent royalties and commercialization, helping to offset bond repayment costs while stimulating California's biotechnology economy and potentially reducing long-term healthcare expenses.
* **Stable Research Investment:** Backers argue that a multi-year bond guarantees consistent, sustained funding for scientific discovery and grant programs that might otherwise face uncertainty under fluctuating annual state budget cycles or federal funding limits.

### Arguments for Voting "No"
* **State Debt and Budget Impact:** Opponents emphasize the measure's fiscal impact, noting that repaying $8.4 billion in general obligation bonds would cost California's General Fund an estimated $500 million to $600 million annually for approximately 20 years, potentially crowding out spending on other essential public services like education, infrastructure, and direct public healthcare.
* **Concentrated Allocation:** Critics question the measure's structure, which directs half of the total bond proceeds to a single University of California-affiliated nonprofit medical research institute, raising concerns about competitive distribution of research funds and oversight.
* **Uncertain Financial Offsets:** Opponents highlight that scientific research carries inherent risk with no guarantee of commercial success or financial return, leaving taxpayers responsible for the full cost of debt service regardless of research outcomes.
