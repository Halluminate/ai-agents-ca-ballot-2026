Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 45 depends on how voters balance the need to accelerate housing and infrastructure development against the desire to maintain comprehensive environmental review and public oversight. Both supporters and opponents offer distinct perspectives grounded in differing policy priorities.

### Arguments for Voting "Yes" (Supporters)

Supporters of the measure—including the California Council for Affordable Housing, the California Water Association, and the California Children’s Hospital Association—argue that the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) is frequently weaponized through protracted litigation to delay or block essential projects. 

Key points raised by supporters include:
* **Addressing Urgent Crises:** Proponents contend that California faces severe shortages in affordable housing, healthcare facilities, and modern water and transit infrastructure. Imposing strict timelines on environmental reviews and court challenges helps ensure these critical projects are built faster and more affordably.
* **Curtailing Abuse and Reducing Costs:** Supporters argue that lengthy and unpredictable review timelines lead to escalating construction costs and bureaucratic delays, often driven by legal challenges unrelated to genuine environmental concerns.
* **Predictability for Key Sectors:** By setting clear deadlines for agencies to complete reviews and for courts to resolve disputes, supporters assert the measure provides much-needed regulatory certainty without eliminating environmental oversight entirely.

### Arguments for Voting "No" (Opponents)

Opponents of the measure—including the Coalition for Clean Air, California Environmental Voters, Physicians for Social Responsibility–Los Angeles, and the National Wildlife Federation—argue that CEQA is California's cornerstone environmental and public health law, and that truncating the review process weakens vital community protections.

Key points raised by opponents include:
* **Protecting Public Health and the Environment:** Opponents maintain that thorough environmental reviews are necessary to identify and mitigate harms such as toxic air emissions, water pollution, and habitat destruction before projects proceed.
* **Limiting Community Input and Justice:** Critics contend that shortening deadlines for public review and legal challenges disproportionately impacts disadvantaged and lower-income communities, reducing their ability to identify environmental risks and advocate for necessary safeguards.
* **Fiscal and Administrative Pressures:** Opponents and fiscal analyses note that imposing expedited deadlines could place significant financial and staffing burdens on state and local agencies—potentially costing tens of millions of dollars annually—which could lead to rushed reviews or administrative strain.

### Summary

* A **"Yes"** vote favors setting deadlines on environmental reviews and lawsuits to speed up approvals and construction for housing, healthcare, transit, and water projects.
* A **"No"** vote favors keeping the existing CEQA process and legal timelines in place to preserve full public oversight, community review, and environmental and health evaluations.
