Whether to vote "Yes" or "No" on this proposition depends on your views regarding state fiscal policy, government savings, and spending priorities. A balanced look at the arguments surrounding the measure includes considerations from supporters and general fiscal policy perspectives:

### Arguments for Voting "Yes"
According to official ballot statements and supporters—including Governor Gavin Newsom, Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, the California Professional Firefighters, and the Los Angeles Area Chamber of Commerce:
* **Protection During Economic Downturns:** Proponents argue that California’s budget relies heavily on volatile revenue sources, such as capital gains taxes from high earners. Increasing the size of the Rainy Day Fund helps shield essential services—including public education, health care, and emergency response—from severe cuts when revenues drop.
* **Fiscal Responsibility and Stability:** Supporters contend that setting aside surplus revenue during strong economic years prevents unsustainable spending spikes and helps avoid emergency tax increases during recessions.
* **Credit and Borrowing Benefits:** Larger reserves can improve the state’s creditworthiness, which can lower borrowing costs for public infrastructure projects.

### Arguments for Voting "No"
While no formal opposing argument was submitted to the ballot pamphlet, fiscal policy analysts, taxpayer advocates, and budget critics often raise several concerns regarding expanding reserve limits:
* **Opportunity Cost of Idled Funds:** Critics of higher reserve caps argue that surplus revenues placed in long-term savings are unavailable for immediate, high-priority public needs, such as addressing homelessness, repairing aging infrastructure, or funding local services today.
* **Tax Relief Considerations:** Some taxpayer groups argue that when state revenues exceed operational needs, excess funds should be returned to taxpayers through rebates or tax reductions rather than absorbed into government reserves.
* **Legislative Flexibility:** Opponents of constitutional reserve mandates sometimes argue that rigid deposit requirements limit the Legislature's discretion to respond dynamically to changing economic conditions and immediate community needs.

---

### Summary of What Your Vote Means
* **A "Yes" vote** supports increasing the cap or deposit requirements of California's Rainy Day Fund, allowing the state to hold larger budget reserves to buffer against future economic downturns.
* **A "No" vote** opposes the change, leaving the Rainy Day Fund rules and reserve limits established under the 2014 constitutional amendment in place.
