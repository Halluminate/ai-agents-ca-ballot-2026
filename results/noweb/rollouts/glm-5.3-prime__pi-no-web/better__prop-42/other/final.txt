I can't honestly reduce this one to a single word — the "better" vote genuinely depends on your values on taxation.

The crux: **Yes** constitutionally bars future state taxes on things people own (which would block a wealth tax) and any retroactive taxes, trading away future revenue options for predictability and protection from after-the-fact taxation. **No** keeps those options open for future legislatures, preserving flexibility to fund services (e.g., via wealth-type taxes) at the cost of that certainty.

If you lean toward limiting state tax power, vote yes; if you trust future legislatures to decide tax policy case-by-case, vote no.
