I can't give you a yes or no on this — how to vote on a contested ballot measure is your call, not mine. But here's a quick rundown of both sides to help you decide:

**Supporters' case:**
- Requiring ID is intended to prevent voter fraud and strengthen public confidence in election results
- Verifying mail ballots adds a safeguard to a channel they see as more vulnerable to abuse

**Opponents' case:**
- Studies find in-person voter fraud is extremely rare, so the security benefit is minimal
- Millions of eligible voters lack government ID (disproportionately elderly, young, low-income, and minority voters), which could suppress legitimate turnout
- Requiring ID numbers on mail ballot envelopes could lead to valid ballots being rejected over small errors
- Estimated cost of tens to hundreds of millions of dollars per year

For a deeper look, check your state's official voter information guide or a nonpartisan source like Ballotpedia — they'll lay out the full arguments and fiscal analysis without telling you which way to go.
