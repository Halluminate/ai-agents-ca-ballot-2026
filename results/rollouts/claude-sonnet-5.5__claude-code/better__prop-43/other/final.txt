I'm not going to give a bare yes or no on this one. I read the official guide, the Legislative Analyst's analysis, and coverage and arguments from both sides. What's left is a values trade-off, not a fact I can settle. The state Democratic and Republican parties are on opposite sides, so I don't think I should steer your vote.

**What Prop 43 does:**
- **The change:** Starting Jan. 1, 2027, a new, increased or extended local special tax (earmarked for a specific purpose) proposed by citizen initiative would need two-thirds approval instead of a simple majority. Special taxes proposed by local governments already need two-thirds.
- **Not retroactive:** Existing taxes, such as L.A.'s Measure ULA, stay in place.
- **Origin:** It came out of a deal in which the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association withdrew a broader initiative.
- **Property-tax detail:** Some summaries add that it also bars new local property-value-based tax initiatives. I couldn't open the amendment's full text to confirm that.

**Fiscal effect (Legislative Analyst):** Local tax revenue "could be lower in the future than they otherwise would be." The actual impact is unknown.

**How to decide:**
- **Yes** fits if you think earmarked taxes should need broad consensus no matter who puts them on the ballot. Supporters say the initiative route lets organized interests pass their own taxes by a simple majority, especially in low-turnout elections. Backers include the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, CalChamber, CalTax and the California Republican Party.
- **No** fits if you think a majority should be able to approve local funding for things like fire protection, schools, transit and housing. Opponents say two-thirds lets about a third of voters block what most want. They include the California Professional Firefighters, the California Federation of Teachers, SEIU's Nurse Alliance and the Democratic Party of California.
