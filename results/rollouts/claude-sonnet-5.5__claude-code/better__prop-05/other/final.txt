I'm not going to give you a bare yes or no. I read the official guide and analysis, CalMatters, SPUR, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association's guide, and the editorial positions. The mechanics of the measure aren't really disputed. The disagreement is over a values tradeoff, and a one-word answer from me would put my values in place of yours.

**What Prop 5 does:** Recall ballots would ask only whether to remove the official, with no replacement question. The vacancy would then be filled like other vacancies. The [official analysis](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/5/analysis.htm) says the net fiscal effect is unknown, possibly millions saved or spent.
- **Governor:** The lieutenant governor takes over. If the recall succeeds early in the term, a special election is held with the next statewide primary.
- **Other statewide offices:** The governor appoints a replacement.
- **Legislators:** A special election is held about four months later ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/10/california-election-proposition-5-explainer/)).
- **The recalled official:** They could run in a special election but could not be appointed.

**A yes vote fits if** you think a recall should decide only whether to remove someone. It also fits if you want to avoid a crowded replacement field where a plurality winner takes office. In 2021, the top replacement candidate drew about 28% of all voters. Supporters include the League of Women Voters, Common Cause, the state Democratic Party, and the Sacramento Bee.

**A no vote fits if** you think voters who remove an official should also choose the successor on the same ballot. It also fits if you'd rather not leave that to succession rules, appointees, or a vacancy, or have a recalled official able to run again. Opponents include the state Republican Party, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, and the SF Chronicle.
