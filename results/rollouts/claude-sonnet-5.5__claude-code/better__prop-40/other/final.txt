I'm not going to give a one-word answer here. I read the voter guide, the Legislative Analyst's (LAO) analysis, legal and economic critiques from both sides, and the polling. Whether to vote yes or no comes down to how you weigh values and risk, and informed experts split on it.

**What it does:** It's a one-time 5% tax on the net worth of roughly 200 billionaires who were California residents on Jan. 1, 2026. 90% of the money goes to health care. The revenue is exempt from Prop 98 (the school-funding guarantee) and the state spending limit.

**What the evidence shows**
- **Revenue:** The LAO expects tens of billions of dollars in temporary revenue. It also expects a possible income-tax loss of under $1 billion a year. It gives no net figure.
- **Outside estimates:** These diverge. Sponsors say about $100 billion. Hoover/Stanford economists say $30–40 billion up front but a net loss of $25–39 billion. Each side disputes the other's assumptions.
- **Legal risk:** Expect litigation. The drafters and critics disagree on whether the retroactive residency date and worldwide-asset rules would survive.
- **Legislature's role:** Lawmakers get spending discretion. They can amend the tax by a two-thirds vote if the change "furthers the purposes." Legal experts disagree on how far that goes.
- **Props 41 and 42:** Prop 40 is nullified if either of them gets more yes votes than Prop 40 does.
- **Who's where:** Backers include SEIU-UHW (the sponsoring union), Bernie Sanders, the state Democratic Party, the Labor Federation and six Nobel economists. Opponents include Gov. Newsom, the California Teachers Association, the California Medical Association, hospital and clinic groups, Planned Parenthood affiliates, firefighters, the building trades and most major editorial boards.
- **Polls:** Late-October polls had it at 42–46% yes.

**How to decide**
- **Lean yes** if taxing extreme wealth to offset federal health cuts is a priority for you. You'd also need to find the LAO's modest income-tax-loss figure and the drafters' legal defense persuasive.
- **Lean no** if you give more weight to legal fragility, one-time money for ongoing costs, spending discretion, the Prop 98 carve-out, and the risk of billionaires leaving.

If you tell me which of those trade-offs matters most to you, I'll tell you which vote fits.
