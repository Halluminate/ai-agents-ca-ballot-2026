I'm holding back on a bare yes or no. I read the official guide, the LAO analysis, the measure's text, and analyses from both sides. The research doesn't settle this one, because it comes down to a value judgment about wealth taxes and constitutional limits on future taxes. I'd rather not make that call for you.

**What your vote does**
- **Yes:** Writes a ban into the state constitution on new state taxes on owning personal property, such as stocks, retirement accounts and business interests. It also bans most retroactive taxes. Existing taxes are grandfathered, and only another constitutional amendment could undo it. If it gets more Yes votes than Prop 40 (the one-time billionaire tax), courts could void Prop 40 even if Prop 40 also passes.
- **No:** The state keeps the option to create such taxes.

**A quick way to decide**
- Vote **no** if you want Prop 40 to be able to take effect, or want future wealth or asset taxes to stay possible.
- Vote **yes** if you oppose Prop 40 or wealth taxes generally, or want a permanent constitutional guarantee against asset taxes and retroactive taxes.

**Contested points**
- **Revenue:** The LAO says it "could reduce future tax revenues," but the timing and size are unclear. A Hoover Institution analysis expects little direct fiscal change, since California doesn't currently tax such property.
- **Retirement savings:** Supporters say it protects retirement savings from double taxation. CalMatters and the SF Chronicle report that no active proposal targets those accounts, and Prop 40 largely exempts them, except Roth IRAs over $10M. Supporters say the point is to close what they call a constitutional loophole before such a tax is proposed.
- **Drafting:** Some tax-law professors say it is broadly drafted and could constrain things like vehicle license fee reform. They back Prop 40, so they aren't neutral.
- **Money and endorsements:** About $159M has been raised for Yes, mostly from Sergey Brin, against about $34.5M for No, mostly from SEIU-UHW. Newsom, CalTax and the CA Republican Party support it. The CA Democratic Party, SEIU-UHW and Bernie Sanders oppose it.
