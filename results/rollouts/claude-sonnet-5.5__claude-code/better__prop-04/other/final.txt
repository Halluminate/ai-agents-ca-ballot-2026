I'm not going to give a bare yes or no on this one. Prop 4 turns on a values tradeoff: whether tax money should ever fund campaigns, and whether you trust officials to write the rules. A one-word answer from me wouldn't give you anything you could check. Here is what I found.

**What it does**
- It repeals the 1988 ban, so the state and local governments *may* create public campaign financing programs. It creates no program and spends no money itself.
- It bars funds earmarked for education, transportation or public safety. It also bars paying legal fees, fines or personal-loan repayments. Candidates must show broad-based support and accept spending limits.
- Supporters say it also triples maximum fines for illegal foreign campaign money.
- The Legislative Analyst estimates state costs of a few hundred thousand dollars a year, for the Fair Political Practices Commission (FPPC). Local costs "could be significant" for governments that opt in.

**Strongest case for yes**
- Only a few charter cities (LA, SF, Oakland, Berkeley, Long Beach) can run programs now. This lets other governments choose.
- Research on Seattle's voucher program and on Arizona and Maine found more donors, more candidates and more competition. Backers include the League of Women Voters, Common Cause, ACLU California Action, the Democratic Party and the Sacramento Bee.

**Strongest case for no**
- Caps on candidates and dollars, the "strict criteria" for qualifying, and funding sources are left to future officials. The FPPC isn't required to enforce local programs.
- It doesn't limit private contributions or independent spending, and the evidence that public financing reduces big-money influence is mixed. Taxpayers could fund campaigns they oppose.
- Opponents include CalTax, Howard Jarvis, the SF Chronicle, the San Diego Union-Tribune and the Mercury News. Voters rejected similar measures in 2000 and 2010, and the October PPIC poll had Prop 4 behind, 45% to 52%.

**Quick way to choose**
- **Yes** if you want communities to have the option and see private money as the bigger problem.
- **No** if you object to tax dollars funding campaigns on principle, or want firmer rules and more evidence first.

If you tell me which of those matters more to you, I'll give you the yes or no that matches.
