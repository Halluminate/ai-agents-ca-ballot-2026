I'm not going to give you a one-word yes or no. I did the research, and it didn't turn up a fact that settles the question. It showed a real tradeoff between two values. On a contested ballot measure, I think the useful thing is to lay that tradeoff out and leave the vote to you.

**What Prop 5 does**
- **Today:** A state recall asks two questions: should the official be removed, and who should replace them? The top vote-getter wins the second question, and no majority is needed. Prop 5 deletes the second question.
- **Filling the seat:** The vacancy is filled the usual way. Legislative seats go to a special election, and the governor appoints replacements for most other statewide offices.
- **If a governor is recalled:** The Lieutenant Governor takes over. If the recall comes early in the term, they serve until voters elect a new governor at a future statewide election. If it comes later, they serve out the term.
- **Recalled officials:** They can't be appointed to the vacancy, but they could run in a special election.
- **Cost:** The Legislative Analyst says the net fiscal effect is unknown. It could mean savings or costs of millions of dollars per election, under 0.1% of the General Fund.

**The choice is between** a replacement who wins with a minority of votes, and a successor you didn't get to pick.
- **Lean yes if** you think a recall should only decide whether someone is removed. Supporters point to 2003. Gray Davis was removed with 55.4%, but Arnold Schwarzenegger won with about 48.6% in a 135-candidate field. They also argue the current system invites strategic recalls. Backers include:
  - the California Democratic Party
  - the League of Women Voters
  - Common Cause
  - Dan Schnur
  - Secretary of State Weber
  - SPUR
  - the McClatchy/Sacramento Bee editorial boards
- **Lean no if** you think voters who remove someone should also choose the replacement, rather than the Lieutenant Governor, an appointee or a later election. Opponents also cite the cost of extra special elections and legislative seats sitting vacant. Opponents include:
  - the California Republican Party
  - Republican legislative leaders
  - Election Integrity Project California
  - the SF Chronicle
  - the San Diego Union-Tribune and OC Register (per CalMatters)

**Worth knowing**
- **Chronicle's objection:** The Chronicle's "no" doesn't defend the status quo. It calls the second question problematic. Its objection is the provision letting a recalled official run again, which the current Constitution bars.
- **Ballot title:** The title says "statewide officers," but legislative recalls are covered too. The author acknowledged it may confuse voters.
- **Signatures:** Prop 5 doesn't change the signature requirements for starting a recall.
- **Polls and money:** PPIC's likely-voter polls had it trailing, 35–59 in September and 45–52 in October. As of early October, no committee had reported raising money on either side.
