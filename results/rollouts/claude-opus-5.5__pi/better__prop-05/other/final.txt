I won't pick yes or no for you on this one. It's a contested ballot measure that comes down to what you value, and credible groups on both sides disagree. Here's what decides it.

**What Prop 5 does:** It removes the "who should replace them?" question from state recall ballots.
- **Governor:** the lieutenant governor takes over. If the recall happens early in the term, voters later pick a new governor, and the winner needs a majority.
- **Other statewide officers:** the governor appoints a replacement, subject to legislative confirmation.
- **Legislators:** a special election months later. The recalled official could run in it, which is barred today.

**Lean yes if** your main worry is someone winning a recall with a small share of the vote, or recalls being used to redo elections a party lost.
- In 2018, Josh Newman's replacement got about 50,000 votes, fewer than the roughly 66,000 cast to keep him.
- In 2021, Larry Elder would have become governor with about 28% of the people who voted in the recall, had it passed.
- Backed by the League of Women Voters, Common Cause, the state Democratic Party and the Sacramento Bee.

**Lean no if** you want voters, not the governor or lieutenant governor, choosing who comes next.
- Other drawbacks: legislative seats would sit empty for months, extra special elections would cost money, and a recalled official could run again. The measure's author told the SF Chronicle he couldn't explain why that last rule is there.
- The state's independent oversight agency, the Little Hoover Commission, agreed the current system is flawed. But it warned against this kind of succession setup and recommended a different fix.
- Opposed by the state Republican Party, the SF Chronicle, Mercury News, OC Register and San Diego Union-Tribune.

**Gut check:** What bothers you more: someone taking office with about 30% of the vote, or voters losing their direct choice of a replacement? Your answer to that is probably your vote.
