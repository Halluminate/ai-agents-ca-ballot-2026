**no**

I recommend a No vote on Prop 41. I'm not saying a No vote supports the billionaire tax (Prop 40):

- **What it's really for:** Prop 41 was paid for mainly by Sergey Brin's committee to cancel Prop 40. If 41 gets more yes votes, 40 is voided even if it passes. If you oppose the billionaire tax, voting No on 40 already does that job without a permanent change to the state constitution.
- **The spending-cap rule:** It permanently writes into the constitution that no new state tax since Jan. 1, 2026 can sit outside the 1979 state spending limit (the Gann limit). If a tax does, the state can't collect it at all. The state already hit that limit in 2022. When it's at the limit, money from a tax voters approved for one purpose might not be spendable on that purpose.
- **Possible side effects:** Legal analysts and the California Budget & Policy Center warn it could endanger Medi-Cal provider taxes and fees that bring in federal matching money. Prop 40's drafters say it could even threaten Prop 3 on this ballot. These are legal risks, not certainties, and the Legislative Analyst didn't flag the Prop 3 conflict.
- **The audits:** They largely duplicate what the State Auditor and Legislative Analyst already do. They must recommend 10% savings for every program whether or not waste exists. Taxpayers pay for audits of initiatives that never make the ballot. Even GrowSF, which endorses Yes, calls the audit part "weak machinery."
- **Who opposes it:** Opposition crosses the usual lines. The Mercury News and Bakersfield Californian oppose Prop 40 and still say No on 41. The SF Chronicle, Sacramento Bee, League of Women Voters and SPUR also say No.

The case for Yes is mainly fiscal discipline. If you strongly believe every new tax should count under the spending cap, with no exceptions, Yes fits that view. That's the position of the Chamber of Commerce, CalTax, Howard Jarvis, the state Republican Party, and the Orange County Register and San Diego Union-Tribune editorial boards. In the late-October PPIC poll, likely voters were split 48% yes to 49% no.
