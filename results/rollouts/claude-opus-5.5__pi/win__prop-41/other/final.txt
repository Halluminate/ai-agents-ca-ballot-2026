I couldn't find any results yet. Sources as recent as Nov. 2–3, 2026 still show zero votes counted, so this is a forecast, not a reported outcome.

- **Polls are below 50% and falling.** The final PPIC poll (Oct. 16–22) had Prop 41 at 48% yes, 49% no, down from 51–44 in September. Berkeley IGS (Oct. 19–25) had 38% yes, 47% no, 16% undecided, down from 37–33 in September. IGS poll director Mark DiCamillo called below 50% and trending down "ominous signs," since undecided voters tend to vote no.
- **The backers stopped pushing it.** The Yes on 41 committee raised no new money after Sept. 20 and had about $22,000 left on Oct. 17. Building a Better California, the Brin-led anti-wealth-tax group behind Prop 41, put its late money into No on 40 and Yes on 42. The New York Times reported that Prop 41 had been "de-prioritized."
- **Endorsements cut against it.** The California Democratic Party, the state labor federation, SEIU, the ACLU and several major papers (Sacramento Bee, SF Chronicle, Mercury News) oppose it. Newsom endorsed Prop 42 but took no position on 41. Only about three in ten Democrats back it in PPIC's poll.
- **Turnout favors the No side.** Democrats are returning ballots at high rates, with turnout up among young and Latino voters, groups that oppose Prop 41.
- **Prediction markets lean no.** Kalshi had Prop 41 at about 43% to pass. Polymarket was higher, but it's very lightly traded.

My rough estimate is a 20–30% chance that Prop 41 passes.
