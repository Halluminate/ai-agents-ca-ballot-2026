I'm not going to give you a one-word answer on this one. After researching it, I don't think there's an objectively "better" vote. It comes down to how you weigh majority rule against making taxes harder to raise, and that's your call. Here's the short version.

**What Prop 43 actually does**
- Starting in 2027, a local tax earmarked for a specific purpose (a "special tax") would need two-thirds of the vote if citizens put it on the ballot by petition. Today it needs a simple majority. Special taxes put on the ballot by cities and counties already need two-thirds.
- It doesn't repeal any existing tax. Renewing an existing citizen-passed special tax would need two-thirds, though. General taxes, which aren't earmarked, still pass with a simple majority.
- Both campaigns overstate things. The Yes side suggests Prop 43 adds new rules on how the money gets spent, but special taxes are already legally limited to their stated purpose. The No side's warnings about fire and police funding only apply to earmarked taxes proposed by citizens.
- Lawmakers voted for it almost unanimously as part of a deal to get a broader Howard Jarvis initiative off the ballot. Even its author, Assemblymember Buffy Wicks, is urging a No vote.

**Lean YES if you think:**
- The same two-thirds bar should apply no matter who puts a special tax on the ballot.
- Interest groups use petition drives to get around Prop 13's two-thirds rule.
- Earmarked tax increases should need broad agreement.

**Lean NO if you think:**
- A local majority should be able to tax itself for things like fire protection, transit or housing.
- It's wrong that about a third of voters could block a tax most people support. Taxes that passed with 55–65% of the vote, like LA's "mansion tax" and Sonoma County's fire tax, would have failed under this rule.

**Who's on each side**
- **Yes:** Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, CalTax, CalChamber, the California Republican Party, and the editorial boards of the Mercury News, San Diego Union-Tribune and OC Register.
- **No:** Gov. Newsom, the California Democratic Party, unions for firefighters, teachers and nurses, the League of California Cities, the League of Women Voters, SPUR, and the Sacramento Bee editorial board.

If you tell me which of those priorities matters most to you, I'll tell you which vote matches it.
