**no**

I expect Prop 45 to fail. It's close, but No has the edge. The election hadn't happened yet when I checked on Nov 2–3, so this is a forecast, not a result.

- **Polls:** The last public poll, from PPIC on Oct 16–22, had 46% yes and 50% no. Support has fallen from 73% in July, when voters got only a general description, to 49% in September once they read the ballot wording. A no-leading measure that is still losing ground this late usually fails, and undecided voters tend to vote no. An August poll by FM3 had 49% yes, falling to 38% after voters heard arguments for and against.
- **Endorsements:** The state Democratic Party opposes it, along with the main labor federation, the building trades unions, SEIU, nurses, firefighters, the League of Women Voters and environmental groups. Newsom and both candidates for governor took no position. The Republican Party supports it, but six county GOP groups and the Tea Party caucus came out against it.
- **Who is voting:** Early ballots are modeled at about 57% Democratic, a stronger Democratic lean than expected, and PPIC found 54% of Democrats voting no.
- **Data centers:** Opponents' argument that the measure would fast-track data centers appears to have hurt the Yes side.
- **Forecasts:** Betting markets on Nov 2 gave it about a 32% chance of passing, on both Kalshi and Polymarket. One forecasting model, On Point Politics, called it a toss-up with a 47.8% chance.

The Yes side's best case is money: it raised about $56 million to the No side's roughly $30 million. Several major newspapers, including the Mercury News and the Sacramento Bee's parent chain, also endorsed it.
