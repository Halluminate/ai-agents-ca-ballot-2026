**Yes.** It's very close to a coin flip, but Yes looks slightly more likely to win. Results weren't out when I checked: AP's live page still showed 0% counted.

**For Yes:**
- **PPIC poll (Oct 16–22):** 51–47 Yes. Republicans backed it 84–15, independents 53–43, and about a third of Democrats (32%) supported it too.
- **Money:** supporters raised about $138–157M, mostly from Sergey Brin's group, with $65M put in during October alone. There was almost no money spent specifically against it; the opposing union committee's funds went mainly to Prop 40.
- **Endorsements:** Gov. Newsom, the firefighters' union, the building-trades unions, the main police union, the state Republican Party and CalChamber all back it. Its "protect your retirement savings" message plays well with older midterm voters.
- **Prop 40 is losing:** prediction markets give the billionaire tax about a 21% chance of passing. In PPIC's poll, Yes on 42 tracks No on 40 almost exactly, so a clear defeat for Prop 40 should carry 42.
- **Markets and forecasters:** Kalshi and Polymarket put Prop 42 at about 54–56% to pass. The On Point Politics forecast gives it 57%.

**For No:**
- **Berkeley IGS poll (Oct 19–25):** 43–45 No, with 12% undecided. Opposition rose 11 points since September while support stayed flat.
- **Downward trend:** PPIC also slipped, from 54% to 51%. Measures under 50% in late polls that are losing ground usually fail.
- **Opponents:** the state Democratic Party, the California Labor Federation and most Bay Area and Sacramento newspapers oppose it.
- **Turnout:** early ballots lean heavily Democratic, and young and Latino voters, who oppose it, are turning out in larger numbers than in 2022.

The prediction markets are thin, and my own estimate puts the final Yes vote around 50–52%, so a narrow loss is entirely plausible.
