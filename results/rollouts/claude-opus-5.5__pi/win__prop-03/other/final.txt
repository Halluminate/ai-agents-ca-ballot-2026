**yes**

The election is November 3, 2026, and I couldn't find any results yet. Every signal I found before the vote points to Prop 3 passing:

- **Polls:** Every poll had Yes above 50%, and support held steady into late October. PPIC had it at 58–40 in September and 58–41 in mid-October. Berkeley IGS had it at 54–37 in late October, the only one of seven measures it tested with majority support. A third poll, by AIMSA, had 56% Yes.
- **Money:** The Yes side raised about $81 million, mostly from the California Teachers Association. The only committee opposing it reported $5,500, and there was no ad campaign against it.
- **Endorsements:** Supporters include Gov. Newsom, Xavier Becerra (the leading candidate for governor), the state Democratic Party and major unions. Even the billionaire-funded group fighting the separate billionaire tax (Prop 40) endorsed Prop 3. Opponents include the Republican Party, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association and CalTax.
- **History:** Voters approved the same tax in 2012 with 55.4% and extended it in 2016 with 63.3%. Renewing an existing tax tends to do better than proposing a new one.
- **Political mood:** Democrats were more enthusiastic about voting this year, and about 80% of them supported Prop 3.
- **Betting markets:** On October 29, Kalshi gave it a 95% chance of passing and Polymarket 94%.

**Risks:** California tax measures often fail, and support for propositions tends to slip near Election Day. There were five tax measures on this ballot, so voters may have been tired of them or confused with Prop 40. A rival teachers union said its own polling showed lower support than PPIC's. None of these looked big enough to erase Prop 3's lead.
