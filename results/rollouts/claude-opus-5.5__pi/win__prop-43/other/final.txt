Prop 43 (ACA 22, on the November 3, 2026 ballot) looks likely to fail. I found no official results yet; the latest sources are from November 2. Here's what points to a loss:

- **Polls:** It trails in every public poll. PPIC had it at 42% yes to 55% no among likely voters on October 16–22, worse than September's 43–51. Berkeley IGS had it at 36% yes, 46% no and 18% undecided on October 19–25. Only Republicans back it in the PPIC poll (68%), against 28% of Democrats and 41% of independents. It's under 50% in every region.
- **Ballot title:** Voters will see "Limits Voters' Ability to Raise Revenues for Local Government Services."
- **Opponents:** Governor Newsom, Democratic nominee Becerra, the California Democratic Party, firefighters, teachers, SEIU, and the state's city, county and special-district associations. Even Buffy Wicks, who wrote the measure as part of a deal to get a broader Howard Jarvis initiative off the ballot, opposes it. Every newspaper editorial board found in one roundup recommended a no vote.
- **Money:** The yes side has raised more, about $16.4M to $10.4M, but polling hasn't moved its way.
- **Prediction markets:** As of November 2, traders gave it about a 15% chance of passing (Kalshi 12%, Polymarket 18%).
