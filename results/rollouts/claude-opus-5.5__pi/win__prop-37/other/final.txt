Yes. Prop 37 will very likely pass. I couldn't find any results yet. The latest pages I found are from November 2, 2026, the day before the election, so this is a forecast and not a result.

- **Polls:** The Public Policy Institute of California (PPIC) had it at 65% yes and 32% no in late October, up from 61–35 in September. It was the strongest of the 14 measures on the ballot. A separate AIMSA poll of 2,000 likely voters (October 19–24) found 59% support. PPIC found majorities in favor across regions and demographic groups. Even Republicans were nearly split, 47% yes to 51% no.
- **Money:** The yes side raised about $33–35 million, mostly from the Realtors, the carpenters' unions and Building a Better California. No opposition committee reported any contributions, and no argument against it was filed for the state voter guide.
- **Supporters:** Governor Newsom, Becerra (the Democratic nominee for governor, leading by 24–27 points), the state Democratic Party, the state labor federation, the California Teachers Association, SEIU and the California Chamber of Commerce all back it. The state Republican Party stayed neutral.
- **Opponents:** The Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, the League of Women Voters, Reform California, a few minor parties and several newspapers oppose it. They ran no paid campaign.
- **Ballot wording:** The ballot summary says "no direct state or local costs," because the homebuyers' loan payments repay the bonds, not taxpayers.

**Caveat:** PPIC has overstated support for bonds before. In the March 2024 primary it had Prop 1 at 59%, and that measure passed with only 50.2%. Even a gap that large would still leave Prop 37 above 50%. In the November 2024 election, PPIC's bond numbers were close to the actual results.
