The latest polls, the money, and the endorsements all point to Proposition 5 failing. It's the November 3, 2026 measure that would remove the "who should replace them?" question from state recall elections. Results hadn't been reported yet when I checked.

- **Polls:** The Public Policy Institute of California (PPIC) poll from October 16–22 had it losing 45% yes to 52% no, with 3% undecided. Its September poll was worse, at 35–59. PPIC is the only public pollster that asked about Prop 5. Support went up 10 points between the two polls, but it was still short of 50%. Measures polling under 50% this late usually fail, because undecided voters tend to vote no.
- **Who supports it:** Only 56% of Democrats back it, even though the state Democratic Party endorsed it. Support is 40% among independents and 28% among Republicans.
- **No campaign:** As of October 22, no committee had formed or raised money for either side. The measure's author, Josh Newman, said he was "not optimistic."
- **Newspapers:** Most major editorial boards urged a no vote, including the SF Chronicle, Mercury News/East Bay Times, San Diego Union-Tribune and OC Register. The Sacramento Bee was the main paper backing it.
