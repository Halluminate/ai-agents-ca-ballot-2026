I'm confident in this. Results weren't posted when I checked (election night is November 3, 2026), so this is a forecast.

- **Polling:** The Public Policy Institute of California, the only pollster that asked about Prop 2, had it at 60% yes to 39% no in September and 61% to 38% in October. In October it had majority support in every region and every demographic group. Republicans were opposed, 23% yes, but Democrats were at 85% and independents at 57%. This pollster's October numbers have usually landed within about 5 points of the final result, so Prop 2 would need an unusually large late collapse to fail.
- **No real opposition campaign:** As of October 22, the Secretary of State listed no campaign committee for or against Prop 2. Opposition came from the Republican Party, Carl DeMaio's Reform California, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, which called it a "Band-Aid," and a few editorial boards. None of them funded a "No" campaign, so most voters will mainly see the friendly ballot title, "Increases State's Rainy Day Fund."
- **Support:** Newsom, Becerra and the state Democratic Party back it, along with firefighters, hospitals, the LA Chamber of Commerce and the state PTA. The SF Chronicle, Mercury News and Sacramento Bee editorial boards endorsed it. The midterm looks Democratic-leaning, with Becerra up 24 to 27 points.
- **History:** Earlier rainy-day fund measures passed easily: 71% in 2004 and 69% in 2014.
- **Betting markets:** On November 2, Kalshi gave it a 92% chance of passing and Polymarket about 72%, though very little money was being traded.
