**No.** This is my judgment on a disputed political question, and a "yes" vote is a reasonable choice if you want California to never have a wealth tax.

Prop 42 would amend the state constitution in two ways:

- **New tax ban:** the state could never create a new tax on owning personal property. That covers stocks, business stakes, savings, retirement accounts and belongings, but not real estate.
- **Backdated tax ban:** most new taxes could not reach back to earlier income, actions or residency. The only exception is a law passed by the Legislature during a governor-declared emergency, reaching back at most one year, with the money spent on that emergency.

Its main purpose is to cancel Prop 40, the one-time 5% billionaire tax. If both pass and 42 gets more yes votes, 40 is void.

**Reasons for no:**
- **It doesn't protect your 401(k) from any real proposal.** There is no plan to tax middle-class retirement accounts. Prop 40 itself excludes them, and every past wealth-tax bill targeted the very rich and died in the Legislature.
- **You don't need it to stop Prop 40.** If you oppose the billionaire tax, you can just vote no on 40. Recent polls show 40 losing (UC Berkeley's IGS: 42% yes, 50% no; PPIC: 46% yes, 52% no).
- **The side effects are broad and permanent.** Undoing it would take another statewide vote. The backdating ban is stricter than current federal law. It could block a common budget move: tax changes passed in June that apply to the whole year, as in 2020 and 2024. It could also hamper rules meant to stop people dodging a tax they know is coming.
- **Its scope may end up in court.** Prop 40's own authors argue it could affect future vehicle license fee changes and even Prop 3. The campaign rejects this, and these critics aren't neutral.
- **Groups that didn't back the billionaire tax still oppose 42.** The SF Chronicle, League of Women Voters and SPUR took no position on 40 but oppose 42. The Mercury News/East Bay Times opposed both, saying it could have supported 42 if it had only cancelled 40.

**Reasons for yes:**
- **Endorsements:** Gov. Newsom, the state Chamber of Commerce, the California Taxpayers Association, the Republican Party, firefighter and police unions, and the San Diego Union-Tribune and Orange County Register.
- **Economic case:** Hoover Institution analysts say a firm promise of no wealth tax could keep wealthy residents, who pay a large share of state income tax, from leaving.
- **Fairness:** taxing people for income or residency from before a tax was passed is unfair.

**No**
