**yes**

The election hadn't been decided when I checked. Every results page I found, including AP, the Secretary of State and The Ballot Book, still showed "awaiting results." So this is a forecast. I'd put the chance that Prop 38 passes at roughly 80%.

- **Polling:** PPIC is the only public pollster that asked about Prop 38. Its October 16–22 survey of likely voters had it ahead 57% to 41%, up from 55% to 42% in September. In past November elections, PPIC's late bond numbers were close or came in under the result. In 2024, Prop 2 polled 52% and got 58.6%, and Prop 4 polled 60% and got 59.8%. Its big misses ran the other way, but in March primaries: 2024's Prop 1 polled 59% and passed with only 50.2%.
- **Money:** The Yes side reported about $71 million in contributions through October 22, almost all from Gary Michelson. The No side reported about $12,000 and ran no paid ads.
- **Endorsements:** Both the state Democratic and Republican parties back it, as does Governor Newsom, along with patient groups like the Alzheimer's Association, the Michael J. Fox Foundation and nurses' associations. Opponents include nearly every major newspaper editorial board, the League of Women Voters, Common Cause, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, the DSA and Tom Steyer.
- **Betting markets:** On the night of November 2, Kalshi had it at about 89% to pass and Polymarket at about 72%.
- **Precedent:** California voters have approved both earlier medical-research bonds: Prop 71 (stem cells, 2004) with 59%, and Prop 14 (stem cells, 2020) with 51% despite having less money behind it.

**What could flip it:** Press coverage has hammered the fact that half the $8.4 billion is written so only Michelson's own institute qualifies. CalMatters reported in October that the institute had just three employees. Voters are also gloomy about the economy, and late-deciding voters tend to break against bonds. For No to win, though, support would need to fall about 8 points from the last poll.
