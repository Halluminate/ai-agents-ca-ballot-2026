

I've researched this thoroughly across the official Voter Information Guide, the Legislative Analyst's Office analysis, campaign arguments/rebuttals, and independent reporting (LA Times, McClatchy, SF Chronicle, CalMatters, Politico, CBS) plus tax-law commentary.

## **NO**

Here's why, in brief:

**1. Its real, binding effect is a "poison pill" against the billionaire tax (Prop 40).** Prop 42's core legal function is to nullify Prop 40 (the one-time 5% tax on California billionaires) if Prop 42 gets *more "yes" votes than Prop 40* — even if Prop 40 itself wins a majority. The nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office explicitly warns of this. The cleanest way to decide is to recognize that a "yes" on 42 is mainly a vote to kill the billionaire tax via a side measure.

**2. The "protect your retirement" framing is widely viewed as a decoy.** Multiple experts (including UC Berkeley law professor Brian Galle, who helped draft Prop 40) and editorial boards describe the retirement-savings warnings as a "red herring." California does not currently tax the ownership of financial assets, and no retirement/savings tax is actually pending — so the stated purpose doesn't match the measure's timing, funding (~$128M, largely from billionaire donors like Sergey Brin), or structure.

**3. It imposes a permanent constitutional tax restriction on speculative grounds.** It would lock the state into its Constitution against *any* new personal-property tax, reducing fiscal flexibility in a state with large unfunded pension liabilities and volatile revenues — all to preempt a tax that isn't on the ballot for ordinary people.

**4. It's structurally anti-democratic.** It lets a smaller, heavily funded coalition override the majority's direct decision on the actual tax question (Prop 40) through a "most yes votes wins" conflict rule. If you oppose the billionaire tax, the direct and honest way to say so is to vote **NO on Prop 40** — not to pass a separate constitutional amendment with a misleading label.

**Bottom line:** Vote **NO** on Prop 42. (If you support the billionaire tax, NO is even more clear-cut; if you oppose it, vote NO on Prop 40 directly rather than through this decoy.)
