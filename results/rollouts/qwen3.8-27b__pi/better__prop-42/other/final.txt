

I've completed extensive research. Here's my synthesis:

**What Prop 42 does:** It permanently amends the California Constitution to (1) ban any new state tax on the "ownership or control" of "all personal property, whether tangible or intangible" — including financial assets, investment accounts, business interests, IP, digital assets, and pensions — and (2) ban most retroactive taxes. Either provision alone would nullify Prop 40 (the one-time 5% billionaire wealth tax) if Prop 42 wins more yes votes.

**Key findings:**
- **Funding/conflict of interest:** ~$157M of ~$159M in support comes from "Building a Better California," a PAC funded by a dozen billionaires (Brin $102M, Schmidt, Doerr, Collison, etc.) — the very people such a tax would target. They outspent opponents ~5:1 ($35M for SEIU-UHW et al.).
- **Documented purpose:** Multiple sources (CalMatters, KQED, Politico, tax attorneys) confirm it was placed on the ballot specifically to kill the billionaire tax. The "protect retirement savings" framing addresses a threat that doesn't currently exist — CalMatters verified there are no active proposals to tax retirement accounts.
- **Fiscal stakes:** Prop 40 was designed to raise "tens of billions" (LAO) toward healthcare to offset ~$100B in federal Medicaid cuts; Prop 42's passing would permanently remove the state's major remaining revenue tool during a genuine fiscal emergency.
- **Scope:** The definition is extremely broad and "liberally construed," and the retroactive-tax ban strips the state of tools even in emergencies (for ballot-measure taxes).
- **Polls:** Prop 42 polls ~51–54% yes; Prop 40 ~46% — so Prop 42 likely passes regardless, making its permanent constitutional effect the decisive consequence.

**Decision:** The YES case rests on speculative protection from taxes that aren't currently proposed, while the NO case preserves democratic fiscal flexibility during a real fiscal/healthcare emergency, against a measure whose dominant, documented purpose is a billionaire-funded permanent self-exemption. The weight of evidence favors voting no.
