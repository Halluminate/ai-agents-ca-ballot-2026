

I've now completed extensive research on California Proposition 37 (November 3, 2026) — "Creates Loan Program for Middle-Income Buyers of Qualified New Homes." Here's a summary of my findings:

**What it does:** Authorizes CalHFA to sell up to $25 billion in revenue bonds to offer fixed-rate second mortgages covering up to 17% of the price of *newly built* homes (priced ~$1M–$1.5M), for buyers with income under 200% of area median income who put down at least 3%. Bonds would be repaid from homebuyers' payments, not taxpayers.

**Key concerns found in research:**
- **Voters can't know the terms:** The LAO notes it's unknown whether investors will even buy these riskier second-mortgage-backed bonds, and at what rate. The promised "below-market" rates (cited in the only ballot argument, which had no opposing argument submitted) depend on foundations buying bonds at ~3% — unconfirmed.
- **Affordability math is weak:** PPIC found less than 40% of eligible renters could afford the added monthly payment on an entry-level home; for homes near the price cap, less than half of one percent of eligible renters could afford them.
- **Targeting is loose:** No priority for first-time/first-generation buyers; households earning $300K+ in wealthy counties qualify; former homeowners not excluded — League of Women Voters (a major "No" voice) warns it may reinforce racial and economic disparities.
- **Price-inflation risk:** Standard down-payment-assistance critique (AEI, Hoover): subsidizing demand without verified new supply just bids up prices; the "qualified builder" construction incentive's impact is uncertain (proponents' own "190,000 homes" estimate is acknowledged as high-end).
- **"No taxpayer cost" has an asterisk:** A failing $25B state-branded program could invite political bailout pressure despite the legal no-liability language.
- **Endorsements split but No-leaning among news orgs:** Bay Area News Group, Press-Enterprise, OC Register, and San Diego Union-Tribune endorse No; LWVC, Reform California oppose. Yes: CA Association of Realtors (largest funder, ~$33M), unions, builders, SF Bay Guardian, Sacramento Bee, SF Chronicle.
- **Skeptics note limited downside, but also limited upside:** Even supportive analysts like the California Budget & Policy Center's director say they're skeptical it works as advertised.

**My decision:** The measure asks voters to authorize $25 billion in borrowing with unknown interest rates, loan terms, and unclear benefits, targeting a very broad "middle class," while the main claimed advantage (cheap loans) rests on unconfirmed philanthropic funding. The documented criticisms from the nonpartisan LAO, PPIC, and LWVC outweigh the benefits.
