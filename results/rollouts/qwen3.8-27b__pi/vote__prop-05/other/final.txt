The URL's live ballot page (Nov. 3, 2026 general election) is Proposition 5, "Changes Recall Election Process for Statewide Officers" (SCA 1, authored by former Sen. Josh Newman): it would remove the second question ("who should replace the recalled official?") from recall ballots, so vacancies would instead be filled the way any other vacancy is—gubernatorial appointment for executive offices, special election for legislative seats, and the Lieutenant Governor taking over the governorship. Weighing the official Legislative Analyst analysis, the full proposed constitutional text, both sides' printed arguments and Q&As, the Clark v. Weber (2022) ruling, the 2021 recall's actual results and costs, and editorial/independent coverage (CalMatters, SF Chronicle, Mercury News, PPIC 59–35 polling): the core tradeoff is that voters keep the power to oust an official by majority, but lose the power to choose the replacement—handing that choice to the sitting governor (potentially for years, in executive offices), to the Lieutenant Governor (for a recalled governor), and creating months-long vacancies plus extra special elections, while also letting a just-recalled official run to reclaim the seat using unlimited recall-defense money (a provision even the author couldn't explain). The "plurality successor" defect is real but narrow and rare (two governor recalls ever; only 6 successful state recalls since 1913), and the current system is the one where the people always pick the successor.
