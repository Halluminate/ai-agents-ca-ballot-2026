

Based on my research into California Proposition 43 (Nov 3, 2026 ballot) — the measure to require two-thirds approval for voter-initiated local special taxes:

**Polling evidence (all show No leading):**
- UC Berkeley IGS / LA Times poll (Oct 19–25, 2026): 36% Yes vs. 46% No
- PPIC October 2026 statewide survey: 42% Yes vs. 55% No (support has been *dropping*, not rising)
- On Point Politics forecast (updated Nov 2, 2026): "Proposition 43 is **all but certain to fail**" — Yes at 42% vs. the 50% needed; only a **3% chance to pass** (97/100 simulations have No winning)

**Structural factors favoring "No":**
- The status quo is majority vote; PPIC's survey director noted uncertain voters "tend to err on the side of 'no'"
- Broad, well-organized opposition: firefighters, teachers, SEIU, local governments, housing/transit advocates
- Precedent: In 2024, Prop 5 (a similar tax-threshold change) failed by ~10 points
- The measure only prospectively restricts future citizen-initiated taxes, limiting its appeal

The "No" side is clearly more likely to win.
