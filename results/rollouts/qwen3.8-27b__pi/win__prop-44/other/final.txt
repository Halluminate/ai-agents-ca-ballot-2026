

The research is conclusive. Key findings for California Proposition 44 (November 3, 2026):

**Polls:**
- A mid-September PPIC poll found Prop 44 was the *least popular* of the propositions surveyed, with only **34% of likely voters supporting** it (one in three).

**Spending:**
- From July 1 to September 19, the "Yes" campaign (SEIU-UHW) spent just ~$20,000, while the "No" campaign spent **$32+ million**. Totals through October 22: Yes ~$16.9M vs. No ~$38.6M.

**Endorsements:**
- "No" side: both major state parties, the Governor (announced opposition Oct 9, joining 300+ organizations and 1,000 clinics), California Primary Care Association (co-sponsor), California Medical Association, Planned Parenthood Affiliates, California Hospital Association, California Teachers Association, state Chamber of Commerce, and newspaper endorsements (e.g., Sacramento Bee/McClatchy).
- "Yes" side: essentially just SEIU-UHW/AFL-CIO.

**Substance:**
- Clinics already average ~80% of revenue on care; one analysis found 89% of clinic organizations would fail the 90% test in year one, facing ~$1.6–1.7B in penalties — a powerful "No" narrative.

Historically, ballot initiatives in California fail more often than they pass, and this one has near-universal elite opposition and low polling. The "No" side is far more likely to win (i.e., the measure is likely to be rejected).
