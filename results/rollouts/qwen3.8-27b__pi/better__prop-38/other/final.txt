

Based on my research, here is my assessment of California Proposition 38 (November 3, 2026 — "$8.4 billion in bonds for immunology medical research"):

**Key findings:**
- **Hidden single recipient:** The measure sends half the bond ($4.2 billion) to one "unnamed" UC-affiliated nonprofit meeting hyper-specific criteria (3.5M+ outpatient visits, 35,000+ inpatients, 200,000+ sq ft, $250M in philanthropy, two ~$50M donations matching verbatim). CalMatters' analysis and a **UCLA spokesperson confirmed only one institute qualifies: CIII**, co-founded by billionaire Gary Michelson — who authored the measure, chairs the institute's board, and donated $61M+ of the $71M raised. The Yes campaign's own spokesperson concedes CIII is "very likely" to qualify.
- **Transparency exemption:** The text states the institute "shall not be deemed a public agency for any purpose under state law" — exempting it from the Public Records Act and open-meeting laws. Three former nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office analysts publicly warned it "hijacks the initiative process to steer public funds to a billionaire's philanthropic project, and then hides its tracks."
- **Unproven recipient:** CIII had only 3 employees as of last month, a fund balance under $20M, a president reporting 2 hours/week on its tax form, no public strategic plan, and a building not complete until 2027. The state already appropriated $500M for its facility.
- **Debunked "pays for itself" claim:** The prior stem cell bond (Prop 71, 2004) returned only ~$16M in IP revenue against ~$6B in bond costs — under 1%. The LAO itself calls revenue "uncertain" and possibly decades away. Taxpayers would owe $500–600M/year from the General Fund for ~20 years (total cost $12B+).
- **Narrow, closed earmark:** Only immunology/immunotherapy counts; grants go only to institutions on a closed council that must confer with CIII, which also gets clinical-trial collaboration rights.
- **Overwhelming No consensus:** 12 newspaper editorial boards (SF Chronicle, Mercury News, OC Register, McClatchy CA, etc.), League of Women Voters of CA, Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Assn., Common Cause, Consumer Watchdog, Tom Steyer, George Skelton.
- **The "federal cuts" rationale is overstated:** Courts and Congress have largely restored/preserved NIH funding.
- **Polls:** 55–57% support, so it could pass — but majority polling doesn't make a flawed structure sound.

The science is laudable, but this is $8.4B of voter-mandated General Fund debt with an undisclosed, criteria-fitted private earmark, waived transparency laws, and a self-payback promise contradicted by California's own experience. The better-informed vote is:
