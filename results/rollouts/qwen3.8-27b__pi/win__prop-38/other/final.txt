

Based on my extensive research, I have a clear picture of California's Proposition 38 (November 3, 2026): $8.4 billion in bonds for immunology/immunotherapy research, half to a single UC-affiliated institute (effectively the Michelson-backed CIII) and half to competitive grants.

**Evidence favoring YES:**
- **Polling**: PPIC's September 2026 statewide survey (the only public poll on it) shows 55% yes vs. 42% no, 3% undecided. Even Democrats (76-21) and independents (54-43) lean yes; only Republicans oppose (76-23).
- **Money**: The most one-sided financial race on the ballot — $76.2 million for yes (Michelson's foundation funded ~$40-50M) vs. just $3,782 reported by the opposition committee.
- **Endorsements**: Governor Newsom (Oct. 2026), both the state Democratic and Republican parties, 60+ medical/patient groups (Alzheimer's Association, Michael J. Fox Foundation, ALS Association, NMS, Prostate Cancer Foundation, ANA/CA, etc.), and graduate student unions.
- **Precedent**: California voters have a strong record of approving research bond measures — Prop 71 (2004, stem cells) passed ~62%, Prop 14 (2020, cancer research) passed 60.6%.

**Evidence favoring NO:**
- Every editorial board I could find opposes it; critics call it "ballot-box science," note $4.2 billion funneled to one billionaire-backed institute, and flag $500-600M/year debt for ~20 years. Older voters (55+) nearly split (50-47).

**Weighing it**: A 13-point lead in the only poll, a ~20:1 money advantage, rare bipartisan elite endorsement, and California's history of approving research bond measures all point the same way. Editorial opposition alone has not defeated well-resourced bond measures in this state.

**yes**
