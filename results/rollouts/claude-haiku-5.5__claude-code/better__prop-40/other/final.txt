I won't give a one-word answer on this one. Whether yes or no is better depends on values the research can't settle, and the key predictions are disputed. A bare verdict would overstate what the evidence shows. Here's the picture and where your vote turns.

**What it does:** A one-time 5% tax on the net worth of roughly 200 billionaires who were California residents on Jan. 1, 2026. It's due in 2027, and payment can be spread over five years. At least 90% goes to health care. The revenue is exempt from school-funding requirements and the state spending limit.

**Revenue is disputed:** The LAO says the state "probably would collect tens of billions of dollars" over several years, with income-tax losses probably under $1 billion a year. The union projects about $100 billion over five years. A Hoover Institution working paper (Jaros & Rauh) projects about $30 billion up front, but a net loss of about $39 billion once lost income taxes are counted. The core disagreement is whether departing billionaires cancel the revenue, and whether the tax should be treated as one-time or effectively permanent.

**Legal and structural risk:** The Jan. 1, 2026 residency date is expected to be challenged as retroactive. One expert says it "ends up in court." Because the tax is in the constitution, lawmakers can adjust it by two-thirds vote only in ways that further its purposes. CalMatters reports that expanding it beyond billionaires would probably need another statewide vote.

**Props 41 and 42 could void it:** Both were written to counter Prop 40. Under the state constitution's rule, the measure with the most yes votes prevails in a conflict. So if 41 or 42 passes with more yes votes than Prop 40, the tax could be voided even if Prop 40 passes. In PPIC's Oct. 27 poll, Prop 40 was at 46% yes and 52% no, down six points since September. Prop 42 had 51% support and Prop 41 had 48%.

**Money and the sponsor:** Opponents, including business groups and wealthy donors, have put about $317 million into the campaign, which also backs 41 and 42. Opponents include Gov. Newsom, the teachers' union, Planned Parenthood, and several major newspapers. Supporters include Sen. Bernie Sanders, the state Democratic Party, and the state labor federation. The sponsor's leader, SEIU-UHW's Dave Regan, is the subject of an internal review over alleged intimidation and extortion. He denies the allegations and calls them a smear campaign.

**Where your vote turns:**
- **Leans yes** if you think the state should raise a large one-time sum from the very wealthy for health care, to offset federal cuts that could remove about 2 million residents from coverage. You also need to accept the retroactive date and years of litigation, and find the LAO or union estimates more credible.
- **Leans no** if you oppose wealth taxes or constitutional lock-ins, find the net revenue closer to the Hoover estimate, or think the legal and expansion risks outweigh the health money. Opponents also say a one-time levy won't fix budget problems and could push wealthy people out of state.

A yes on 40 isn't enough by itself to make it take effect. If you're voting yes, the 41 and 42 results matter too.

If you tell me which of these trade-offs matter most to you, I can help you work through how they apply.
