I'm not going to give a one-word yes or no on this one, even though you asked. Whether Prop 38 is worth it depends on how you weigh the research upside against roughly $10–12 billion in repayments and the measure's design. That's a values call on a contested ballot question, so I'd rather give you the key facts and a way to decide.

**What a yes vote does**
- Authorizes **$8.4 billion** in state bonds for immunology and immunotherapy research.
- Half (about $4.2B) goes to one institute chosen by the state Department of Public Health. The other half funds competitive grants.
- Treatments from funded research generally must be sold in California at a 20% discount, and the state gets 10% of the research revenue.
- Cost: about **$500–600 million a year for about 20 years** from the General Fund, roughly 10% more than paying cash. The LAO says research revenue could offset some or all of it, but the amount and timing are uncertain. The total is about $10–12B by my arithmetic.

**Case for yes**
- The campaign argues federal research funding is unreliable and the state needs a stable source.
- Backers say immunotherapies already treat some cancers and this could speed treatments to patients.
- Supporters include Gov. Newsom, the California Democratic Party, the Michael J. Fox Foundation, and the Alzheimer's Association.

**Case for no**
- CalMatters finds the criteria likely leave only one eligible institute: the California Institute for Immunology and Immunotherapy, which billionaire Gary Michelson co-founded. Gifts from Gary and Alya Michelson closely match the bond's wording. The campaign says the language wasn't written for that institute.
- As of September the institute had three hires. Its UCLA research park won't be complete before late 2028 at the earliest. The institute is exempt from open-meeting and public-records laws.
- Critics say bonds are the wrong tool for funding one research field, the debt would crowd out other priorities, and the payoff is uncertain.
- Most Yes money (roughly $66–71M raised) comes from Michelson and his nonprofit. Five newspaper editorial boards oppose the measure. The American Association of Immunologists' policy committee recommended dropping its endorsement, but its council kept it.

**Caveats**
- Campaign totals and the repayment term vary by source. The LAO says 20 years; some reports say 25.
- Voting no doesn't end state research bonding. A $7.5B science and health research bond (SB 895) is set for the March 2028 ballot.

**How to decide**
- **Yes** if you think the research is worth about $10–12B in repayments over roughly 20 years, and you can accept one institute receiving half the money under those exemptions.
- **No** if the debt or the design concerns outweigh the research for you.

If you're voting today, polls close at 8 p.m. local time.
