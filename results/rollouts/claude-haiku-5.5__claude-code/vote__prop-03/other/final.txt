**Yes.**

Prop 3 keeps the top rates (10.3% to 12.3%) from falling back to 9.3% in 2031. The Legislative Analyst's Office estimates $5 billion to $15 billion a year is at stake, with about 40% going to schools and community colleges. The administration projects structural deficits of roughly $10 billion a year through 2029–30. The best case for No is that the top combined rate would stay at 13.3%, and research on the 2012 increase found behavioral responses offset about 45% of the windfall in year one. If you put tax competitiveness and spending restraint first, vote No.

Caveats: I couldn't access the measure's full text, so the audit claims in the official Yes argument and the health-care share are unverified. Supporters say a No could cost schools up to $15 billion a year, but the LAO's 40% school share implies about $2–6 billion (my arithmetic; the sources don't fully reconcile this). Prop 3 is the income-tax extension, not the separate billionaire tax (Prop 40).
