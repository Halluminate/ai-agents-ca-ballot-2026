I'm not going to give a bare yes or no. Whether Prop 42 is better depends on contested value judgments: whether a billionaire tax should stay possible, and whether tax limits belong in the Constitution. I don't think I should make that call for you. Here is what I found in the official guide, the Legislative Analyst's analysis, the measure's text, and press and expert coverage.

**What it does**
- It bars new state taxes enacted on or after Jan. 1, 2026 on owning personal property. That is defined broadly to include stocks, retirement accounts, business interests, intellectual property and art.
- It also bars most new retroactive taxes, including ones based on past residency.
- It doesn't repeal existing taxes, and by its text it doesn't cover income, capital-gains or real-estate taxes.
- If it gets more "yes" votes than Prop 40 (the one-time billionaire tax), Prop 40 could be voided even if it also passes.
- The Legislative Analyst says it could make it "somewhat harder" for the state to raise taxes. How much is unclear.

**Case for yes:** A permanent constitutional ban on wealth taxes and retroactive tax surprises. Backers include Gov. Newsom, the firefighters and building-trades unions, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, and the San Diego Union-Tribune. They point to wealth-tax bills introduced since 2019.

**Case for no:**
- About $128M was raised in support, almost all from the billionaire-funded committee that also fights Prop 40.
- Opponents include SEIU-UHW, the state Democratic Party, Bernie Sanders, and the SF Chronicle and Sacramento Bee editorial boards. They call it a decoy, noting that Prop 40 exempts most retirement accounts.
- The Mercury News also opposes Prop 40, yet says Prop 42 is too broad.
- Prop 40's drafters say vague wording like "ownership or control" could hamper vehicle-fee changes and Prop 3's capital-gains taxation. The Yes campaign disputes the Prop 3 claim.

**Quick guide**
- If you want Prop 40 to be able to take effect, vote no.
- If you oppose wealth taxes and want them banned constitutionally, vote yes.
- If you oppose Prop 40 but think Prop 42 is too broad or vague, vote no.
