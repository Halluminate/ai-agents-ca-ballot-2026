I'm not going to give a bare yes or no on this one. I read the measure text, the Legislative Analyst's analysis, and coverage and endorsements from both sides. The better vote depends on your own values, mainly on whether you want Prop 40 (the billionaire tax) to take effect. Picking for you would mean taking a side in a contested political fight.

What I found:

- **What it does:** The State Auditor would review programs funded by new special taxes, both before voter initiatives reach the ballot and every four years afterward. It also bars new state taxes from exempting their revenue from the Gann spending limit. The Legislative Analyst's Office (LAO) expects audit costs in the low millions of dollars a year and calls the net fiscal effect unknown.
- **Main effect:** The LAO says that if Prop 41 gets more "yes" votes than Prop 40, Prop 40 could be blocked even if a majority approves it. Prop 41 is funded largely by Sergey Brin and other tech billionaires who oppose Prop 40.
- **Supporters:** CalTax, CalCPAs, CalChamber, former State Auditor Tom Hayes, and the San Diego Union-Tribune editorial board call it taxpayer protection.
- **Opponents:**
  - SEIU-UHW (Prop 40's sponsor), the California Democratic Party, and the SF Chronicle and Sacramento Bee call it a poison pill aimed at Prop 40.
  - The League of Women Voters of California opposes it because the Gann limit is too rigid.
  - The Mercury News also opposes Prop 40. It likes the audits but objects to extending the Gann limit to all new taxes.

If you want Prop 40 to take effect, vote no on 41. If you oppose Prop 40, it comes down to whether you also want the audit and spending-limit rules for every future special tax. Prop 40's opponents are split on that. Tell me where you stand on Prop 40 and I'll give you the one-word answer that matches it.
