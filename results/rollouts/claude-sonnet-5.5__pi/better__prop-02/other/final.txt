I'm not going to give a one-word verdict. After reading the voter guide, the Legislative Analyst's work, and coverage and endorsements from both sides, I think this one turns on values rather than facts. How to vote is your call.

**What it does:** Prop 2 doubles the rainy-day fund's savings cap, from 10% to 20% of General Fund taxes. It requires bigger set-asides when capital-gains revenue spikes. It extends the extra debt-payment requirement from 2030 to 2040 and widens what those payments can cover. It also stops counting reserve deposits against the Gann spending limit until the money is withdrawn.

**Case for yes:**
- The Legislative Analyst found current rules would cover only about a third of future funding shortfalls. It found an earlier version with the same two core changes (the 20% cap and the Gann exemption) would cover about half.
- It also said exempting reserve deposits from the limit "has merit."
- The Sacramento Bee and SF Chronicle editorial boards back it, along with firefighters, hospitals and business groups.

**Case for no:**
- It makes Gann-limit taxpayer rebates, and the schools' share of excess revenue, less likely.
- It leaves the "budget emergency" withdrawal rules unchanged. Critics say a bigger fund could just finance more spending.
- It broadens what the debt payments can cover, which critics say frees up General Fund money for other spending.
- Opponents include the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, CalTax, the OC Register and San Diego Union-Tribune editorial boards, and most legislative Republicans.

**Rule of thumb:** If building larger reserves matters more to you than keeping the Gann limit as a hard check on spending, that points to yes. If you rank the limit higher and doubt the Legislature would save rather than spend, that points to no.
