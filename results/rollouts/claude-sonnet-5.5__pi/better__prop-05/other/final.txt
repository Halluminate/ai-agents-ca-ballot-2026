I'm going to pass on a one-word pick. This measure splits along party lines, and the choice depends on how you weigh competing values, not on a fact I can look up. Here is what the research found.

**What it is:** The link shows the Nov. 3, 2026 Prop 5 on recall elections.
- **Yes** removes the "who replaces them?" question from state-level recalls. Legislative seats would be filled by special election, other statewide offices by the governor's appointment, and a recalled governor by the Lieutenant Governor.
- **No** keeps today's two-question ballot.

**Best case for yes:**
- Today a replacement needs only a plurality. In 2018, Josh Newman's replacement won with just under 34% of the vote, while about 42% voted against the recall.
- Only 7 of the 19 states with state-level recall use same-day replacement elections (NCSL).
- Even the SF Chronicle, which opposes the measure, says it might support dropping the second question on its own.
- Supporters include the League of Women Voters, Common Cause, the Democratic Party, the Sacramento Bee/McClatchy editorial boards and LA Times columnists.

**Best case for no:**
- Voters lose a direct say in who replaces a recalled official.
- Legislative seats stay vacant until a special election.
- The Lieutenant Governor, who may share a recalled governor's politics, would take over.
- The measure lets recalled officials run in the replacement election, which is barred today. Newman told the Chronicle, "It wouldn't pain me if it wasn't there."
- It leaves California's low signature thresholds for qualifying a recall unchanged.
- Opponents include the California Republican Party, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, and the editorial boards of the Mercury News/East Bay Times, SF Chronicle, OC Register and San Diego Union-Tribune.

**Other facts:**
- The Legislative Analyst calls the net fiscal effect unknown: savings or costs of millions per recall, under 0.1% of the General Fund.
- The latest PPIC poll (Oct. 16–22) shows 45% yes and 52% no.

**Rule of thumb:**
- If you most want replacements to have majority support, that points to yes.
- If you want voters to choose the replacement when they remove someone, or the "recalled official can run again" provision bothers you, that points to no.
