**no**

The No side looks slightly more likely to win Prop 42. This is a forecast, not a result. The AP, Secretary of State and county results pages I checked all showed 0% counted, and I found no post-election reporting.

- **Polls:** The two best polls disagree. PPIC (Oct 16–22) had Prop 42 at 51% yes, 47% no. Berkeley IGS (Oct 19–25) had it at 43% yes, 45% no, with 12% undecided. Both show support falling since September, and IGS's undecided voters moved almost entirely to No last month. The IGS pollster called the trend "ominous." A 2025 check suggests both pollsters understated Democratic-side turnout, which would hurt Prop 42.
- **Electorate:** The expected electorate is heavily Democratic. Becerra leads the governor's race by about 24 to 27 points, and early ballots are about 51% Democratic. PPIC found Democrats about 32% yes on Prop 42, and IGS found them lower, near 19%. I estimated that Prop 42 needs roughly 30% or more of Democratic-leaning voters to support it to reach 50%.
- **Opposition:** The state Democratic Party, the California Labor Federation and several major newspaper editorial boards oppose it. The media framing is that it is a "poison pill" aimed at killing Prop 40, the billionaire tax. IGS found opposition much higher among voters who knew that.
- **For Yes:** The Yes campaign has a large money edge, about $157 million against roughly $32 million shared with the Prop 40 campaign. Newsom, firefighters, building trades and police unions back it, and its "protect your retirement" message is popular. Prediction markets put its chance at about 54–57%, though those markets are thin and had it near 80% a week earlier.

My estimate is that Yes ends up around 49%, so a narrow loss is somewhat more likely than a narrow win.
