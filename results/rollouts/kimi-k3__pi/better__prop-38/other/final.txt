Based on my research, here's what I found about Proposition 38 (November 3, 2026 California General Election):

**What it does:** Authorizes $8.4 billion in general obligation bonds for immunology/immunotherapy research, costing taxpayers $500–600 million annually for ~20+ years from the General Fund.

**Key findings:**

1. **Half the money ($4.2 billion) is effectively earmarked for one private institute.** The measure's eligibility criteria (200,000+ sq ft facility, specific hospital admission volumes, a $50M microbiome donation, etc.) are so precise that only one entity in California qualifies: the California Institute for Immunology and Immunotherapy at UCLA — co-founded and principally funded by billionaire Gary Michelson, who *wrote the proposition* and poured ~$40M+ into the Yes campaign (Yes side raised $71M; No side raised ~$12K).

2. **Transparency exemptions.** The measure's language specifically exempts the institute receiving $4.2 billion in public money from the California Public Records Act, open-meeting laws, and political reform laws (CalMatters).

3. **The institute is barely operational** — it had only three employees as of October 2026, far short of its own staffing plans.

4. **The "pays for itself" claim is speculative.** The nonpartisan LAO says revenue from discoveries is uncertain and could take decades, if it materializes at all. The measure commits funds during a structural state budget deficit.

5. **Broad, cross-ideological opposition:** League of Women Voters, Common Cause, Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, and editorial boards of the Mercury News/East Bay Times ("an $8.4 billion money grab"), Sacramento Bee/McClatchy papers, Press Democrat, and Orange County Register all urge No. An SF Chronicle columnist called it "the worst measure on California's ballot." Even the American Association of Immunologists' own policy committee recommended rescinding its endorsement after learning of the earmark.

While immunology research is a worthy cause — and federal funding cuts are a real problem — this measure is a fiscally irresponsible, self-dealing vehicle that locks billions in borrowed taxpayer money into a predetermined private nonprofit while waiving standard transparency laws.

**no**
