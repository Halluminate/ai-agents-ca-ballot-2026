**No.**

I researched this extensively (the official voter guide, the LAO's impartial analysis, the full initiative text, campaign finance records, polling, expert commentary, and editorial boards). This is the November 3, 2026 measure sponsored by the California Chamber of Commerce to amend CEQA.

The short version of my reasoning: CEQA genuinely needs reform — deadlines and curbs on abusive litigation are sensible, and even opponents concede that. But Prop 45 goes far beyond deadlines. It lets applicants dictate a single project "alternative," freezes review standards at application, caps public comment, restricts tribal consultation to federally recognized tribes only, and severely constrains courts (270-day limits, remedies limited to pieces of projects, an interpretive clause tilted toward approval). It applies the same fast track not just to infill housing and clean energy, but to freeway expansions, greenfield sprawl in fire zones, warehouses, and possibly data centers — with poll-tested exclusions (high-speed rail, the Delta tunnel, nuclear) that reveal it's not principled policy.

Tellingly, many leading CEQA *reformers* — the SF Chronicle editorial board (a longtime critic of CEQA abuse), former Senate leader Darrell Steinberg, Sen. Catherine Blakespear, Berkeley and Stanford environmental law scholars, and Gov. Newsom (who stayed neutral) — declined to back it, largely because the Legislature already enacted major CEQA reforms in 2025 (AB 130/SB 131) and is better positioned to keep iterating than a 41-page, corporate-drafted initiative statute that's hard to amend. When the people who spent years fighting CEQA abuse say "right problem, wrong tool," that's decisive for me.

**Vote No.**
