**What Prop 43 does (Nov. 3, 2026 ballot):** A legislative constitutional amendment (ACA 22) requiring two-thirds voter approval — up from a simple majority — for citizen-initiated local special taxes, effective Jan. 1, 2027. It also constitutionally bars using initiatives to impose property taxes beyond Prop 13's 1% limit. It's prospective only (existing taxes grandfathered), and it was a compromise: the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association withdrew a broader, already-qualified anti-tax initiative in exchange.

**Context:** Since the CA Supreme Court's 2017 *Upland* decision, citizen-initiated special taxes have needed only a majority vote; roughly two dozen have passed with 50–66% support, funding childcare, homelessness services, fire protection, health services, and transportation.

**Weighing it:** The "yes" side has a genuine consistency argument (the same tax shouldn't face different thresholds based on who files the paperwork, and government allies have gamed the initiative route). But the "no" case is stronger: the measure cuts no one's taxes, permanently embeds a supermajority requirement in the constitution that lets a 33.4% minority veto what up to 66.6% of voters want, weakens the citizen initiative — the tool voters use when officials won't act — and would have killed numerous majority-approved measures funding fire, health, and housing services. Even the measure's own author, Asm. Buffy Wicks, now opposes it, and California's history with two-thirds requirements (e.g., the pre-2010 budget rule) shows they produce gridlock and minority rule rather than better policy.
