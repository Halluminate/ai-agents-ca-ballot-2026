Based on extensive research, here's what I found about California Proposition 44 (November 3, 2026):

**What it does:** Requires nonprofit Federally Qualified Health Centers (community clinics serving low-income patients) to spend at least 90% of revenue on "program services," with penalties equal to any shortfall. Clinics currently average ~80%.

**Key findings:**

1. **It's a single-special-interest measure.** The sole funder (~$17M) is SEIU-UHW, the union led by Dave Regan, who has a documented 15-year pattern of filing ~50 ballot initiatives as negotiating leverage—including three dialysis propositions voters rejected by 20–36 points. A federal racketeering lawsuit filed by the California Primary Care Association alleges Regan offered to call off Prop 44 in exchange for the clinics' cooperation in unionizing 25,000 workers. Notably, the union's failed 2025 legislative version would have *exempted* clinics that created "labor-management cooperation committees"—revealing the measure's true purpose.

2. **The policy itself is flawed.** These are nonprofits already legally required to reinvest 100% of revenue into their mission—there are no profits to curb. Analyses found 88–91% of clinics would fail the 90% threshold in year one, facing ~$1.7 billion in penalties. The measure doesn't even define which expenses qualify, leaving it to the Attorney General, creating uncertainty around spending on translation, care coordination, IT, and facilities—at a time when federal Medicaid cuts already threaten clinic survival.

3. **Opposition is remarkably broad and bipartisan:** both the California Democratic *and* Republican parties, the California Medical Association, American Academy of Pediatrics, Planned Parenthood, the California Teachers Association, school nurses, and emergency physicians. Every editorial board I found (Sacramento Bee/McClatchy, Bay Area News Group, OC Register, Press-Enterprise) recommended No. The only support is SEIU-UHW itself and the state labor federation—and even the union slashed its own campaign funding late in the race.

**no**
