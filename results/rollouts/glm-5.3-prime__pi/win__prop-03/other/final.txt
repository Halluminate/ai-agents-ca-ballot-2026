I have gathered extensive evidence on California's 2026 Proposition 3. Here's a summary of my research:

**The Measure:** Proposition 3 on the November 3, 2026 ballot makes permanent the existing high-income tax rates (first passed as Prop 30 in 2012, extended by Prop 55 in 2016) on individuals earning over $371,000, maintaining $5–15 billion annually for schools and health care.

**Key evidence on which side is likely to win:**

1. **Polling — consistent majority support:**
   - PPIC (Oct. 16–22, 2026): **58% Yes** / 41% No
   - UC Berkeley IGS (Oct. 19–25, 2026): **54% Yes** / 37% No — the *only* one of seven surveyed measures with majority support
   - AIMSA (Oct. 19–24): 56% Yes
   - PPIC September: 58% Yes; IGS September: 54% Yes — support has been stable at 54–58% for months, with very few undecided voters
   - A forecast model gives Yes ~57% with a ~95% chance of passing

2. **Fundraising — a 16,000:1 blowout:**
   - Yes side: **$89.3 million** (CTA $60.2M, NEA $7.5M, CSEA, CFT, SEIU)
   - No side: **$5,500 total** (essentially no campaign at all)

3. **Historical precedent:** Voters have approved this exact tax twice — Prop 30 (2012, 55.4%) and Prop 55 (2016, 62.4%).

4. **Favorable framing:** It's an *existing* tax being made permanent ("protects schools without raising your taxes"), a popular cause (education/health care), in a Democratic-leaning midterm electorate (Democrats support it 82%).

The "No" campaign has virtually no money, and while undecided voters and tax-measure fatigue typically break toward "no," the Yes side's lead is large, stable, and backed by an enormous ground operation (200,000+ doors knocked). The **Yes side is far more likely to win**.
