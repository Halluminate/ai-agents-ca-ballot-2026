Based on my research, I have a clear picture of this race. Here's the evidence I gathered:

**California Proposition 1 (Nov. 3, 2026) — Veterans and Affordable Housing Bond Act:**

**Polling (the strongest indicator):**
- PPIC October 2026 survey (Oct. 16–22): **60% yes / 38% no** among likely voters, with only 2% undecided
- PPIC September 2026 survey: 55% yes / 43% no — support *increasing* as Election Day approaches
- AIMSA poll (Oct. 19–24): 54% support
- Partisan breakdown: 81% of Democrats, 54% of independents support; even homeowners favor it 54–77% range across demographics, with majorities across all major regions

**Structural factors favoring Yes:**
- **Zero organized opposition**: No argument against was submitted for the official voter guide, and no opposition committee has registered with the Secretary of State
- **$15.3 million raised in support** (top donors: Airbnb, carpenters' unions, nonprofit housing developers), backed by US Vets, Habitat for Humanity, CA Federation of Teachers, Gov. Newsom, and the Democratic gubernatorial nominee
- **Historical precedent**: Since 2000, California voters have passed 23 of 26 statewide bond measures (88%). Similar housing bonds passed in 2018 (Prop 1: 56.2%) and 2024 (Prop 1: 50.2%)
- The measure leads with a 22-point margin and has trended upward late in the campaign, with affordability/housing ranked the top issue on the ballot

**Factors favoring No (much weaker):**
- Republican opposition (79% opposed) and some fiscal-conservative editorial skepticism (e.g., George Skelton in the LA Times), but California's electorate is heavily Democratic, and Republicans' usual weakness in midterms limits this

The Yes side holds a large, stable-to-growing double-digit lead with no funded opposition campaign — this is about as lopsided as California ballot contests get.
