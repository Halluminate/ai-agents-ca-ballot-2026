Based on my research, this page covers California's November 2026 Proposition 4 — the measure repealing the state's ban on public funding of election campaigns (SB 42, the "California Fair Elections Act of 2026").

**Key findings:**

1. **Polling consistently shows Yes trailing:** PPIC's October 2026 survey (Oct 16–22) found 45% yes vs. 52% no among likely voters; their September survey showed 42% yes vs. 53% no. Berkeley IGS's late-October poll showed a 41%–41% tie with 18% undecided — and pollster Mark DiCamillo noted undecided voters tend to break against measures late. Even in the Bay Area and LA, support is below 50%.

2. **Historical precedent is strongly against it:** California voters twice rejected nearly identical measures — Proposition 89 (2006) and Proposition 15 (2010), which lifted the same 1988 ban but lost 57.3% to 42.7%.

3. **Prediction markets heavily favor failure:** Kalshi showed ~19% and Polymarket ~22–27% chance of passage just before Election Day.

4. **Weak campaign resources:** The Yes side raised under $1 million with no major advertising, while major editorial boards (SF Chronicle, OC Register, Sacramento Bee) recommended No, and even Democratic-aligned independents lean against it.

The "No" side is much more likely to win.
