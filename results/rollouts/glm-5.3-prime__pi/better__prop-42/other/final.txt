Based on my extensive research, here's what I found:

**What Prop 42 actually does:**
- Adds a constitutional amendment ("Retirement and Personal Savings Protection Act") barring any new state tax on the *ownership* of personal property — defined extremely broadly to include **all** personal property: financial assets, business interests, intellectual property, digital assets, and retirement accounts — and bans retroactive taxes.
- Contains a "conflicting measures" clause designed to **nullify Prop 40** (the billionaire wealth tax) if Prop 42 gets more yes votes.

**Key findings from research:**
- It was placed on the ballot via ~$100M+ from billionaires led by Google co-founder Sergey Brin (plus Eric Schmidt, John Doerr, Vinod Khosla), who would personally pay the Prop 40 tax — the LA Times called it one of the most expensive ballot campaigns in state history.
- The "protect your retirement savings" framing is a scare tactic: the Legislative Analyst's Office confirms Prop 40 **explicitly excludes real estate, pensions, and retirement accounts** from its billionaire tax, and no serious, realistic proposal exists to tax ordinary Californians' 401(k)s (which would require a 2/3 legislative vote and be politically suicidal).
- The measure's broad language would permanently ban *any* state wealth tax — even narrowly targeted ones — embedding restrictions in the Constitution that only future voters could undo, while constraining state revenue options during federal healthcare funding cuts.
- Editorial and civic opposition is nearly unanimous: SF Chronicle ("poison-pill measure... California isn't coming for your retirement"), Sacramento Bee/McClatchy ("the 401(k) decoy"), Mercury News/East Bay Times ("needlessly and recklessly narrows" the state's options), SPUR, MALDEF, Bay Area Reporter, and others all recommend No. Even boards that oppose Prop 40 itself also oppose Prop 42 as overreach. The main endorsements for Yes come from the measure's billionaire-funded campaign and Gov. Newsom's endorsement statement.

The measure solves a problem that doesn't exist (taxing retirement accounts) while permanently foreclosing a tax option solely to benefit the ~200 billionaires funding it — a "poison pill" with misleading marketing.
