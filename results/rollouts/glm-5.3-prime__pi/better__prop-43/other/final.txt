I've researched this thoroughly — the official voter guide, the Legislative Analyst's analysis, the ballot arguments, legal background (the 2017 *Upland* decision and Prop 13/218 history), editorial positions, and polling. Key considerations that shaped my decision: Prop 43 would permanently entrench a two-thirds supermajority threshold that lets ~33% of voters block what a clear majority (even 62–65%) supports — measures that have recently funded wildfire response, fire protection, homelessness services, and housing. It doesn't cut anyone's taxes, the initiative's own legislative author (Buffy Wicks) only placed it on the ballot as a bargaining chip and urges a No vote, and it doubles down on the minority-veto provisions of Prop 13 that have already distorted California's tax system and constrained essential local services.
