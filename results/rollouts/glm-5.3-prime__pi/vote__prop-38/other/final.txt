# Vote No on Proposition 38

After extensive research — the official Legislative Analyst's analysis, CalMatters' investigation, STAT News reporting, the League of Women Voters' review, editorial positions, and both campaigns' arguments — my recommendation is:

## NO

**Why:** While the goal (funding immunology research to fight cancer, Alzheimer's, and heart disease, especially amid federal NIH cuts) is genuinely worthy, the measure's design has serious problems:

1. **It's effectively a self-dealing earmark.** Half the money — $4.2 billion — goes to a single unnamed institute whose criteria (UC affiliation with 35,000+ inpatient admissions, 200,000+ sq. ft. facility, $250M in donations, $50M microbiome gift, etc.) are so tailored that **UCLA's own spokesperson confirmed only one entity qualifies**: the California Institute for Immunology & Immunotherapy, co-founded by billionaire Gary Michelson — the same man who wrote Prop 38 and has put ~$40 million into the campaign for it. That institute is not yet open and reportedly had as few as three employees and no confirmed researchers as of recent months.

2. **The "pays for itself" claim is speculative.** The precedent is poor: the 2004 stem-cell bond (Prop 71) produced only $15.6 million in royalties on $3 billion by 2022. The Legislative Analyst says revenue timing and amounts are unknown and could take decades.

3. **Bonds are the wrong financing tool for research.** This commits $500–600 million/year from the General Fund (competing with schools, Medi-Cal, and other priorities) for ~20 years — at a 10% premium over paying upfront — for ongoing scientific spending rather than long-lived infrastructure.

4. **Unusually broad opposition.** The League of Women Voters and editorial boards across the political spectrum — San Francisco Chronicle, Sacramento Bee/McClatchy papers, San Diego Union-Tribune, Mercury News, Orange County Register, Press Democrat — all urge No, calling it "ballot-box budgeting" that locks billions into one field regardless of how science and public health needs evolve. Even the leading immunology society was internally divided on it.

A "No" vote isn't a vote against medical research — it's a vote against a flawed, billionaire-backed mechanism for funding it. California can and should support this research through the normal budget and legislative process with genuine competition.
