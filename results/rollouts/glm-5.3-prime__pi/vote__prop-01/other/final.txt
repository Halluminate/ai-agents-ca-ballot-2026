**Yes.**

*Why (briefly):* This Prop 1 (Nov. 2026, the $11.25B Veterans and Affordable Housing Bond) addresses a documented gap — nearly 40,000 affordable units are permitted and partially financed but stalled for lack of state subsidy, and each state dollar leverages roughly $3.60 in federal, local, and private funds. The repayment cost ($500–600M/year, ~0.25% of the General Fund) is modest for the scale of the problem, the veterans' portion is self-repaying, and even the state Republican Party is neutral with literally zero dollars raised against it — no official opposition argument was even submitted. The main criticisms (it adds debt without fixing construction-cost root causes) are real but don't outweigh getting shovel-ready homes built in a state with 180,000+ homeless residents.
