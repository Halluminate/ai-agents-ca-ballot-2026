**California Proposition 5 (Nov 3, 2026 ballot)** — a legislative constitutional amendment that would change the recall election process by removing the "successor/replacement" question from recall ballots, filling vacancies instead via special election or appointment.

**Key evidence:**

1. **Polling consistently shows the "No" side ahead:**
   - PPIC September 2026: 35% Yes / 59% No — the weakest position of *any* measure on the ballot, with opposition crossing party lines
   - PPIC October 2026 (final poll, Oct 16–22): 45% Yes / 52% No — improved, but still trailing by 7 points with only 3% undecided. Only 56% of Democrats, 28% of Republicans, and 40% of independents supported it.

2. **Every major newspaper editorial board opposed it:** SF Chronicle ("a flaw so bizarre even its author doesn't like it"), Mercury News/East Bay Times, San Diego Union-Tribune, Press Enterprise, Santa Cruz Sentinel — all urging rejection.

3. **The measure is "orphaned":** Politico reported no campaign contributions on either side, and the measure's own author, former Sen. Josh Newman, said he was "not optimistic" about its chances.

4. **Historical pattern:** California ballot measures trailing in final polls with under 50% support almost always fail, since undecided voters tend to break toward "No." The 2024 Prop 5 (a different measure) also failed despite late polling.

Since the ballot language asks voters to vote YES (change the recall process) or NO (keep the current process), and the "No" side leads in all polls with broad editorial opposition, the "No" side is more likely to win.
