*Prediction markets (Nov 2–3):* Kalshi ~56–57%, Polymarket ~54% → **~55% to pass**; separate market prices "Prop 42 gets more yes votes than Prop 40" at 63–67%, and "Prop 40 takes effect" at only 13%.

*Advantages for the Yes side:* overwhelming money ($159M raised vs. $33M, ~$65M spent on Prop 42 in October alone); Gov. Newsom's Oct 9 endorsement; a broad blue-collar coalition (CA Professional Firefighters, Building & Construction Trades, PORAC, AMVETS, Small Business Assn., CPA society, CA GOP); a simple, resonant "protect your retirement savings" message that ads keep deliberately decoupled from the billionaire tax; a midterm electorate skewed older (55+ favored it 61–34). PPIC — the probability-panel poll with almost no undecideds — is the only late survey putting it above 50%, and it outpolls Prop 40 in every final survey.

*Risks:* a clear negative trend (IGS "No" jumped 34→45); relentless media/editorial framing as a "billionaire-funded trick" (Chronicle, Merc News, Sac Bee, LA Times all "No"); the Democratic Party, SEIU-UHW and Bernie Sanders opposing; and 14-measure ballot confusion that typically penalizes complex initiatives.

*Net assessment:* The evidence points to a near coin-flip that leans toward passage — the market consensus (~55%), the majority-Yes PPIC reading, the 5-to-1 closing-week spending edge, the governor's endorsement and the older midterm electorate together outweigh the negative trend and hostile editorial environment. The affirmative side is modestly more likely to prevail.
