**No.**

**What my research found (Nov. 3, 2026 ballot):**

- **It fixes a non‑problem.** Prop 42 is sold as protecting 401(k)s, pensions and life savings, but California taxes retirement money only when withdrawn (as it has for decades), and there is no active proposal to tax the *ownership* of those accounts. Its real target, Prop 40, **explicitly exempts** real estate, pensions and retirement accounts (only Roth IRAs over $10M are included). The five bills the Yes campaign cites (AB 2088, AB 310, ACA 8, AB 259, ACA 3) all had thresholds of $15M–$1B and none passed. Prop 42 also carves out real property, so it doesn't even protect home equity, contrary to some Yes ads.
- **The real cost is permanent and broad.** It writes into the Constitution a ban on *any* new state tax on the ownership or control of *all* personal property, tangible and intangible — stocks, business interests, intellectual property, digital assets, personal belongings — at any wealth level, plus a near‑total ban on retroactive taxes (only a 365‑day emergency exception). Undoing it requires another constitutional amendment. That locks away options California may need later (e.g., Vehicle License Fee reform, new local personal‑property taxes, loophole closures effective on introduction, Medi‑Cal provider‑fee renewals that reach back to pending periods).
- **Hidden mechanism.** Its "conflicting measures" clause voids Prop 40 **in its entirety** if Prop 42 gets more yes votes — never disclosed in the ballot title or its ads. Tax scholars (Gamage/Galle/Shanske) also warn the sloppy drafting invites litigation that could be aimed at Prop 3's millionaire tax and capital‑gains revenue.
- **Follow the money.** ~$128M to the Yes committee, essentially all from "Building a Better California" ($333M total), funded by roughly ten billionaires who would owe Prop 40 — Brin ($102M), Schmidt ($40M), Doerr ($40M), Collison ($33M), Khosla, Larsen, Moritz.
- **You don't need it to reject the wealth tax.** Just vote no on Prop 40 (now polling 46–42% yes, 50–52% no). Boards that *oppose* Prop 40 — Sac Bee, Mercury News/East Bay Times, Press Democrat, McClatchy — still say no on 42. The League of Women Voters is **neutral** on Prop 40 but opposes 42; SPUR, the CA Democratic Party, and the SF Chronicle also oppose it. Yes comes from the SD Union‑Tribune, OC Register, Howard Jarvis Assn., Chamber, and Newsom.

**Vote: No.**
