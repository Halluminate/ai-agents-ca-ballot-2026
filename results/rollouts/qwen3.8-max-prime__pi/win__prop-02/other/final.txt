**Research summary — California Proposition 2 (Nov. 3, 2026): "Increases State's Rainy Day Fund" (ACA 20, the "Save for California's Future Act")**

**Polling (all showing a decisive Yes lead):**
- PPIC Sept 2026 (likely voters, after reading title/summary): **60% yes / 39% no**
- PPIC Oct 16–22, 2026: **61% yes / 38% no / 1% DK** — support *rose* slightly, described by PPIC as one of the strongest results on the ballot
- Cross-partisan: Democrats 85% yes, independents 57% yes, Republicans 23% yes. Majorities in every region and every age, gender, education, income, homeownership and racial/ethnic group
- Berkeley IGS did not even include it in its final Oct 29 poll of contested measures
- Only 1% of likely voters named it the proposition they're most interested in (low salience, no controversy)

**Structural advantages for Yes:**
- Legislatively referred constitutional amendment — passed on a **bipartisan supermajority**: Assembly 59–8, Senate 29–2. Needs only a simple majority
- **"Opponents: None submitted"** — no official rebuttal argument filed with the Secretary of State
- **Zero money on either side**: SOS Quick Guide reports *no contributions* to Yes on 2 **or** No on 2 committees. There is no funded opposition campaign, no ads, no mail
- Endorsed by Gov. Newsom, Democratic gubernatorial nominee Xavier Becerra, CA Professional Firefighters, LA Chamber of Commerce, California State PTA, Consumer Watchdog, hospitals, health clinics, educators, plus business/labor/tech/developer groups
- Opposition is limited to the CA Republican Party, Reform California/Carl DeMaio and two GOP assemblymembers — with no resources in a state where Democrats outnumber Republicans roughly 2-to-1

**Historical precedent:**
- The 2014 Prop 2 that created this exact rainy-day fund passed with **69.1%**, winning 57 of 58 counties
- The 2024 Prop 2 (school bond) passed with 57%
- Unopposed, low-salience legislative amendments with a positive "fiscal responsibility/savings" framing are among the most reliable passes in California

**Risks to Yes:** Republican opposition is intense (~77% no); midterm electorates skew older and more conservative; some critical press (LA Times, OC Register) on the Gann Limit/rebate loophole; 14 measures may cause ballot fatigue. But fatigue produces undervotes, not No votes, and the measure would need an ~11-point shift against it with no campaign infrastructure to deliver that shift — while polling trended *upward*, not down (contrast Prop 40, which collapsed).

The Yes side is overwhelmingly more likely to win.
