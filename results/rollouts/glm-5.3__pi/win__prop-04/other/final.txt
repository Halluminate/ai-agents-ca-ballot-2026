I have completed extensive research on California Proposition 4 (November 3, 2026 ballot — repeals the prohibition on public funding of election campaigns). Here's a summary of what I found:

**The Measure:** Prop 4 would lift California's 1988 (Prop 73) ban on public campaign financing, letting state/local governments create taxpayer-funded campaign programs. Placed on the ballot by the Democratic legislature.

**Polling evidence (Yes consistently below 50%):**
- PPIC September: Yes 42% / No 53%; PPIC October (final): Yes 45% / No 52%
- Berkeley IGS late October: tied 41%–41% with 18% undecided; NPP/independent voters break No (46–34); Republicans overwhelmingly No (81–9); Democrats support only ~60–62%
- IGS pollster Mark DiCamillo: "It's close, but it's still below 50%. Most voters tend to vote no in the late going if they're not convinced" — calling weak independent support "a signal of potential failure"
- Support exceeds 50% in only one region (SF Bay Area, 51%)

**Historical precedent (heavily against):**
- 1988: The public-financing ban (Prop 73, 58%) outpolled the public-financing alternative (Prop 68)
- 2000: Measure allowing public funds for candidate advertising rejected by ~two-thirds
- 2010: Prop 15, an essentially identical "California Fair Elections Act," failed 57%–43%

**Campaign environment:**
- Yes side raised only ~$1M (modest, no real statewide ad campaign); No side raised $0 but doesn't need money — "No" is the default
- Major editorial boards (SF Chronicle, San Diego Union-Tribune, OC Register, LA Daily News, Press Democrat, Modesto Bee) oppose; only a few (Sacramento Bee, LA Times' Skelton) support
- PPIC finds Californians in a "historically sour mood" — hostile terrain for taxpayer-funded campaign measures

**Conclusion:** Every data point — both PPIC polls showing No ahead, undecided voters breaking toward No, weak Democratic support that cannot offset overwhelming Republican and independent opposition, a long track record of California voters rejecting public campaign financing, minimal campaign resources, and hostile editorial coverage — indicates the "No" side is more likely to win.
