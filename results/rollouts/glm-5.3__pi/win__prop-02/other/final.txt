I now have comprehensive evidence from polling, historical precedent, campaign finance, and endorsements. Here's my assessment:

**Measure:** Prop 2 (Nov. 3, 2026, California) — "Increases State's Rainy Day Fund. Legislative Constitutional Amendment." Doubles the cap on the Budget Stabilization Account from 10% to 20% of General Fund taxes, extends required debt payments to 2040, and exempts reserve deposits from the Gann spending limit.

**Key evidence on which side is more likely to win:**

1. **Polling:** The final PPIC survey (Oct. 16–22, 2026, likely voters, ±3.8% MoE) shows **61% Yes vs. 38% No**, stable from September (60–39). Support is at or above majority across *all* major regions and across age, gender, education, homeownership, income, and racial/ethnic groups (85% of Democrats, 57% of independents, 23% of Republicans).

2. **Historical precedent:** The nearly identical 2014 Proposition 2 (the original rainy day fund amendment) passed with **69% of the vote**, carrying 57 of 58 counties.

3. **No organized opposition:** The official ballot label lists "Opponents: None submitted." No campaign committee has registered on either side — there's essentially no funded opposition. Only one Republican assemblymember (David Tangipa) signed the rebuttal argument.

4. **Broad, bipartisan-flavored support:** Passed the Legislature nearly unanimously (Assembly 59–8, Senate 29–2, including one GOP vote); backed by Gov. Newsom, gubernatorial frontrunner Xavier Becerra (who leads 61–37 in the same poll), firefighters, the LA Chamber of Commerce, hospitals, the California State PTA, SPUR, and major editorial boards (Sac Bee, SF Chronicle).

5. **Favorable framing:** "Rainy Day Fund" and fiscal prudence are popular concepts; the ballot label emphasizes protecting schools, healthcare, and public safety.

The only headwinds are Republican/Reform California opposition and the taxpayer-rebate/Gann Limit argument, but with a 23-point polling lead, no opposition money, and a Democratic-leaning midterm electorate, the Yes side is heavily favored.
