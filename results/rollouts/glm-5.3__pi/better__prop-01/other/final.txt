Based on my extensive research, I've weighed the following:

**What Prop 1 does (Nov. 2026):** Authorizes $11.25 billion in state bonds for housing — $7.2B for affordable multifamily rental housing, $1.25B for the self-sustaining CalVet home loan program (repaid by veterans themselves), $1.1B for homeownership assistance, plus funding for farmworker, student, tribal, and infrastructure needs. Cost: $500–600M annually for ~25 years, about 0.25% of the General Fund.

**Case for yes:** California faces a documented crisis (180,000+ homeless, over half of renters cost-burdened, only ~22% can afford a median home). State housing funds have run dry — an estimated 40,000–46,000 already-approved affordable homes are stalled awaiting funding, which this bond directly unblocks. It unlocks federal matching funds, includes no new taxes, the veterans program has historically cost taxpayers nothing, and recent streamlining laws make the money go further. Support is overwhelming and bipartisan in practice: Gov. Newsom, veterans groups, teachers, labor, Habitat for Humanity, League of Women Voters, apartment association, UC/CSU, cities and counties — with PPIC polling showing ~60% support.

**Case for no:** Adds debt amid a structural budget deficit (state already pays ~$6B/year on bonds); doesn't fix root causes like high construction costs and regulation; interest adds ~15% to the total; and 40,000 units is a fraction of the overall need. Opponents are a handful of Republican lawmakers and some editorial boards (OC Register, SD Union-Tribune, Mercury News, Skelton of the LA Times).

**Weighing it:** The "no" arguments are largely philosophical objections to debt, while the "yes" case addresses a concrete, urgent funding gap with shovel-ready projects, a modest relative cost, accountability requirements, and an unusually broad coalition — with literally no organized opposition campaign or even a submitted ballot argument against it.
