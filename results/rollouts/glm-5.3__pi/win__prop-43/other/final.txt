Based on my extensive research, I have a clear picture:

**Proposition 43 (Nov. 3, 2026, California)** would raise the vote threshold for citizen-initiated local special taxes from a simple majority to two-thirds. It was placed on the ballot by the Legislature (ACA 22, Wicks) as a compromise to get the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association to withdraw a broader initiative.

**Key evidence on which side is more likely to win:**

- **Polling — every public poll shows the "No" side leading:**
  - PPIC final poll (Oct 16–22): **42% Yes / 55% No** (only 3% undecided; No's lead grew from 51% in September)
  - Berkeley IGS (Oct 19–25): **36% Yes / 46% No** (up from 31–40 in September)
  - Support is below 50% in every region, with only 28% of Democrats in favor — fatal in a deep-blue midterm electorate (Becerra leads the governor's race 61–37 in the same poll)

- **Opposition coalition:** Governor Newsom (announced opposition Oct. 12), the California Democratic Party, League of California Cities, County associations, 50+ mayors, 100+ local governments, firefighters/teachers/nurses/SEIU unions, and even the measure's own author, Assemblymember Buffy Wicks, who publicly campaigns against it

- **Support:** mainly HJTA, CalTax, and the Republican Party — a distinct minority coalition in California

- **Funding:** The Yes side's headline $16–17M is mostly pre-deal signature-gathering money; the No side has $9M+ of active union money and a decisive polling trend in its favor

The "Yes" side (Prop 43 passing) trails by 9–13 points in the final polls with virtually no undecided voters left, against a unified Democratic/local-government opposition in a Democratic-leaning midterm electorate.
