Based on my research, here's what I found about California Proposition 42 (November 3, 2026 election):

**The measure:** A constitutional amendment prohibiting new state taxes on personal property (retirement accounts, savings, financial assets) and most retroactive taxes — designed as a "poison pill" countermeasure to Prop 40, the billionaire wealth tax.

**Key evidence on which side is more likely to win:**

1. **Polling (essentially tied, slight Yes edge):** IGS Aug: Yes +3; PPIC Sept: Yes +11; IGS Sept: Yes +9; PPIC Oct: Yes +4 (51–47); IGS Oct (final poll): No +2 (43–45). Support has eroded late but Yes led in 5 of 6 polls.

2. **Prediction markets (as of election day):** Kalshi ~56% Yes, Polymarket ~54.5% Yes, TruthIndex 56% — all lean toward the measure passing.

3. **Forecast model (On Point Politics, Nov 2):** Yes at 50.7% projected vote, **57% chance to pass** — "toss up, Yes slightly favored."

4. **Campaign finance:** Yes side raised ~$128–159 million (Building a Better California, funded by billionaires like Sergey Brin and John Doerr) vs. only ~$23–35 million for the No side (SEIU-UHW) — a 4–6x spending advantage.

5. **Endorsements:** Yes has Gov. Gavin Newsom, the California Republican Party, firefighters, building trades, and veterans groups — an unusual bipartisan coalition. No has Bernie Sanders and SEIU-UHW.

6. **Structural factors:** Tax-prohibition measures framed as "protecting retirement savings" have historically performed well with California voters, and Prop 42 consistently outpolls both Prop 40 and Prop 41 in head-to-head polling.

The prediction markets, the statistical forecast, the massive spending advantage, the powerful framing, and the broad endorsement coalition all point modestly in the same direction, despite the final IGS poll showing a narrow No lead.

**Answer: yes**
