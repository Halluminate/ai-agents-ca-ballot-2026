A similar number-matching rule in Texas rejected about 12% of mail ballots in its first election and roughly 3% in later general elections, versus about 1% before. Prop 39 doesn't spell out a notice or fix-it step for mismatched numbers. Fraud of the kind it targets is very rare in California, and because this is a constitutional amendment, changing its core rules later would take another statewide vote. It's a judgment call, though. If you weigh ID requirements and the objectivity of a number match more heavily than the rejection risk, voting yes is defensible.
