**Yes**, narrowly. It's a close call and partly a values question.

- **Current rules can seat a minority winner.** In 2018, Newman's replacement won with 31.8% of the votes on the recall question, while 41.9% voted to keep him. In 2003, Schwarzenegger won with 46.8% of that vote. Prop 5 would instead fill a recall vacancy through a special election that needs a majority (with a runoff if no one gets one), through succession by the statewide-elected lieutenant governor (for a recalled governor), or through the governor's appointment, which needs legislative confirmation or takes effect automatically after 90 days.
- **The costs and gaps are real but modest.** A recalled legislator's seat would typically stay empty four to five months unless the election is consolidated with another one, and the recalled official could run again in that special election. The Legislative Analyst calls the net cost unknown. Prop 5 also doesn't change what the recall election itself costs.
- **Vote No if** you want the replacement chosen on the same ballot, or you find the vacancy gap or the rerun provision disqualifying. The title ("Statewide Officers") also understates the scope, since legislators are covered too.

The latest PPIC poll (Oct. 16–22) had it at 45% yes and 52% no. The campaign split is largely partisan, and polls close at 8 p.m. on Nov. 3.
