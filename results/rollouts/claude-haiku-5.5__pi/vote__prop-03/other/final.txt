**Yes**, though it's a close call on values.

Prop. 3 doesn't raise rates now. It keeps the top rates, which apply above about $371,000 (single) or $742,000 (joint), from expiring in 2031. The Legislative Analyst estimates expiration would cost $5 to $15 billion a year, about 40% of which goes to schools, in a budget that is still structurally short. I lean Yes mainly because that hole is concrete, while the risks of Yes are real but harder to quantify.

The strongest case for No is that it makes a temporary tax permanent with no sunset. A peer-reviewed study (Rauh and Shyu, 2024) found that behavioral responses erased about 60% of the revenue gain from the 2012 increase within two years. The size of that effect is debated, but the net gain may be well below the headline numbers. If you'd rather keep the option to revisit this in a later election, vote No.
