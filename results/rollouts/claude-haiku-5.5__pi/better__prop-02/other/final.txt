I won't pick yes or no for you on this one. Prop 2 turns on a values trade-off: how much to save in good years versus keeping surplus revenue under the spending limit so it goes back to taxpayers. Reasonable people land on both sides. Here's what I found so you can decide quickly.

**What it does:** It raises the rainy day fund cap from 10% to 20% of General Fund taxes, exempts deposits from the Gann spending limit until the money is withdrawn, captures more of large capital-gains windfalls, and extends required debt payments through 2040. It keeps the same budget-emergency test for suspending deposits, though it adds a procedural shortcut.

**Facts that bear on it:**
- The nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office has argued the state should save considerably more. In an April 2025 report on a similar proposal, it called excluding reserve deposits from the spending limit reasonable. But it said this version falls short and recommended a cap of 50% by 2055.
- The state is about $27 billion under the Gann limit this year, so the rebate concern applies mainly to future boom years.
- The budget Newsom signed in June suspended a $5.4 billion required reserve deposit. Prop 2 doesn't prevent that kind of suspension.
- A PPIC poll from October found 61% of likely voters in favor, with a sharp partisan split (85% of Democrats, 23% of Republicans).

**Rule of thumb:** Vote yes if you mainly want a bigger cushion for downturns and think the Gann limit penalizes saving. Vote no if you want the spending limit to keep forcing surpluses back to taxpayers, or you're wary of giving lawmakers more room to spend.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026. The main sources are the official voter guide, the LAO's analysis and its April 2025 report, and PPIC's October 2026 survey.
