I'm not going to pick yes or no for you on this one. It turns on a values question, how much debt to take on for subsidized housing, and informed people split on it. The LAO takes no position, and the editorial boards are divided. I checked the official guide, the LAO analysis, the bill record, recent polls, the Treasurer's debt report, and editorials from both sides. Most of the facts are not in dispute, so the real question is how to weigh them. Here's the test:

- **Vote YES** if you think subsidized housing is worth roughly $500–600 million a year from the state budget for about 25 years.
- **Vote NO** if you think the budget can't carry that debt right now, or that the money would go further after cost reforms.

**What's not disputed**
- The measure authorizes $11.25 billion in bonds. The $10 billion portion is repaid from the General Fund at $500–600 million a year for about 25 years, roughly 0.25% of the budget. The $1.25 billion for veterans' home loans is repaid by the veterans.
- Borrowing costs about 15% more, after inflation, than paying cash, according to the LAO.
- The LAO estimates up to 40,000 rental units and help for up to 40,000 homebuyers.
- It passed 29–2 in the Senate and 61–7 in the Assembly. The official guide prints no argument against it, and no opposition campaign has reported any money.

**Strongest case for Yes**
- Nearly 40,000 approved affordable units are stalled for lack of final financing, according to Enterprise (via CalMatters).
- The state money pairs with federal tax credits that Congress expanded in 2025. Units generally must stay affordable for 55 years.
- The Treasurer says the state's debt indicators remain within historical norms.
- In PPIC's October poll, 60% of likely voters said yes. Newsom, the Democratic Party, labor, and the Chronicle and Sacramento Bee editorial boards support it.

**Strongest case for No**
- It locks in General Fund debt for decades. The LAO calls the budget structurally imbalanced and "overextended." The Treasurer warns that debt growing faster than the budget erodes the state's credit strengths. Revenues are running high on AI-driven stock gains, which the LAO says may not last.
- Subsidized housing in California costs about 1.5 times market-rate housing (RAND), and the bond doesn't address that. Even the Sacramento Bee, which endorsed it, said proponents couldn't readily show statewide results from the 2018 housing bond.
- Only about 11% goes to veterans, which critics call misleading.
- The Mercury News/East Bay Times, OC Register, and San Diego Union-Tribune editorial boards oppose it, as does LA Times columnist George Skelton. If reform is your priority, Prop 45 on the same ballot would speed environmental review.
