**No.**

It's a values call, but the evidence on this measure leans toward no. A yes locks in constitutional changes and a legally contested tax for revenue that is uncertain and temporary.

- **Revenue is very uncertain.** Backers estimate about $100 billion. The nonpartisan LAO says "tens of billions." Hoover Institution economists estimate about $30 billion after departures and a litigation discount, and a net loss over time. Most of the gap comes from assumptions about court outcomes and departures.
- **It's a bridge, not a fix.** The measure's own findings put federal Medi-Cal losses at up to $19 billion a year, and state budget deficits are projected at $20–35 billion a year. At $30–100 billion total, the money covers roughly 1.5 to 5 years of federal losses.
- **It writes exemptions into the constitution.** The revenue is exempt from the school funding guarantee (Prop 98), the spending limit, and rainy-day rules. The nonpartisan Budget Center says schools would get less than if the money were ordinary General Fund revenue. Undoing this would take another statewide vote. The Legislature can also amend the statute by a two-thirds vote if the change is "consistent with and furthers" the measure's purposes.
- **Legal and migration risks are real.** The Tax Foundation calls the retroactive residency date fatally flawed. The drafters say facial challenges are weak. Either way, expect years of litigation. The LAO puts lost income tax from departures under $1 billion a year; Hoover puts it at $1–3.5 billion.

If you vote yes, note two things. Props 41 and 42 each cancel Prop 40 if they get more yes votes than it does. In PPIC's late-October poll, Prop 42 led Prop 40, 51% to 46% yes. Prop 40 also trailed in late polls (PPIC 46–52, Berkeley IGS 42–50).

Vote yes instead if you think the Medi-Cal gap is urgent enough to justify this risk. The federal cuts are real (the administration projects 1.3 million Medi-Cal disenrollments through 2029–30), and opponents haven't offered a funded replacement. You'd also need to expect courts to uphold most of the tax.

*Based on the official measure text, LAO and Budget Center analyses, PPIC and Berkeley IGS polls, and coverage through Nov. 3, 2026.*
