Whether to vote **yes** or **no** on California Proposition 40 is a value judgment that depends on your priorities regarding tax policy, public health funding, and the state economy, so it cannot be answered with a simple "yes" or "no." Below is an overview of the measure, its nonpartisan fiscal analysis, and the main arguments from both sides to help you make your own decision.

---

### What Proposition 40 Does
Proposition 40 is a constitutional amendment and statute on the November 3, 2026 California ballot:
* **One-Time Wealth Tax:** Imposes a one-time tax of up to 5% on the net worth of individuals and trusts who were California residents as of January 1, 2026, with covered assets exceeding $1 billion (phasing in between $1 billion and $1.1 billion). Covered assets include businesses, securities, art, collectibles, and intellectual property, while generally excluding real estate and certain pensions/retirement accounts.
* **Use of Revenue:** Requires 90% of the revenue to be spent on public health care services and the remaining 10% on education, food assistance, and tax administration.
* **Constitutional Exemptions:** Exempts the revenue from existing state constitutional requirements regarding minimum school funding (Proposition 98), budget reserves, and the state spending limit (Gann Limit).

### Nonpartisan Fiscal Impact (Legislative Analyst's Office)
* **Temporary Revenue Increase:** Likely generates tens of billions of dollars for the state spread over several years (with payments starting in 2027 and an option for taxpayers to pay over five years with an added annual charge).
* **Potential Ongoing Revenue Loss:** Could reduce ongoing annual state income tax revenues by less than $1 billion per year if some billionaires relocate out of California or take other actions that reduce their taxable income, which could reduce General Fund money available for services like schools, health care, and public safety.
* **Administrative Costs:** Tens of millions of dollars per year over several years to administer and collect the tax, paid out of the tax proceeds.

---

### Arguments in Favor (Vote **YES**)
Supporters—including SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West, the California Nurses Association, U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders, and Rep. Ro Khanna—argue:
* **Protects Health Care Funding:** A "yes" vote raises tens of billions of dollars (supporters estimate around $100 billion) to offset federal health care cuts, keep hospitals and emergency rooms open, and preserve coverage and affordability for millions of Californians.
* **Tax Fairness:** Ultra-wealthy individuals hold trillions in unrealized wealth that often goes untaxed, resulting in lower effective tax rates compared to working families who pay taxes on every paycheck.
* **Targeted Scope:** The tax applies only to a small group of roughly 200 California billionaires and helps fund essential medical care, food assistance, and education programs rather than shifting costs onto middle- and working-class taxpayers.

### Arguments Against (Vote **NO**)
Opponents—including the California Teachers Association, California Medical Association, California Taxpayers Association, California Chamber of Commerce, State Building and Construction Trades Council, and Gov. Gavin Newsom—argue:
* **Long-Term Budget & Economic Harm:** A one-time wealth tax provides only temporary money while driving wealthy taxpayers and businesses out of California, permanently shrinking the tax base that funds nearly half of state income taxes and costing the General Fund billions over time.
* **Bypasses School Funding & Spending Protections:** By exempting its revenues from Proposition 98 and the state spending limit, the measure sidesteps constitutional guarantees for public school funding and taxpayer rebates, and lacks strict oversight on how health care funds are distributed.
* **Precedent & Future Expansion Concerns:** Opponents argue the measure sets a precedent of taxing certain retirement accounts and gives the Legislature broad authority to amend provisions of the law without voter approval.

---

### Summary
* Vote **Yes** if you believe California should levy a one-time 5% wealth tax on residents worth over $1 billion to generate dedicated multi-year funding primarily for health care programs.
* Vote **No** if you believe the tax will harm California’s long-term tax base and economy, or you oppose exempting the new revenue from constitutional school-funding and spending-limit rules.
