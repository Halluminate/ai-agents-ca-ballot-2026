Whether to vote **yes** or **no** on California Proposition 43 depends on your priorities regarding taxpayer protections, local government funding, and voter approval thresholds, so it cannot be answered with a single word. 

Here is a breakdown of the measure and the main arguments on both sides from the Official Voter Information Guide to help you make your decision:

### What Proposition 43 Does
Currently, local "special taxes" (taxes raised for a specific purpose) placed on the ballot by local governing boards require a two-thirds vote to pass, while recent court decisions have allowed local special taxes proposed via citizen initiatives to pass with a simple majority (over 50%) vote. 

Beginning January 1, 2027, Proposition 43 would require all new, increased, or extended voter-proposed local special taxes to receive a **two-thirds (66.7%) vote** to pass. According to the Legislative Analyst's Office, this would make it harder to pass certain local taxes, potentially resulting in lower local government revenues in the future than would otherwise occur.

---

### Arguments for Voting YES
A **YES** vote means voter-proposed local special taxes would require two-thirds approval by voters.

* **Restores Proposition 13 Protections:** Supporters argue that recent court rulings created a loophole allowing special interests to bypass Proposition 13’s longstanding two-thirds vote requirement for earmarked local taxes.
* **Curbs Cost-of-Living Increases:** Proponents state that Californians already face the nation's highest taxes and cost of living, and requiring broad two-thirds consensus helps protect homeowners, renters, and businesses from costly new sales, parcel, utility, and real estate transfer taxes.
* **Accountability in Spending:** Supporters contend that the measure ensures tax revenues are spent strictly on the specific purposes voters approved and prevents local politicians and special interests from easily raising taxes.
* **Notable Supporters:** Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, California Taxpayers Association, Family Business Association of California, and California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce.

---

### Arguments for Voting NO
A **NO** vote means voter-proposed local special taxes could continue to be approved by a simple majority vote.

* **Preserves Majority Rule:** Opponents argue that democratic elections should be decided by a majority of voters, whereas a two-thirds requirement allows a minority of voters (33.4%) to veto measures supported by up to 66.6% of the community.
* **Protects Funding for Local Services:** Opponents state that raising the vote threshold will make it much harder for communities to fund essential local priorities—such as fire protection, 911 emergency response, public schools, road repairs, affordable housing, and local healthcare clinics.
* **Benefits Corporate Interests Over Communities:** Critics contend that the measure does not cut existing taxes, but instead makes it easier for wealthy corporate donors and developers to block local funding measures.
* **Notable Opponents:** California Professional Firefighters, California Federation of Teachers, Nurse Alliance of SEIU California, California School Employees Association, and Non-Profit Housing Association of Northern California.

---

### Summary
* Vote **YES** if you believe all local special taxes—including those proposed by voter initiative—should require a two-thirds supermajority vote to protect taxpayers from tax increases.
* Vote **NO** if you believe local voters should be able to pass citizen-initiated special taxes for community services and infrastructure by a simple majority vote.
