I cannot tell you whether to vote "yes" or "no" on a ballot measure, as deciding how to vote depends on your personal priorities and how you weigh the competing impacts of the proposal. Instead, here is an overview of **California Proposition 44** based on the Official Voter Information Guide to help you make your own decision.

---

### What Proposition 44 Would Do
* **90% Spending Requirement:** Requires private nonprofit safety-net clinics—specifically Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs)—to spend at least **90% of their annual revenue** on "program services" that advance their charitable purpose (providing health care and related patient services). This caps administrative and other overhead costs at 10%. According to the Legislative Analyst’s Office (LAO), these clinics currently report spending an average of about 80% of their revenue on health care services.
* **Definitions and Waivers:** Tasks the California Attorney General with defining in detail which expenses count toward the 90% threshold, using existing federal financial reports as a baseline. Clinics could apply for temporary state waivers in certain circumstances.
* **Penalties:** Clinics that fall below the 90% mark must pay a penalty to the state equal to the shortfall. A clinic can recover the penalty if it meets the spending requirement within five years; otherwise, the state keeps the funds to spend on clinic workforce programs.
* **Fiscal Impact:** The LAO estimates state enforcement costs in the **low tens of millions of dollars annually**, paid for by fees charged to affected clinics, along with other uncertain state and local costs depending on how clinics adjust their spending or operations.

---

### The Case for Voting YES
A **YES** vote means private nonprofit safety-net clinics would be required to spend at least 90% of their annual revenue on health care program services.

* **Supporters** (including SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West) argue that the measure holds clinic executives accountable and ensures taxpayer funds are prioritized for direct patient care and clinical support rather than excessive CEO salaries, consultants, and administrative overhead.
* **Protection of Healthcare Dollars:** Proponents emphasize that clinics meeting the standard face no penalties, clinics that fall short have up to five years to come into compliance and get their money back, and any unrecovered penalty funds remain within the healthcare system to recruit and train clinic workers.
* **Transparency:** Supporters argue the measure increases public transparency around how safety-net clinics spend their funding at a time when healthcare budgets face strain.

---

### The Case for Voting NO
A **NO** vote means the new 90% spending requirement and penalty structure would not go into effect.

* **Opponents** (including the California Medical Association, American Academy of Pediatrics California, California Primary Care Association, Planned Parenthood Affiliates of California, and California School Nurses Organization) argue the 10% cap on non-program spending is arbitrary and unworkable.
* **Risk of Clinic Closures and Service Cuts:** Because the average clinic currently spends roughly 80% under existing reporting categories, opponents warn that forcing an immediate jump to 90%—combined with new enforcement fees and penalties—would push many clinics to operate at a loss, cut vital services, or close down, pushing more patients into hospital emergency rooms.
* **Existing Oversight:** Opponents note that FQHCs are already non-profit organizations governed by boards that include patients and are subject to strict federal and state regulations and mandatory audits.

---

### How to Decide
* Vote **YES** if you believe stricter statutory limits on administrative overhead are needed to curb executive pay and direct a higher share of clinic revenue toward program services.
* Vote **NO** if you believe the 90% mandate is too rigid and risks financially destabilizing community clinics or reducing access to care in underserved areas.
