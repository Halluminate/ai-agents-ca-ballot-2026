Whether to vote **yes** or **no** on California Proposition 3 is a value judgment that depends on your views on taxation, state spending, and public funding priorities, so it cannot be answered with a simple "yes" or "no." Below is an overview of the measure and the main arguments on both sides from the Official Voter Information Guide to help you make your own decision.

---

### What Proposition 3 Does
Proposition 3 is an Initiative Constitutional Amendment that would make permanent the higher state personal income tax rates on high-income earners (individuals earning over $371,000 or couples earning over $740,000, adjusted for inflation—roughly the top 2% of California taxpayers). These rates were originally approved as a temporary tax increase under Proposition 30 (2012) and extended under Proposition 55 (2016), and are currently scheduled to expire in 2031.

* **Fiscal Impact:** According to the Legislative Analyst's Office, the measure would maintain between **$5 billion and $15 billion** in annual state revenue after 2030, depending on economic and stock market conditions. Roughly 40% of this revenue would go to K–12 schools and community colleges, with the remainder going to the state General Fund (supporting programs like Medi-Cal) and budget reserves.

---

### Arguments for Voting YES
A **YES** vote means the income tax increase on high-income earners in place since 2012 will become permanent instead of expiring in 2031.

* **Protects Education and Healthcare Funding:** Supporters argue that allowing the tax rates to expire would result in billions of dollars in cuts to public schools, community colleges, and healthcare programs like Medi-Cal, leading to larger class sizes, educator layoffs, and reduced access to care.
* **No New Taxes on Most Californians:** Proponents emphasize that the measure does not raise taxes beyond current rates and only maintains the existing rates paid by the wealthiest 2% of taxpayers for the past 15 years.
* **Accountability Requirements:** Supporters point out that the education funds are constitutionally dedicated to local schools and community colleges, cannot be spent on administrative overhead, and are subject to annual independent audits and public disclosure.
* **Key Supporters:** California Teachers Association, California State PTA, California School Nurses Organization, California Professional Firefighters, and Planned Parenthood Affiliates of California.

---

### Arguments for Voting NO
A **NO** vote means the income tax increase on high-income earners in place since 2012 will expire as scheduled in 2031.

* **Keeps the Promise of "Temporary" Taxes:** Opponents argue that voters approved these higher tax rates in 2012 and 2016 based on explicit promises that they would be temporary, and making them permanent breaks that commitment.
* **High Tax Burden and Cost of Living:** Critics note that Californians already face the highest income, sales, and gas taxes—as well as the highest overall cost of living—in the country.
* **Concerns Over State Spending:** Opponents contend that California has a spending and accountability problem rather than a revenue problem, pointing to rapid growth in the state budget and wasteful spending on state programs. They argue the state should reform how it spends existing money rather than locking in permanently higher taxes.
* **Key Opponents:** California Taxpayers Association, Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, Family Business Association of California, and California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce.
