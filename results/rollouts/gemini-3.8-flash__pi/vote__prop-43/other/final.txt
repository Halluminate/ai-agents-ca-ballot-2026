### What Proposition 43 Does
* **Background:** Under the California Constitution (following Proposition 13), local "special taxes"—taxes dedicated to a specific purpose, such as parks, housing, or transportation—proposed by local governing bodies (city councils, county supervisors) require a two-thirds (66.67%) supermajority to pass. However, subsequent court rulings determined that this two-thirds threshold did not apply to tax measures placed on the ballot by citizens gathering signatures (citizen initiatives), allowing those to pass with a simple majority (over 50%).
* **The Proposal:** Beginning January 1, 2027, Proposition 43 would amend the California Constitution to require a **two-thirds vote** for *all* local special taxes, including those proposed by citizen initiatives. It applies prospectively, meaning taxes already approved before 2027 would remain in effect, but future increases, extensions, or new citizen-proposed special taxes would face the higher threshold.

---

### What Your Vote Means

* **A YES vote means:** Starting in 2027, any local special tax placed on the ballot by citizen petition would require a two-thirds (66.67%) vote to pass, matching the threshold already required for taxes proposed by local city councils and county boards.
* **A NO vote means:** Local special taxes placed on the ballot by citizen petition would continue to require only a simple majority (over 50%) to pass.

---

### Key Arguments in Favor (Vote YES)

Supporters include the **California Taxpayers Association**, the **Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association**, and the **California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce**:

1. **Restores Proposition 13 Protections:** Proponents argue that the initiative process has been used as a "loophole" to circumvent Proposition 13's original intent, allowing special-interest groups and local governments to enact higher sales, utility, and parcel taxes with only simple majority support.
2. **Protects Taxpayers and Homeowners:** Supporters contend that California already has among the highest tax rates and costs of living in the country. Raising the threshold to two-thirds makes it harder to pass costly new local real estate transfer taxes, parcel taxes, and sales taxes.
3. **Encourages Broad Consensus:** Proponents believe that dedicated public tax increases should require broad community support (two-thirds) rather than a narrow majority.

---

### Key Arguments Against (Vote NO)

Opponents include the **California Professional Firefighters**, the **California Federation of Teachers**, the **Nurse Alliance of SEIU California**, and **Assemblymember Buffy Wicks** (the measure's legislative author, who introduced ACA 22 strictly as a compromise to remove an even more restrictive retroactive initiative):

1. **Protects Majority Rule:** Opponents argue that requiring a two-thirds supermajority allows a 33.4% minority to override the will of nearly 67% of voters, giving a small fraction of the electorate veto power over local community decisions.
2. **Safeguards Essential Public Services:** Citizen initiatives are frequently used by local communities to fund critical services—such as 911 dispatch, fire protection, emergency medical care, road repairs, public schools, and affordable housing. Opponents argue this higher barrier makes it much harder for local communities to address urgent funding needs.
3. **Maintains Local Citizen Initiative Power:** Opponents emphasize that the citizen initiative is a fundamental constitutional right reserved to voters to bypass legislative inaction or address local problems directly.
