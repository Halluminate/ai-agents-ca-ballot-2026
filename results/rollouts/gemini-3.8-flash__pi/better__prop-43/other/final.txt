### What Proposition 43 Does
* **Current Law:** Under the California Constitution (stemming from Proposition 13 and subsequent amendments), local special taxes—taxes dedicated to a specific public purpose—require a two-thirds (66.7%) supermajority vote when placed on the ballot by local governing bodies (such as city councils or county boards of supervisors). However, following California court rulings interpreting the 2017 *California Cannabis Coalition v. City of Upland* decision, local special taxes placed on the ballot via citizen initiative petitions currently require only a simple majority (over 50%) to pass.
* **The Measure (ACA 22):** Beginning January 1, 2027, Proposition 43 would raise the voter approval threshold for citizen-initiated local special taxes from a simple majority to a two-thirds supermajority, aligning them with the threshold required for taxes proposed by local governing boards. It would apply prospectively and would not invalidate previously approved measures.

---

### Arguments in Favor of Voting **YES**

* **Restoring Taxpayer Protections:** Proponents argue that court decisions created an unintended loophole in Proposition 13 that allows special-interest groups and local officials to circumvent two-thirds voter approval simply by qualifying measures through citizen petitions. A "Yes" vote closes this loophole and restores equal standards for all special taxes.
* **Cost of Living Relief:** Supporters contend that Californians already face among the highest tax burdens and housing costs in the nation, and that making local taxes harder to pass helps protect homeowners, renters, and small businesses from parcel taxes, sales taxes, and real estate transfer taxes.
* **Broad Consensus Requirement:** Backers argue that dedicated tax increases should require broad community agreement (a two-thirds consensus) rather than a narrow majority before public funds are earmarked and taxes are raised.
* **Key Supporters:** Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, California Taxpayers Association, Family Business Association of California, California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce, and California Small Business Association.

---

### Arguments in Favor of Voting **NO**

* **Protecting Majority Rule:** Opponents argue that requiring a two-thirds vote allows a minority of voters (33.4%) to veto the will of the majority (up to 66.6%), which they view as fundamentally undemocratic and an impediment to local democracy.
* **Funding Critical Public Services:** Opponents emphasize that local communities rely on citizen-led ballot measures to fund essential services—including fire protection, 911 emergency response, wildfire prevention, neighborhood schools, road repairs, public healthcare facilities, and affordable housing. Raising the threshold would make securing this funding much more difficult.
* **Grassroots Solutions:** Opponents contend that citizen initiatives are often the only effective avenue for communities to address urgent local needs when governing bodies cannot or will not act, and that this measure weakens citizens' ability to solve local problems.
* **Key Opponents:** California Professional Firefighters, California Federation of Teachers, Nurse Alliance of SEIU California, California School Employees Association, Californians Against Waste, Non-Profit Housing Association of Northern California, and Governor Gavin Newsom.
