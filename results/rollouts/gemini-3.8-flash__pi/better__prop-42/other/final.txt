Whether it is better to vote **yes** or **no** on Proposition 42 is a subjective decision for each voter, as there is no objective or consensus answer. A voter's choice depends on their individual values, financial priorities, and views on state taxation and government revenue.

Here is an overview of what each vote means and the primary arguments on both sides:

---

### **A "YES" Vote**
* **What it means:** You support amending the California Constitution to prohibit the state from enacting new taxes on personal property (such as financial assets, stocks, investment accounts, and retirement savings) and to restrict certain retroactive taxes.
* **Arguments in favor:**
  * **Protects savings and retirement:** Supporters argue that Californians already pay income taxes when earnings are made and should be constitutionally protected against future taxes on their accumulated savings, 401(k)s, pensions, or other personal assets.
  * **Limits retroactive taxation:** Proponents emphasize that taxpayers should have stability and predictability, arguing that taxes applied retroactively to past activities are unfair.
  * **Key supporters:** California Professional Firefighters, State Building and Construction Trades Council of California, California Small Business Association, and AMVETS Department of California.

---

### **A "NO" Vote**
* **What it means:** You oppose amending the California Constitution in this manner, keeping current constitutional tax authority intact and allowing the state to continue to have the option to tax financial assets or personal property in the future.
* **Arguments against:**
  * **Blocks wealth taxation:** Opponents argue that the measure is primarily designed as a "poison pill" to nullify Proposition 40 (the proposed billionaire wealth tax), since conflicting measures with more votes take precedence.
  * **Fiscal impact on public services:** Critics contend that restricting the state’s taxing options could limit future state revenues needed for healthcare, public education, and emergency services.
  * **Key opponents:** U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders, SEIU United Healthcare Workers West (SEIU-UHW), and several major newspaper editorial boards.

---

Because deciding how to vote on a ballot initiative involves balancing these competing policy priorities, a definitive **yes** or **no** recommendation cannot be provided.
