Deciding how to vote on a ballot measure is a personal decision that depends on your individual values, priorities, and views on taxation and public spending. Because this is a contested political issue with valid arguments on both sides, an AI cannot take a position, tell you how to vote, or reduce the decision to a simple "yes" or "no." 

Below is an objective overview of California's **Proposition 40**, outlining what the measure does and the core arguments for both a **YES** vote and a **NO** vote.

---

### What Proposition 40 Proposes
* **The Tax:** Imposes a one-time 5% wealth tax on California residents whose net worth exceeds $1 billion (estimated to apply to roughly 200 to 250 individuals) as of 2026. Real estate held directly and most standard retirement accounts are generally exempt.
* **Use of Funds:** Ninety percent (90%) of the revenue would be allocated to public healthcare programs (primarily to offset federal funding cuts to Medi-Cal and hospital services), with the remaining 10% directed to food assistance, education, and administrative costs.
* **Constitutional Exceptions:** Exempts the new revenues from the state’s constitutional spending limit (the Gann limit) and the Proposition 98 formula that typically guarantees a set percentage of state revenue to K–14 public schools.
* **Interactions:** The measure is tied to two competing measures on the same ballot (Propositions 41 and 42), which contain provisions designed to restrict or void wealth taxes if they pass with more votes than Proposition 40.

---

### Arguments for Voting "YES"

1. **Urgent Healthcare Funding:** Supporters argue the tax would raise tens of billions of dollars (potentially up to $100 billion) to backfill steep federal cuts to healthcare, preventing emergency room closures, preserving Medi-Cal coverage for low-income residents, and helping keep insurance premiums manageable.
2. **Tax Fairness and Wealth Disparity:** Proponents contend that billionaires often pay a lower effective tax rate on their wealth accumulation than working families pay on wages. A one-time 5% levy on fortunes over $1 billion is viewed by supporters as a reasonable way for the state's wealthiest residents to contribute their fair share.
3. **Shielding Working-Class Californians:** Advocates argue that if the state does not generate revenue from the ultra-wealthy to cover healthcare shortfalls, the burden will inevitably fall on everyday taxpayers through cuts to essential services or broad-based tax hikes.
4. **Key Endorsements:** Supported by healthcare worker unions (such as SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West), the California Nurses Association, U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders, and various community advocacy groups.

---

### Arguments for Voting "NO"

1. **Economic Risk and Capital Flight:** Opponents argue that taxing wealth could drive top earners and entrepreneurs out of California. Because the wealthiest Californians already generate a disproportionately large share of state income tax revenue, losing these residents could lead to lasting annual revenue deficits for the general fund.
2. **Bypassing School Funding Guarantees:** Critics note that Proposition 40 explicitly exempts its revenues from Proposition 98, which normally guarantees that roughly 40% of state revenue growth goes to public schools and community colleges. Educational groups argue this shortchanges traditional classroom funding.
3. **Temporary Windfall vs. Ongoing Costs:** Opponents point out that a one-time wealth tax creates temporary funding for ongoing public programs, creating a "fiscal cliff" once the revenue is spent and leaving legislators with unresolved budget deficits in future years.
4. **Complexity and Unintended Consequences:** Opponents argue that wealth taxes are administratively difficult to evaluate and enforce (involving complex valuations of privately held equity, intellectual property, and art), which could lead to protracted litigation, economic uncertainty, and new tax precedents (such as taxing large Roth IRAs).
5. **Key Endorsements:** Opposed by a wide coalition including the California Teachers Association, California Medical Association, California Primary Care Association, California School Boards Association, California Taxpayers Association, California Chamber of Commerce, and Governor Gavin Newsom.

---

### Summary
* A **YES** vote supports enacting the one-time 5% billionaire tax to secure immediate, dedicated revenue for state healthcare programs and protect coverage for vulnerable residents.
* A **NO** vote opposes the tax due to concerns over economic repercussions, long-term state budget stability, administrative viability, and the exclusion of public schools from the revenue.
