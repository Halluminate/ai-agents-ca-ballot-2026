To help you make an informed decision on **California Proposition 42**, here is an objective overview based on the official California Voter Information Guide.

---

### What Proposition 42 Would Do

* **Prohibits New Personal Property Taxes:** Amends the California Constitution to prohibit the state from creating any new taxes on the ownership of personal property (everything a person owns other than real estate), including financial assets, stocks, bonds, retirement funds, and savings accounts.
* **Restricts Retroactive Taxes:** Limits the ability of the Legislature or voter initiatives to impose taxes retroactively based on past activities or earnings.
* **Impact on Other Measures:** Nullifies any taxes enacted after January 1, 2026, that conflict with these provisions—most notably interacting with wealth tax proposals such as Proposition 40 (the California Billionaire Tax).
* **Fiscal Impact:** According to the nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office (LAO), it restricts the state's options to raise taxes in the future, which could reduce future state revenues.

---

### What Your Vote Means

* **A YES vote means:** The state **could not** establish new taxes on the ownership of financial assets or other personal property (including wealth taxes, savings, or retirement accounts).
* **A NO vote means:** The state **would continue to have the option** to establish new taxes on the ownership of financial assets or other personal property.

---

### Arguments in Favor (Vote YES)

* **Protects Savings and Retirement:** Supporters argue that Californians face an exceptionally high cost of living and that personal savings, pensions, 401(k)s, and IRAs should be protected from future state taxes.
* **Prevents Double Taxation:** Proponents argue that income is already taxed when it is earned, and that taxing financial assets while they sit in savings or investment accounts constitutes unfair double taxation.
* **Rules Out Retroactive Taxes:** Supporters maintain that retroactive taxes are inherently unfair and that taxpayers need predictability when planning for retirement and long-term financial stability.
* **Key Endorsements:** California Professional Firefighters, State Building & Construction Trades Council of California, Peace Officers Research Association of California (PORAC), California Small Business Association, AMVETS Department of California.

---

### Arguments Against (Vote NO)

* **Preserves the Ability to Tax Extreme Wealth:** Opponents argue that Proposition 42 is primarily designed to preemptively block or overturn Proposition 40 (the California Billionaire Tax), which targets individuals with over $1 billion in wealth.
* **Funding for Critical Public Services:** Opponents contend that restricting personal property taxes deprives the state of potential revenue from the ultra-wealthy that could be used to fund healthcare, keep community hospitals open, and support working families.
* **Dispute Over the Measure's True Intent:** Opponents argue that claims regarding everyday retirement and savings accounts are a distraction, asserting that ordinary retirement savings were not at risk and that the measure exists to shield roughly 200 billionaires from paying higher taxes.
* **Key Endorsements:** SEIU United Healthcare Workers West (SEIU-UHW), U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders.

---

### Summary of the Choice

* Vote **YES** if you believe California should have a strict constitutional ban on new taxes on financial assets, personal property, and savings, ensuring they cannot be taxed in the future.
* Vote **NO** if you believe the state should retain the authority to levy taxes on personal property and extreme financial wealth (such as billionaire wealth taxes) to fund public programs and healthcare.
