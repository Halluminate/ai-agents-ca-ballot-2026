### What Proposition 41 Does

Proposition 41 is a citizen-initiated constitutional amendment on California's November 2026 ballot titled **"Prohibits New State Taxes That Exclude Revenues From State Spending Limit. Requires Audits for New State Special Taxes."**

If passed, Proposition 41 would:
1. **Mandate Pre-Election and Recurring Audits:** Require the California State Auditor to audit programs proposed to receive funding from new or increased special taxes before they appear on the ballot (once 25% of required petition signatures are collected), with summaries published in the state Voter Information Guide. It also requires the State Auditor to conduct follow-up audits every four years for programs funded by newly approved special taxes.
2. **Enforce the State Spending Limit (Gann Limit):** Prohibit new state taxes enacted after January 1, 2026, from exempting their revenues from the state's voter-approved spending limit.
3. **Interact with Proposition 40:** Under the California Constitution, if two conflicting measures pass on the same ballot, the one receiving more "yes" votes prevails on conflicting provisions. Because Proposition 40 (the proposed "Billionaire Tax Act") exempts its revenues from the Gann limit, Proposition 41 is structured to directly conflict with and potentially nullify Proposition 40 if Proposition 41 passes with a higher vote total.

### Arguments in Favor of Voting YES

* **Government Accountability and Transparency:** Supporters—including the California Society of Certified Public Accountants (CalCPAs), the California Taxpayers Association (CalTax), and various regional chambers of commerce—argue that Californians pay high taxes without sufficient oversight. They contend that requiring independent, public audits before and after special taxes are passed ensures government accountability, discourages wasteful spending, and gives voters clear data before voting on tax proposals.
* **Fiscal Discipline:** Proponents believe the state spending limit (the Gann limit) exists to keep state budget growth aligned with economic and population growth. They argue that ballot measures should not carve out exemptions to spending caps, as doing so leads to unsustainable government expansion.
* **Countering the Billionaire Wealth Tax:** Many business and taxpayer advocates argue that Proposition 40’s proposed wealth tax poses significant economic risks, including the potential flight of high earners, capital, and businesses from California. Voting "yes" on Proposition 41 serves as a safeguard to prevent Proposition 40 from taking effect.

---

### Arguments in Favor of Voting NO

* **Viewed as a "Poison Pill" Initiative:** Opponents—including SEIU United Healthcare Workers West (SEIU-UHW), public healthcare advocates, and progressive leaders like Senator Bernie Sanders—argue that Proposition 41 was funded by wealthy opponents of Proposition 40 primarily as a maneuver to block a dedicated tax on billionaires intended to fund healthcare, Medicaid (Medi-Cal), and education.
* **Restricting State Funding Flexibility:** Opponents contend that applying rigid spending limits to new special taxes would prevent California from funding urgent needs or mitigating federal cuts to healthcare, nutrition, and social safety-net programs.
* **Administrative Burden on the State Auditor:** Critics argue that California already has established oversight and audit mechanisms. Requiring mandatory audits of proposed initiatives before they have even fully qualified for the ballot could overwhelm the State Auditor’s resources and create new bureaucratic obstacles for grassroots citizen initiatives.

### Summary

* A **YES** vote favors stricter auditing requirements for new special taxes, adherence to the state spending limit, and blocking or limiting measures like Proposition 40.
* A **NO** vote opposes placing these restrictions and spending caps on new special taxes, maintaining the current state spending limit rules and preserving the legal pathway for Proposition 40.

The decision comes down to each voter's personal priorities regarding state spending caps, tax policy, government oversight, and wealth taxation.
