### What Proposition 41 Proposes
Proposition 41 is an initiative constitutional amendment that would:
1. **Mandate Pre-Election Audits:** Require the independent California State Auditor to review any program proposed to be funded by a new or increased special tax via voter initiative once proponents collect 25% of the required signatures. The Auditor must assess program effectiveness, evaluate whether annual costs can be reduced by at least 10%, and provide an audit summary to be printed in the official Voter Information Guide.
2. **Require Recurring Audits:** Require the State Auditor to conduct ongoing performance and financial reviews every four years for programs receiving revenues from new or increased special taxes approved after January 1, 2026.
3. **Enforce State Spending Limits:** Prohibit new state taxes enacted after January 1, 2026, from exempting their revenues from the state appropriations limit (the Gann Limit). Under California law, if competing or conflicting ballot measures pass in the same election, the measure with the higher vote count takes precedence—meaning Proposition 41 could impact or nullify other tax initiatives, such as Proposition 40 (the proposed billionaire wealth tax).

### A "YES" Vote: Meaning and Arguments

**What it means:** You support requiring independent pre-election and recurring audits of programs funded by new state special taxes, including publishing findings in the voter guide, and subjecting new special tax revenues to the state's constitutional spending limits.

**Key arguments in favor:**
* **Accountability and Transparency:** Supporters (including the California Society of CPAs, the California Taxpayers Association, and the CalAsian Chamber of Commerce) argue that California already has high tax rates and that billions of dollars have been spent on public programs (such as homelessness) with insufficient oversight. Independent audits would ensure public funds are tracked and waste is identified.
* **Informed Voting:** Having the State Auditor analyze proposals before they appear on the ballot gives voters nonpartisan information on whether a proposed tax is truly needed and whether existing programs could be run more efficiently.
* **Fiscal Restraint:** Applying the voter-approved state spending limit to new tax measures ensures that government spending does not grow without boundary and that excess tax revenues are returned to taxpayers as intended under California's constitution.

### A "NO" Vote: Meaning and Arguments

**What it means:** You oppose these changes, maintaining existing auditing procedures and current rules governing state spending limits for new special taxes.

**Key arguments against:**
* **Impact on Other Initiatives:** Opponents (including SEIU United Healthcare Workers West and Senator Bernie Sanders) argue that Proposition 41 was placed on the ballot primarily by wealthy donors as a strategic measure to block or invalidate Proposition 40 (the California Billionaire Tax intended to fund healthcare and education).
* **Restrictions on Essential Public Funding:** Opponents contend that forcing new special taxes to count toward the state spending limit could restrict the state’s ability to fund vital public services like healthcare, emergency response, and schools, potentially triggering spending cuts elsewhere in the state budget.
* **Administrative Burden and Cost:** Requiring audits of citizen initiatives before it is even known whether they will gather enough signatures to qualify for the ballot adds significant costs and administrative burdens to the State Auditor’s office and the state’s General Fund.

* A **Yes** vote favors increased oversight, mandatory audits, and strict adherence to constitutional spending caps for new special taxes.
