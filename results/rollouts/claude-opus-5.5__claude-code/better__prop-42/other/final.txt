I won't give you a one-word answer on this one. I did the research, and it doesn't come down to a fact I can check. It comes down to a value judgment: should California be allowed to tax wealth at all? Voters disagree on that, and I don't think it's my place to pick a side in an election that's tomorrow. Here's how the choice breaks down:

**Vote yes** if you want new state taxes on wealth and assets (everything except real estate) banned in the state constitution permanently, along with most retroactive taxes. Know that it will probably cancel Prop 40, the one-time 5% tax on billionaires, if 42 gets more votes.
- **On the yes side:** Gov. Newsom, CalTax, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, CalChamber, the state Republican Party, the firefighters' and building-trades unions, the OC Register and the SD Union-Tribune.

**Vote no** if you support Prop 40. Also vote no if you're unsure about wealth taxes but don't want a broad, permanent ban written into the constitution.
- **On the no side:** the state Democratic Party, the state labor federation, the ACLU, the Sac Bee and the Mercury News. The League of Women Voters, SPUR and the SF Chronicle take no position on Prop 40 but still say no on 42.

**What matters most:**
- **Voting yes on both 40 and 42 works against itself.** If both pass and 42 gets more votes, 40 is likely void, though courts would make the final call.
- **You don't need 42 to stop the billionaire tax.** Voting no on 40 does that directly. Prop 42 goes much further, and only another statewide vote could undo it.
- **The "protect your retirement" pitch is misleading.** No current proposal would tax ordinary retirement accounts. Prop 40 exempts most of them, except Roth IRAs above $10M. A fact-check found California has never tried to tax savings for all residents.
- **What it leaves alone:** existing taxes like the vehicle license fee, property taxes on homes and land, and income and capital-gains taxes.
- **The ban on retroactive taxes reaches beyond Prop 40.** It would also limit routine tax changes that reach back to the start of the tax year, unless the Governor declares an emergency. Supporters call that fairness; critics say it ties the state's hands in budget crises.
- **Money:** about $159M for yes, almost all from the Sergey Brin–funded Building a Better California, vs. about $35M against, mostly from the SEIU-UHW healthcare union.
