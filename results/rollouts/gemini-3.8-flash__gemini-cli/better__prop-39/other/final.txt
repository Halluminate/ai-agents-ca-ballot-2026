Whether it is better to vote "yes" or "no" on California Proposition 39 is a subjective policy choice, and I cannot advise you on how to vote or provide a binary "yes" or "no" answer. 

Below is an objective overview of the measure and the primary arguments on each side to help inform your decision:

---

### **What Proposition 39 Would Do**
Proposition 39 (on the November 3, 2026 general election ballot) would amend the California Constitution to:
* **Require government-issued photo ID** to vote in person at polling locations.
* **Require the last four digits of a designated ID** written on mail-in ballot envelopes to be counted.
* **Mandate citizenship verification efforts** using government databases for registered voter rolls.
* **Provide free voter ID cards** issued by the state to eligible voters upon request.

---

### **Arguments for Voting "YES"**
* **Voter Confidence:** Proponents argue that photo ID requirements increase public trust in election outcomes and bring California in line with numerous other states.
* **Fraud Prevention:** Supporters contend that requiring verifiable identification deters impersonation and ensures only registered, eligible citizens cast ballots.
* **Key Supporters:** The Transparency Foundation, Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association, Reform California.

---

### **Arguments for Voting "NO"**
* **Voter Access:** Opponents argue that requiring government-issued photo ID creates unnecessary obstacles that disproportionately burden low-income, elderly, rural, and student voters.
* **Existing Protections:** Opponents maintain that California's existing signature verification and registration safeguards are sufficient to ensure integrity without disenfranchising eligible voters.
* **Fiscal Cost:** The Legislative Analyst's Office estimates the measure would cost state and local governments tens of millions to low hundreds of millions of dollars annually to administer.
* **Key Opponents:** ACLU California Action, League of Women Voters of California, California Common Cause.

---
