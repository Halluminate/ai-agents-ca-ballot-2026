I cannot tell you whether to vote "yes" or "no" on California Proposition 44, as how you vote depends on your personal priorities and values. Instead, here is an overview of the measure and the primary arguments from both sides to help you make your own decision:

### What Proposition 44 Does
Proposition 44 (the *Clinic Funding Accountability and Transparency Act*) regulates how private, nonprofit community health clinics—specifically Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs)—spend their revenue. It requires these clinics to spend at least **90% of their annual revenue** on "program services" (such as direct medical, dental, and mental health care, vaccinations, and community outreach), effectively capping administrative and non-program expenses at **10%**. Clinics failing to meet this threshold without a waiver would face financial penalties enforced by the California Attorney General.

* **A "Yes" vote** supports establishing the requirement that specified nonprofit community health clinics spend at least 90% of their annual revenue on direct patient care and program services.
* **A "No" vote** opposes this new spending requirement, maintaining existing state and federal regulations on how community health clinics manage their budgets.

### Arguments for Voting "Yes"
* **Accountability and Transparency:** Supporters (such as SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West) argue that the measure ensures public taxpayer funds and clinic revenues go directly toward patient care and frontline services rather than bloated executive salaries, overhead, or administrative waste.
* **Prioritizing Care:** Proponents believe setting a clear statutory threshold guarantees that low-income patients and underserved communities receive the maximum benefit from the funding directed to these clinics.

### Arguments for Voting "No"
* **Risk to Clinic Operations:** Opponents (including the California Medical Association, the California Primary Care Association, and the American Academy of Pediatrics California) argue that an arbitrary 10% cap on non-program spending is unworkable for many clinics, especially smaller or rural ones that rely on administrative infrastructure for complex billing, IT security, compliance, and facility maintenance.
* **Potential Service Cuts and Closures:** Critics contend that the strict requirements, hefty penalties, and compliance fees (estimated by the Legislative Analyst's Office to cost in the low tens of millions of dollars annually, funded by fees on the clinics) could force clinics to reduce services or close altogether, reducing access to care for low-income Californians.
