I cannot tell you whether to vote "yes" or "no" on a ballot proposition, as that decision depends on your personal priorities and values. Here is a neutral breakdown of **California Proposition 40** from the Official Voter Information Guide to help you decide:

### What Proposition 40 Does
Proposition 40 is an initiative constitutional amendment and statute that would impose a **one-time 5% tax on the net worth of California residents with over $1 billion in assets** (calculated as of January 1, 2026). 
* **Covered assets:** Businesses, securities, art, collectibles, and intellectual property (generally excluding direct real estate holdings, pensions, and retirement accounts).
* **Revenue allocation:** 90% would go toward state healthcare programs (such as Medi-Cal, hospitals, and clinics), and 10% toward food assistance and education programs. It also exempts this revenue from existing state spending limits and Prop 98 school funding formulas.

### Arguments for Voting YES
* **Healthcare & Social Funding:** Raises an estimated tens of billions of dollars in temporary revenue to protect and expand healthcare access, food assistance, and education.
* **Tax Fairness:** Supporters (including SEIU-UHW, the California Labor Federation, and the California Democratic Party) argue that billionaires often pay lower effective tax rates than working families and should contribute more to offset federal healthcare cuts.

### Arguments for Voting NO
* **Economic & Budget Risks:** The Legislative Analyst's Office (LAO) and opponents warn that a wealth tax could prompt high-earners to leave California, potentially reducing ongoing annual income tax revenues by hundreds of millions of dollars per year.
* **Spending Rules & Structure:** Opponents (including Gov. Gavin Newsom, the California Teachers Association, the California Medical Association, and business groups) argue that the measure bypasses constitutional school funding guarantees (Prop 98), relies on one-time money for ongoing needs, and creates administrative challenges in valuing non-liquid assets.
