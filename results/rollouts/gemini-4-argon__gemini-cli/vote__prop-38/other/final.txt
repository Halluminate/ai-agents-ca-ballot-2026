I cannot tell you whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 38, as voting decisions depend on your personal values and fiscal priorities. Instead, here is a breakdown of the measure to help you decide:

**Proposition 38: Authorizes Bonds for Immunology Medical Research**
* **What it does:** Authorizes $8.4 billion in state general obligation bonds to fund immunology and immunotherapy research, with half designated for a UC-affiliated nonprofit research institute and half for research grants.
* **Fiscal Impact:** Increases state costs by $500 million to $600 million annually for about 20 years to repay the bonds, potentially offset if the resulting research generates state revenue.

**Vote YES if you:**
* Want to dedicate $8.4 billion in state bond funding toward immunology research aimed at treating and preventing diseases like cancer, Alzheimer's, and heart disease.
* Support provisions included in the measure, such as a mandated 20% prescription drug discount and independent audit requirements.

**Vote NO if you:**
* Oppose taking on decades of new state bond debt ($500M–$600M per year) that could reduce the state budget's flexibility for other priorities.
* Believe large-scale medical research funding and earmarks to specific institutes should be decided through the standard legislative budget process rather than through ballot initiatives.
