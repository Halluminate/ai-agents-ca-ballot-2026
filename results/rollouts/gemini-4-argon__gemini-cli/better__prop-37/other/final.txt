Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on California Proposition 37 is a personal decision that depends on your priorities regarding housing policy, economic effects, and the role of state-sponsored financing programs, so I cannot tell you how to vote. Here is an overview of the measure and the considerations on both sides:

**What Proposition 37 Does:**
Proposition 37 authorizes up to $25 billion in revenue bonds to establish a loan program for middle-income California residents purchasing newly constructed homes (priced below approximately $1.5 million). Eligible buyers who put at least 3% down and occupy the home can receive fixed-rate second mortgages for up to 17% of the purchase price. The bonds are repaid through the borrowers' mortgage payments rather than state taxes, resulting in no direct fiscal cost to the state or local governments.

* **Reasons to vote YES:** Supporters—including labor unions like the California Conference of Carpenters, the United Nurses Associations of California, and State Treasurer Fiona Ma—argue that the program helps middle-class families overcome high down-payment and interest-rate barriers to homeownership. Because the program is funded through revenue bonds backed by mortgage repayments rather than the state's General Fund, proponents emphasize that it expands access to housing and stimulates new home construction at zero cost or risk to taxpayers.
* **Reasons to vote NO:** Although no formal opposition argument was submitted for the official voter guide, critics of similar housing-subsidy and bond measures generally argue that subsidizing demand without directly removing regulatory barriers to construction can inflate prices for new homes. Others may prefer that the state avoid managing large-scale mortgage lending programs or question whether limiting the benefit strictly to newly constructed homes is the most effective way to address overall housing affordability.
