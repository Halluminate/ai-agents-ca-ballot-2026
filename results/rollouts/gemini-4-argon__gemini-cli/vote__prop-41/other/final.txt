Whether to vote "Yes" or "No" on California Proposition 41 depends on your personal policy priorities, so I cannot give a single "Yes" or "No" recommendation. Instead, here is what each vote means based on the official Voter Information Guide and Legislative Analyst's Office (LAO) analysis so you can decide for yourself:

### **Vote YES if you want to:**
* **Enforce the State Spending Limit:** Prohibit any new state taxes enacted after January 1, 2026, from excluding their revenues from the voter-approved state spending limit (the Gann Limit). Note that if both Proposition 41 and Proposition 40 (the "Billionaire Tax") pass and Proposition 41 receives more votes, this provision would nullify Proposition 40.
* **Mandate Independent Audits:** Require the California State Auditor to review programs funded by proposed special tax initiatives *before* they appear on the ballot (including identifying ways to cut annual costs by at least 10%) and conduct recurring public audits every four years for newly approved special taxes.
* **Align with Supporters:** Supporters (such as the California Taxpayers Association, CalAsian Chamber of Commerce, and California Society of CPAs) argue that taxpayers deserve stricter oversight, transparency, and accountability for how billions in tax dollars are spent on programs like homelessness.

### **Vote NO if you want to:**
* **Maintain Current Spending Limit Rules:** Allow voters and the Legislature to continue exempting certain new special taxes from the state's constitutional spending limit, and preserve the viability of measures like Proposition 40 that rely on such exemptions to fund services like healthcare.
* **Keep Current Audit Processes:** Leave the California State Auditor's responsibilities unchanged without adding mandatory pre-election cost-cutting reviews or new recurring audit requirements paid for out of special tax funds.
* **Align with Opponents:** Opponents (such as SEIU United Healthcare Workers West and Sen. Bernie Sanders) argue that Proposition 41 is a measure designed to undermine and nullify the California Billionaire Tax, protecting wealthy taxpayers at the expense of funding for healthcare and other essential services.
