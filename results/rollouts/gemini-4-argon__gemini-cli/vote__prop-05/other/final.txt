I cannot tell you whether to vote "yes" or "no" on Proposition 5, as voting decisions depend on your personal priorities and values. I can, however, summarize what each vote means to help you decide:

* **A "Yes" vote** removes the replacement candidate question from state recall ballots. If a state official is recalled, the vacancy would be filled through the standard vacancy process (such as a subsequent special election or appointment, depending on the office). Supporters argue this prevents a replacement candidate from taking office with a small plurality of votes (fewer votes than the recalled official received to stay in office) and keeps recall elections focused solely on the official's performance.
* **A "No" vote** keeps the current two-part recall ballot, where voters decide simultaneously whether to recall the official and who should replace them if the recall succeeds. Opponents argue that removing the replacement vote limits voters' direct say in choosing a successor, allows political insiders or appointees to fill the seat temporarily, and could lead to the added cost of a separate special election.
