Whether to vote "yes" or "no" on California Proposition 1 is a matter of personal judgment and depends on your fiscal and policy priorities, so I cannot make that choice for you or answer with just "yes" or "no." Here is a summary of the measure to help you decide:

* **What Proposition 1 does:** Authorizes $11.25 billion in state general obligation bonds to fund affordable housing programs (such as multifamily rental housing, supportive housing, and down payment assistance) and home loan programs for veterans.
* **Fiscal Impact:** Estimated by the Legislative Analyst's Office to increase state General Fund costs by roughly $500 million to $600 million annually for about 25 years to repay the bonds (with the veterans' home loan portion repaid by participating veterans).
* **Arguments for voting YES:** Supporters argue the bond is critical to addressing California's severe housing shortage and homelessness crisis by fast-tracking affordable housing for veterans, seniors, low-income families, and essential workers without raising taxes.
* **Arguments for voting NO:** While no formal opposition argument was submitted to the official voter guide, critics of the bond generally point to concerns over increasing the state's long-term debt obligations and borrowing costs, which must be paid out of the state's General Fund over decades.
