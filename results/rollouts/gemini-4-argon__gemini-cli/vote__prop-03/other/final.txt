I cannot tell you whether to vote "yes" or "no" on a ballot proposition, as that decision depends on your personal priorities and fiscal philosophy. Instead, I can provide a neutral overview of what each choice means for **California Proposition 3 (Permanent Funding for Schools and Health Care by Extending Existing Tax on High Incomes)** so you can decide for yourself:

* **Vote YES** if you want to make permanent the existing income tax rates (in place since 2012) on individuals earning over $371,000 per year. Proponents argue this protects approximately $5 billion to $15 billion in annual state revenue used for public schools, community colleges, and healthcare without raising taxes on the vast majority of Californians.
* **Vote NO** if you want the existing tax increase on high-income earners to expire as scheduled in 2031. Opponents argue that California already has the highest taxes in the nation, that temporary tax increases should expire as originally promised, and that the state should focus on curbing government spending and waste rather than making temporary taxes permanent.
